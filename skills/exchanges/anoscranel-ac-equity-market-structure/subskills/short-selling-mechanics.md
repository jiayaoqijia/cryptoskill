# Short selling mechanics

## Standard

A short sale requires a borrow: someone must lend the shares, and the lender can ask for them back. That recall right is the mechanic most shorts underestimate. A position can be closed not because the thesis changed but because the borrow disappeared, and the cost of the borrow itself can consume the return.

Short interest is the published aggregate of open shorts; days-to-cover is that number divided by average volume. Both are lagged and both are interpreted far more confidently than the data supports. A squeeze happens when holders of the float will not sell and shorts must buy; the mechanics are real, but the prediction of one is not a reliable craft.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance>.

## Decision rules

- Record the borrow rate, the recall terms, and the lender's right to demand shares.
- Record short interest and days-to-cover with their publication dates and lag.
- Size a short so that a recall can be met without forced buying at a bad price.
- Record the dividend and voting obligations that pass to the short.
- Treat squeeze predictions as scenarios, never as plans; record the exit conditions before entry.
- Where borrow is unavailable or expensive, record the constraint rather than substituting options without noting it.

## Procedure

1. Establish the borrow terms: rate, availability, recall.
2. Record short interest and days-to-cover with dates.
3. Record obligations: dividends owed, voting, buy-in risk.
4. Set the recall contingency: what happens, at what size, on what timeline.
5. Write the exit conditions before entry.
6. Record the position with its financing cost.

## Anti-patterns

- Shorting without confirming the borrow.
- Treating short interest as a prediction of direction.
- Ignoring the dividend obligation.
- Sizing without a recall contingency.
- Assuming a short can be held through any squeeze.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Borrow terms recorded.
- [ ] Short interest and days-to-cover recorded with dates.
- [ ] Obligations listed.
- [ ] Recall contingency written.
- [ ] Exit conditions written before entry.
- [ ] Financing cost recorded.

The borrow is a living contract, not a setting. It can be recalled, repriced, or withdrawn, and the borrower learns about it at the least convenient moment. Recording the recall terms before entry is the difference between a position with a known cost and one with an open-ended obligation.
