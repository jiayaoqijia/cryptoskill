# Mergers and acquisitions

## Standard

A merger converts one position into another: cash, shares of the acquirer, or a mix. Each form has a different effect on the record. Cash consideration closes the position and realises a gain or loss; stock consideration carries the basis into the new shares; mixed consideration splits the basis between the cash and stock components according to the terms.

The mechanics arrive with dates: announcement, shareholder vote, regulatory approvals, closing, and the conversion of positions. Until closing, the target trades at a spread to the deal terms, and that spread is the market's estimate of completion risk rather than a valuation of the business.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Record the consideration form, the exchange ratio, and the closing conditions.
- Cash consideration: record the disposal and the realised result.
- Stock consideration: carry the basis into the received shares, recording the ratio used.
- Mixed consideration: allocate the basis between components per the deal terms and say how.
- Record the closing date and the broker's conversion before treating the position as final.
- A revised or failed deal is recorded as an amendment; the original record is retained.

## Procedure

1. Record terms, ratio, and conditions from the definitive documents.
2. Record the anticipated and actual closing dates.
3. Apply the consideration form to the position.
4. Allocate basis where the consideration is mixed, stating the method.
5. Reconcile with the broker's post-closing position.
6. Amend the record if the terms changed.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating the pre-closing spread as a valuation.
- Carrying a cash merger as if shares remained.
- Forgetting to allocate basis in a mixed deal.
- Recording only the closing date and losing the announcement.
- Overwriting the original record after a revision.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Terms, ratio, and conditions recorded.
- [ ] Dates recorded.
- [ ] Consideration applied to the position.
- [ ] Basis allocated where mixed, method stated.
- [ ] Broker position reconciled.
- [ ] Amendments recorded for revisions.

The pre-closing spread is a market estimate of completion risk, and it moves with regulatory news rather than with the target's business. Recording it separately from the position's cost basis keeps the two from being confused when the deal closes or breaks.
