# Bank and credit record

## Standard

The record states what was assessed, with which ratios, at which point in the cycle, and what would change the view. It is written for two readers: the person deciding, who needs the summary, and the person reviewing in a year, who needs the assumptions to have been written down before the outcome was known.

The record separates the three risks a bank carries — credit, capital, and liquidity — because a position can be acceptable on one and fragile on another. It also records the cycle assumption explicitly, because most bank analysis errors are not arithmetic errors; they are the assumption that the current credit environment persists.

Primary sources: <https://www.fdic.gov/> and <https://www.bis.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record credit, capital, and liquidity assessments separately, each with its ratios and dates.
- State the cycle assumption in one sentence and name what would falsify it.
- Record the exposure being considered and the size relative to the portfolio.
- Note supervisory or accounting changes that affect comparability.
- Review triggers are dated: results releases, capital actions, supervisory actions, cycle indicators.
- The record is append-only; corrections are dated amendments.

## Procedure

1. State the entity, the date, and the question.
2. Summarise credit, capital, and liquidity findings with ratios.
3. State the cycle assumption and its falsifier.
4. Record the exposure and its portfolio context.
5. Set review triggers with dates.
6. File the record and reference it from any decision memo.

## Anti-patterns

- Combining the three risks into one verdict.
- Leaving the cycle assumption implicit.
- Recording ratios without dates.
- Omitting the exposure size.
- Editing the record after a supervisory action.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Entity, date, and question stated.
- [ ] Three risk assessments recorded separately with ratios.
- [ ] Cycle assumption and falsifier written.
- [ ] Exposure and portfolio context recorded.
- [ ] Review triggers dated.
- [ ] Record filed and referenced.

## Sources

- FDIC bank data: <https://www.fdic.gov/>
- Basel Committee standards and publications: <https://www.bis.org/>

The record's test is whether a reviewer can tell, without asking, why the position was acceptable at that date. That requires the ratios, the cycle assumption, and the falsifier to sit in one place, written before the outcome existed, so that a later disagreement is about judgement rather than about memory.
