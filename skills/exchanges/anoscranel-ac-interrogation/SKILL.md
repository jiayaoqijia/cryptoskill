# Interrogation

## Overview

The craft of interrogating an idea, plan, spec, or decision before commitment: the "grill me" pattern. The reader runs a structured session that reads the plan cold, surfaces assumptions, tests evidence, maps failure modes, forces alternatives, and then closes on purpose. The output is a revised plan plus a written record, not a verdict from the questioner.

## When to use

- Someone asks to be grilled: "grill me on my plan", "stress-test this idea", "poke holes in this", "what am I missing".
- A decision is approaching commitment and nobody has attacked it yet.
- An idea feels settled too early, or its author cannot state what would change their mind.

## When NOT to use

Reviewing an existing artifact is outside; ac-code-review covers that. Open-ended research is outside; ac-deep-researcher covers that. Writing requirements is outside; ac-spec-writing covers that. Routine two-way-door choices with small stakes need one question, not a session.

## Status and honest limits

- Seedling: built from the public canon cited in each leaf; the full session order has not yet been stress-tested across many live grilling sessions.
- The protocol produces better questions and a written record. It does not produce certainty; the plan owner still decides.
- Questioning works on a willing subject. Against someone defending a fixed position, the session can only surface the disagreement, not resolve it.
