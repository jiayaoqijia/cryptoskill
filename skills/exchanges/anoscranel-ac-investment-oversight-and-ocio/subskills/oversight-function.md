# Oversight function

## Standard

An oversight function exists to test whether delegated decisions are being made within the terms the delegator set. That requires four things: a mandate that states the terms, a monitoring process that measures against them, a reporting line that can act on what it learns, and a record that shows the process ran. Remove any one and the function becomes a formality.

The test of sufficiency is evidential rather than procedural. A committee that meets quarterly and receives reports has not necessarily overseen anything; a committee that has questioned a breach, required a remediation, and checked that it happened has. The record should therefore carry decisions and follow-ups rather than attendance and minutes, because decisions are what demonstrate that the function did something.

Primary sources: <https://www.cfainstitute.org/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the mandate and the party it binds.
- Record the monitoring activities, their frequency, and who performs them.
- Record the reporting line and the escalation route.
- Record the decisions taken and the follow-ups required.
- Record the evidence held for each decision.
- Record the last occasion on which oversight changed an outcome.

## Procedure

1. Record the mandate and the accountable party.
2. Record the monitoring calendar and the owner of each activity.
3. Record the escalation route and its triggers.
4. Record decisions and follow-ups with dates.
5. Record the evidence for each decision.
6. Test the function by asking what it changed in the last year.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a meeting schedule as oversight.
- Recording attendance rather than decisions.
- Leaving escalation undefined until a breach occurs.
- Monitoring without a mandate to measure against.
- Assuming a delegated decision is a delegated responsibility.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Mandate and accountable party recorded.
- [ ] Monitoring calendar recorded.
- [ ] Escalation route recorded.
- [ ] Decisions and follow-ups dated.
- [ ] Evidence held.
- [ ] Last outcome-changing decision recorded.

The most useful question to put to an oversight body is what it declined to approve: a function that has never rejected a recommendation, never required a change, and never escalated a concern is not necessarily well served by its delegates, and the absence of friction is more often a sign of an untested process than of good performance.
