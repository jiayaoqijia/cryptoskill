# Partial and phased transfers

## Standard

A large scheme rarely transfers its liabilities in one transaction. Phasing splits the membership into tranches — by benefit type, by member size, or by a simple segmentation — and insures each as market conditions allow. The approach reduces the risk of transacting the whole scheme at an unfavourable moment, and it lets the scheme learn from the first tranche's execution.

The segmentation decision is where the economics sit. Small pensions and deferred members are typically cheaper to insure per unit of liability than large in-payment pensions, because their administration is simpler and their longevity exposure is less concentrated. Insuring the segments the market prices most attractively first leaves the harder segments for later, when the scheme may have less bargaining power.

Primary sources: <https://www.fca.org.uk/> and <https://www.bis.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the segmentation criterion and the size of each tranche.
- Record which tranches are priced and which remain.
- Record the relative pricing of each tranche against the market.
- Record the administration changes each tranche requires.
- Record the governance process for approving each tranche.
- Record the conditions under which the remaining tranches would proceed.

## Procedure

1. Record the segmentation and the liability in each tranche.
2. Record pricing obtained for each tranche where available.
3. Compare pricing across tranches and record the difference.
4. Record the administration impact of each transfer.
5. Record the approval process.
6. Write the phasing plan with its conditions.

## Anti-patterns

- Insuring the cheapest segments first without considering what remains.
- Assuming a tranche can be priced indefinitely.
- Ignoring the administration cost of transferring in phases.
- Treating each tranche as independent of the scheme's overall funding.
- Overlooking the member communication burden of repeated exercises.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Segmentation and tranche sizes recorded.
- [ ] Pricing per tranche recorded.
- [ ] Relative pricing recorded.
- [ ] Administration impact recorded.
- [ ] Approval process recorded.
- [ ] Conditions for remaining tranches recorded.

The phasing plan should also state what happens if a middle tranche cannot be priced: a scheme that has insured its simplest segments may be left with a remainder that is harder and more expensive to place, and the plan needs a fallback rather than an assumption that the market will be there.
