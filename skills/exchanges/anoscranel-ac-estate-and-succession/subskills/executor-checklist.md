# Executor checklist

## Standard

An executor works from a list that the deceased either left or did not. The list has four parts: where everything is (accounts, documents, keys), who to contact (institutions, advisers, professionals), what order to do things in (death certificates, notifications, valuations, transfers, filings), and what is time-sensitive (deadlines, renewals, claims).

Writing the list is an act of consideration, and it is also a test: a list that cannot be followed by a competent stranger is a plan with gaps. Testing it — by asking someone uninvolved to walk through it — is the cheapest audit available.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.naic.org/>.

## Decision rules

- The list names every account and where its documents are kept.
- Contacts are recorded with departments and reference numbers where available.
- The order of operations is written with the dependencies stated.
- Time-sensitive items are listed at the top with their deadlines.
- The list is tested by a person who has not seen it before.
- The list is updated at the same review as the succession record.

## Procedure

1. Build the inventory: accounts, documents, keys, and locations.
2. Build the contact list with departments.
3. Write the order of operations and the dependencies.
4. List the time-sensitive items with deadlines.
5. Test the list with an uninvolved person.
6. Record the test's findings and fix the gaps.

## Anti-patterns

- A list that names institutions but not departments.
- Omitting where documents are physically or digitally kept.
- Writing the order without dependencies.
- Skipping the test because the list looks complete.
- Never updating the list.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Inventory built with locations.
- [ ] Contacts with departments recorded.
- [ ] Order of operations with dependencies.
- [ ] Time-sensitive items listed with deadlines.
- [ ] List tested by an uninvolved person.
- [ ] Gaps fixed and list dated.

## Sources

- Investor education on estate administration: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>
- Insurance and consumer guidance for claims: <https://www.naic.org/>
- Consumer information on estate matters: <https://www.usa.gov/>

The checklist's test is a rehearsal: hand it to someone who has not seen the estate and ask them to describe the first three things they would do. Any hesitation marks a gap, and the gap is cheaper to fix now than it will be at the moment it matters.

The order of operations matters as much as the inventory: notifying institutions before valuations, and valuations before transfers, prevents the circular requests that stall an estate for weeks.
