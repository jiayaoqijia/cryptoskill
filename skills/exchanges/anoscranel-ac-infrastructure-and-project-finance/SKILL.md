# Infrastructure and project finance

## Overview

A project finance transaction is a claim on a specific cash flow, ring-fenced in a vehicle that has no other business, and its credit is therefore the cash flow rather than a sponsor's balance sheet. That structure determines everything: the debt is sized against the cash flow's coverage, the covenants are set on the coverage ratio, the equity return depends on the refinancing, and the asset's value at the end of the concession is usually zero.

This skill covers that arithmetic and the contracts that produce it: concession life and handback obligations, the difference between availability and demand risk, the DSCR and the lock-up it triggers, the capital expenditure life cycle that owners routinely underestimate, the tariff's inflation linkage, and how refinancing creates or destroys the equity return. Funds and listed vehicles belong to a different skill.

Primary sources: <https://www.worldbank.org/> and <https://www.eib.org/>.

## When to use

- When evaluating a project finance debt or equity position.
- When a concession, availability payment, or demand-risk tariff needs reading.
- When a DSCR or lock-up test is close to its threshold.
- When the capital expenditure cycle or handback obligation matters.

## When NOT to use

- For infrastructure funds and listed vehicles (use ac-alternatives-and-real-assets).
- For direct property rental economics (use ac-direct-real-estate).
- For municipal revenue bonds and their pledges (use ac-municipal-bonds).

## Status and honest limits

Project documents are long and their economics turn on clauses that summaries omit: the handback standard, the change-in-law protection, the termination compensation, and the refinancing gain-share. Those are contractual and jurisdiction-specific, and nothing here substitutes for reading them. Nothing here recommends a project, and a DSCR that looks comfortable in the early years can be consumed by the renewal capital cycle that arrives a decade later, which is the finding most often missed.
