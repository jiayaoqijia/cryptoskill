# Refutational synthesis

## Standard

When studies disagree — different populations reporting opposite experiences, different settings producing contradictory findings — the disagreement is not noise to be averaged away. A refutational synthesis treats the disagreement itself as the finding and asks what explains it: differences in context, in participants, in the moment of data collection, or in the questions asked. Where an explanation accounts for the whole pattern of disagreement, it is itself a synthesis result, and often the most useful one a qualitative review can produce.

The alternative, discarding inconvenient studies or burying them in a majority-of-findings statement, is the standard failure this leaf exists to prevent. A review that reports only where studies agree produces a falsely coherent picture; the disagreement is where the phenomenon's dependence on conditions is visible, and a synthesis that cannot say when a finding applies has said less than the evidence knows.

Primary sources: <https://www.biomedcentral.com/methods> and <https://bmcmedresmethodol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2288-8-45>.

## Decision rules

- Report every case of contradictory findings, with the studies and their contexts identified.
- Search for explanations in differences that are actually recorded — population, setting, timing, question asked.
- State explanations as testable claims about the pattern of disagreement, not as assumptions.
- Rank explanations by how much of the disagreement they account for.
- Where no explanation accounts for the disagreement, state that openly rather than forcing one.
- Never remove a study from the synthesis because its finding contradicts the majority.

## Procedure

1. Identify every case where findings contradict each other, study to study.
2. Tabulate each case with population, setting, timing, and the exact finding of each side.
3. Generate candidate explanations from the recorded differences.
4. Test each explanation against the full pattern of disagreement.
5. Report the explanation that accounts for most, and note what remains unexplained.
6. State the finding as conditional on the explanation: where X, then Y; where not-X, then Z.

## Anti-patterns

- Selecting studies to remove contradiction before synthesis begins.
- Treating a contradiction as a methodological flaw of one study by default.
- Reporting only the majority finding and noting disagreement in a single line.
- Explanations that fit one contradiction but not the rest of the pattern.
- Forcing a unifying conclusion when the evidence genuinely splits.

## Done checklist

- [ ] All contradictions identified and tabulated.
- [ ] Candidate explanations drawn from recorded differences.
- [ ] Each explanation tested against the whole pattern.
- [ ] Best explanation reported with its scope.
- [ ] Unexplained disagreement stated openly.
- [ ] Findings expressed as conditional where the evidence splits.
