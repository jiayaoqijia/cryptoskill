# Drawdown record

## Standard

The drawdown record is the document that answers, in advance, the questions a bad market will raise: how much is being spent, from which assets, under what rule, and what would change it. It is the decumulation counterpart of the investment policy statement, and it inherits the same discipline: written policy, versioned amendments, and a named owner.

It also holds the arithmetic that justifies the policy — the withdrawal rate, the real return assumption, the reserve, the horizon percentile — because a policy without its arithmetic cannot be reviewed when conditions change. The record is read at every review and at every moment of doubt, which is the same thing.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the spending rule, the reserve, the allocation, and the account order.
- Every assumption carries its source or its status as a judgement.
- Versioned amendments only; no silent edits.
- Review triggers are dated: market moves, rule changes, health changes, horizon shifts.
- The record names the person who may change it and the process for doing so.
- A note records which decisions were left for a professional.

## Procedure

1. Assemble the sections from the six leaves.
2. State the assumptions table.
3. Record the owner and the change process.
4. Set the dated review triggers.
5. File the record with the investment policy statement.
6. Amend only through the versioned process.

## Anti-patterns

- A spending plan with no written rule.
- Editing the record after a bad month.
- Leaving assumptions implicit.
- No named owner for changes.
- Filing it separately from the investment policy.

## Done checklist

- [ ] All sections assembled.
- [ ] Assumptions table with sources or judgement status.
- [ ] Owner and change process stated.
- [ ] Review triggers dated.
- [ ] Filed with the investment policy.
- [ ] Amendment process stated.

A drawdown record reviewed only at annual meetings is a record that misses the moments it was written for. The triggers are the important part: each names a condition under which the policy must be re-read, so that the decision to change it is made deliberately rather than in reaction to a headline.

Because the record is read at moments of doubt, its summary should fit on one page: the spending rule, the reserve, the triggers, and the owner. Everything else is the appendix that justifies it, and a reader who needs only the decision should not have to reconstruct it from the arithmetic.
