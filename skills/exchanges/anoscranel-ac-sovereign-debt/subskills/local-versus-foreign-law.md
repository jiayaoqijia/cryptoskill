# Local versus foreign law

## Standard

The same sovereign can issue debt under its own law and under a foreign jurisdiction's law, and the two behave differently when a restructuring arrives. A local-law instrument can be amended by the sovereign's own legislature, which gives the debtor a unilateral route to restructuring; a foreign-law instrument can only be amended through its terms, so the creditors' contractual protections actually bind.

The difference shows up in the price before any crisis and in the recovery during one. Foreign-law bonds typically trade at a spread to local-law bonds of the same issuer, and that spread is the market's price for the creditor's enforcement position. Reading a sovereign's capital structure means separating the two pools, because a restructuring that addresses only one leaves the other untouched and the creditor's recovery depends on which pool holds their claim.

Primary sources: <https://www.imf.org/> and <https://www.bis.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the governing law of each instrument and the jurisdiction of enforcement.
- Record the share of the debt stock in each pool.
- Record the amendment mechanism available under each law.
- Record the spread between comparable local-law and foreign-law instruments.
- Record any statutory or constitutional limit on the sovereign's ability to amend local-law debt.
- Record the enforcement route available for a foreign-law judgment.

## Procedure

1. Classify each instrument by governing law.
2. Record the debt stock in each pool.
3. Record the amendment route for each.
4. Record the observed spread between comparable instruments.
5. Record enforcement options for a foreign-law claim.
6. Write the comparison with the creditor's position stated.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating all of a sovereign's debt as subject to the same process.
- Assuming a foreign-law judgment is straightforward to enforce.
- Ignoring the local-law pool when computing recovery.
- Reading the spread as a pure credit signal rather than a legal one.
- Overlooking constitutional limits that constrain local-law amendment.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Governing law recorded per instrument.
- [ ] Debt stock split recorded.
- [ ] Amendment routes recorded.
- [ ] Spread between pools recorded.
- [ ] Statutory limits recorded.
- [ ] Enforcement route recorded.

The domestic-law route also creates a fairness problem that affects the foreign-law pool: a sovereign that restructures its local-law debt unilaterally and offers foreign-law holders worse terms invites a comparability challenge, and the official sector's willingness to lend can depend on how that challenge is resolved. The two pools are separate in law and linked in negotiation.
