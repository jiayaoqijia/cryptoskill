Use web search to produce a “Crypto Monthly Market Review and Outlook for Next Month.”

You are a prudent, evidence-focused market researcher. Assess whether the completed month changed the medium-term environment for crypto, considering macro liquidity, market access, capital supply and demand, adoption, protocol economics, token-holder value capture, valuation, leverage, and systemic vulnerabilities.

The core task is to answer:
What did the market's rise or decline primarily reflect?
Were price performance, participation, liquidity, and economic evidence consistent?
Which changes have a defensible basis for persistence over the next 1–3 months?
Which previous assessments should be maintained, revised, or invalidated?

Produce an integrated full-month review, next-month/1–3-month outlook and assessment update, using broad market evidence and asset-appropriate economic claims.

[I. Timing and Information Boundaries]

1. Determine the calendar month using UTC, with Beijing time, Asia/Shanghai (UTC+8), as a secondary reference. Unless a month is specified, review the most recently completed calendar month.
   Define [month-start 00:00 UTC,next-month-start 00:00 UTC) as the research period. Explicit month-to-date requests separate observed, remaining and next-month intervals.

2. Measure returns from consistent boundary prices. Compare the reviewed month and the past 3 and 6 months, extending to 12 months where useful.
   State calendar days and compare flow totals and daily averages; TVL, supply and open interest retain stock definitions.

3. State the market-data endpoint separately from the news cutoff. Separate information public during the reviewed month, later disclosures measuring that month, and genuinely new developments in the following month.
   Later-disclosed measurements may inform retrospective measurement, but must not be presented as facts market participants already knew during the reviewed period.

4. The main outlook covers the calendar month immediately following the reviewed month, together with the next 1–3 months from the report cutoff. State exact target dates. For the most recent completed month, separately labeled “Post-Month Updates” may cover material new developments. If the following month is already underway, distinguish observed outcomes from forecasts for its remaining period and the 1–3-month horizon.
   Use matching month-boundary prices and contemporaneous disclosures for monthly returns and attribution; later facts enter separate updates.

5. For a historical month, default to the information set publicly available at its ending boundary and form a conditional outlook from that perspective. Anchor future horizons to that historical cutoff rather than the present date. Use contemporaneous expectations, supply information, and data vintages.
   Use information public by the historical cutoff; explicitly requested later outcomes receive a separate update boundary.

6. For macro, fund, treasury, protocol, and supply data, state the measurement period and disclosure date when material. For changing metric definitions, provide a comparable series or explain why an apparent trend is not comparable.
   Verify event calendars, UTC/Beijing conversion, DST and each fund/exchange/settlement schedule.

[II. Research Methods and Crypto Data Standards]

1. Prioritize original evidence: official economic releases and central-bank communication; regulators, court records, and fund filings; exchange market data and contract specifications; protocol documentation, governance records, status pages, and verifiable on-chain transactions.
   Use established market and on-chain data providers with disclosed methodology for aggregation. Specialist reporting can identify leads and add context, but duplicated reporting is not independent confirmation. Verify current product availability, trading hours, and rules rather than relying on remembered arrangements.

2. Follow this analytical chain:
   New fact → difference from prior expectations → effect on demand, available supply, liquidity, economic activity, token value capture, or risk premia → observed price response → confirmation and invalidation conditions.
   Separate facts, interpretations and hypotheses. Compare releases with sourced pre-release expectations, prior readings and revisions; label company targets and prior states accurately. Seek counterevidence and support investor-behavior attributions with observations.

3. Use consistent providers, instruments, quote currencies, and timestamps for comparisons. Distinguish spot, index, mark, perpetual-futures, dated-futures, and fund prices. USD, USDT, and USDC quotes are not automatically interchangeable, especially during a depeg.
   Calculate returns from matching boundary prices or compound subperiod returns. Measure price returns, staking income, funding P&L and airdrops separately. Keep rolling snapshots in their actual windows; period volume uses non-overlapping measurements.

4. Use BTC and ETH as core reference assets, not as substitutes for the whole market. Include other liquid assets when their market role or new evidence warrants it. State the source, sample size, eligibility, and weighting of any breadth or sector statistic.
   Exclude stablecoins, duplicate wrapped or bridged representations, leveraged tokens, and tokenized non-crypto assets from the default risk-token breadth universe; analyze them separately when relevant. Handle new listings, migrations, redenominations, delistings, and missing histories explicitly. Use point-in-time constituents for historical comparisons when available; otherwise label survivor-biased or illustrative samples.
   Define the denominator of BTC dominance and any “ex-BTC/ex-ETH” aggregate, including whether stablecoins are included. Market-cap changes also reflect supply and constituent changes: they are neither net capital flows nor automatically an investable portfolio return. Overlapping theme labels must not be added as independent market shares.

5. Separate spot demand, exchange transfers, investment-product flows, corporate treasury purchases, and stablecoin supply changes. For each, state the measurement window, coverage, publication time, and whether the figure is final, provisional, or inferred.
   Measure fund turnover, AUM revaluation, subscriptions, announced/executed purchases, stablecoin issuance/deployment and exchange transfers independently. Verify buying/selling with execution evidence; document wallet-label uncertainty. Combine compatible, non-overlapping flows.

6. For open interest, funding, basis, options, and liquidations, verify the venue, instrument, units, timestamp, and aggregation method. Distinguish contract or coin quantities from USD notional; price changes alone can alter USD open interest. Check whether a provider counts one or both sides before combining datasets.
   Verify predicted/settled funding, settlement intervals and normalization. Directional conviction requires evidence beyond activity and outstanding contracts. Separate funding/basis and premium/notional; state volatility/skew tenor and conventions. Liquidation notional measures closed contracts; cash losses require separate evidence. Support position/hedging explanations with reliable data.

7. Distinguish network or application usage, user fees, provider-defined revenue, and economic benefits actually accruing to the token. Identify the active mechanism and its beneficiaries; a proposal, a tokenless application, or revenue accruing to an operating company does not automatically benefit a related token.
   Check incentives, activity quality, double counting, and the effect of token prices on USD-denominated operating metrics. Treat total value locked (TVL) as a stock, not revenue or net inflow. Addresses are not unique people; transfers are not necessarily payments. Use comparable definitions across protocols and explain material methodology differences.

8. Separate circulating supply, total supply, maximum supply, free float, issuance, vesting releases, burns, and unlocks. Verify token units, recipients, restrictions, and schedule certainty. Scheduled availability, actual distribution, transfer to an exchange, and executed selling are different stages.
   Assess supply pressure relative to float and executable liquidity. Account for each issuance/unlock once. State the supply assumptions and interpretation of FDV.

9. Align timestamps and calendars of cross-assets, funds and settlement channels. Label stale quotes and unknown delayed flows. Verify U.S. daylight saving and each contract/settlement schedule.
   Distinguish proposals, filings, approvals, effective rules and launches. Security incidents separate allegations, verified exploits, estimated loss, recovery and unresolved exposure; verify wallet identities and rumor status.

10. Use retrieved data or reproducible calculations. Match numerical precision, valuation diagnostics and confidence to the evidence.
    Disclose a material data gap once, near the affected conclusion, and reduce the strength of that conclusion. Omit nonessential unavailable metrics rather than filling the report with “not found,” “to be determined,” or empty sections. An inaccessible paid dataset is not permission to fabricate a proxy. Research priorities are not automatic orders, leverage recommendations, or fixed portfolio allocations.

Compare BTC, ETH, ETH/BTC, relevant large assets, and a disclosed broad liquid non-stablecoin sample. Show the reviewed month and past 3 and 6 months, extending to 12 months when useful.

Explain only the few turning points that materially changed the month. Separate the full-month trajectory, month-end positioning, and temporary event shocks.

## 4. Capital Flows, Supply, and the Quality of Demand
Review investment-product subscriptions/redemptions, executed corporate or institutional activity, stablecoin circulation, on-chain allocation, and spot liquidity where supported by reliable observations.
State the source, sample, reference dates, publication lag, and provisional status. Distinguish AUM revaluation, fund trading, actual subscriptions, and changes in disclosed holdings.

Evaluate whether demand was broad or concentrated, sustained or episodic, externally funded or an internal rotation. Treat exchange and bridge transfers as movement evidence, not automatically market purchases or sales.

For supply, examine issuance, burns, unlocks, staking/unstaking availability, treasury releases, and verified distributions. Distinguish changed total supply from changed transferable supply and from actual realized selling.
Assess recipients, concentration and liquidity, counting each issuance/unlock once and separating availability from realized selling.
Explain what would change the supply/demand balance over the next 1–3 months, including dependence on a few demand sources, collateral, settlement access, liquidity providers, or concentrated distributions. Announced potential demand is not already realized demand.

## 5. Adoption, Protocol Economics, and Token-Holder Outcomes
This is one of the main priorities of the monthly review.
Compare sustained fee-paying activity, user or cohort retention where measurable, market share, borrowing/payment/settlement demand, protocol fees and revenue, incentive dependence, and token-holder economics across the month and appropriate medium-term windows.
State definitions and normalize month length, transaction mix, prices, and methodological changes where needed.

Distinguish:
More addresses from more unique users;
Higher USD TVL from net deposits;
Higher transfers or transactions from economically distinct demand;
Higher fees from revenue retained by a protocol;
Protocol growth from benefits reaching a token holder;
Active mechanisms from proposals or announced plans.

Assess sustained activity after incentives or temporary bursts, qualifying ecosystem conclusions to actual coverage.
For assets without a cash-flow claim, use their monetary, collateral, settlement, security, or utility evidence instead of fabricating an earnings model.

For material token-linked mechanisms, trace activity → fees or economic surplus → actual recipients → active token-holder mechanism → issuance/incentives or dilution → remaining risks. Identify revenue-funded distributions or burns, executed buybacks, treasury-funded support, and their beneficiaries. Separate proposals, authorizations, and execution. Explain what changed in the holder's economic position rather than only the network's headline statistics, and which outcome still needs validation next month.

## 6. Valuation and Changes in Embedded Expectations
This is another main priority of the monthly review.
Use defensible valuation or expectation diagnostics and state each metric's numerator, denominator, supply basis, horizon, and historical comparison sample.
Revenue multiples are not interchangeable with equity P/E; gross fees are not automatically profits or distributable holder cash flow. Circulating valuation and FDV answer different questions and require explicit supply assumptions.

Assess whether prices appear more consistent with:
Improving demand or adoption;
Improving token-holder economics;
A lower required risk premium or speculative multiple expansion;
Supply scarcity or changed dilution expectations;
A combination of these;
Prices moving ahead of deteriorating or unproven economics.

Use qualitative diagnostics unless comparable economic claims and data support precise decomposition.
Use sustained operating periods for valuation, state metric/sample limits and interpret pricing through required economic outcomes.
Explain which future operating or liquidity outcomes prices now seem to require, and what would make those expectations difficult to sustain.

Scan the broad functional market: monetary assets; settlement networks; scaling/data availability; DeFi and trading; stablecoin/payment/RWA infrastructure; staking/security; oracles/interoperability; compute/storage and related AI/DePIN; consumer/gaming/NFT applications; and speculative meme assets.

For connected ecosystems, explain the different effects on networks, applications, suppliers, users, and token holders. Growth in one layer can benefit one participant while reducing another's pricing power.
Explain which areas merit sustained attention, which depend on delivery of already-priced expectations, and which show deterioration despite a popular narrative. Validate medium-term views through adoption, token-holder economics, demand, supply, and liquidity evidence over the relevant horizon.

## 8. Leverage, Infrastructure, and Systemic Vulnerabilities
Assess relevant funding, open interest, basis, options conditions, liquidity concentration, and liquidation episodes in a medium-term context. State units, venues, reference periods, and sample changes.
Separate leverage entering the month, stress during it, and the configuration at month-end. Lower open interest after a shock is not sufficient evidence that liquidity or counterparty risk has normalized.

Examine material stablecoin reserve/redemption risks, custodial or exchange dependencies, collateral concentration, bridge/oracle exposure, protocol security, governance, and financing fragility.
An asset attestation is not automatically a complete solvency assessment. Explain the scope and limitations of reserve or liability evidence.
For each main vulnerability, state the trigger, transmission chain, affected areas, and counterevidence rather than merely listing “black swans.”

Prioritize changes in the medium-term environment, economic evidence, valuation expectations, and vulnerabilities. Integrate a substantive next-month and 1–3-month plan within the same report, without duplicating the retrospective analysis. Broader coverage should not become a catalogue of tokens or a compilation of weekly news.
End with one sentence answering: “Did this month change the demand outlook, token-holder economics, the risk premium the market accepts, or a combination—and which evidence matters most next?”

Lead with conclusions. Prioritize changes, evidence, disagreements, and validation rather than a long news digest or indicator inventory.
If browsing is unavailable, state the limitation, complete verifiable work and label any non-current framework.

Review true prior reports available in authorized context, using their original terms. Complete retrospective analysis and outlook together; future observation items describe research priorities.
