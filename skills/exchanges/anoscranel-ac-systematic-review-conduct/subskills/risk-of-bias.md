# Risk of bias

## Standard

Risk of bias is an assessment of whether a study's methods make its findings believable — distinct from whether the finding is favorable, elegant, or published somewhere impressive. The assessment uses a named tool matched to the study's design, and that tool's judgment domains are evaluated per study with the evidence for each judgment cited: a "high risk" for blinding is only meaningful because the text shows who could not be blinded.

This judgment is separate from two things it is often confused with. It is not a grade of the evidence after synthesis — that grading happens later, over the whole body of findings. And it is not a quality score that mixes conduct, reporting, and importance into one number. Risk of bias is domain-by-domain, study-by-study, and its output is a set of judgments a reader can check against the quoted study text.

Primary sources: <https://training.cochrane.org/handbook> and <https://community.cochrane.org>.

## Decision rules

- Name the tool for each design family: RoB 2 for randomized trials, ROBINS-I for non-randomized, QUADAS for diagnostic accuracy.
- Judge each domain separately and cite the evidence text that supports each judgment.
- Record "some concerns" as its own category rather than rounding to high or low.
- Assess in duplicate for judgment-heavy domains and report disagreement rates as for screening.
- Do not average domains into a single quality score; the domains are reported as they are.
- Record funding and conflicts of interest as bias signals in their own domain.

## Procedure

1. Select the named tool that matches each included study's design.
2. Pilot the tool's judgments on two studies in duplicate.
3. Assess every study's domains, citing the text for every judgment.
4. Resolve judgment disagreements by discussion or a third assessor.
5. Tabulate per study and per domain.
6. Record the plan for using these judgments in the synthesis before the analysis begins.

## Anti-patterns

- A single "good study / bad study" verdict per study.
- Adequate blinding assumed because the study was published.
- The tool switched between studies without explanation.
- Unfavorable results graded as higher risk in the same judgment.
- A composite quality score reported instead of the domain-level judgments.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Tool named per design family.
- [ ] Each domain judged with cited supporting text.
- [ ] "Some concerns" recorded as its own category.
- [ ] Duplicate assessment for judgment-heavy domains.
- [ ] No composite score used.
- [ ] Funding and conflicts recorded.
