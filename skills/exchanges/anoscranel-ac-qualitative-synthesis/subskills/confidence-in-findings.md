# Confidence in findings

## Standard

Qualitative findings have no confidence interval, but they can still be rated for how much the evidence behind them should be believed. The CERQual approach — Confidence in the Evidence from Reviews of Qualitative research — rates each review finding across four domains: methodological limitations of the contributing studies, relevance of the studies to the finding, coherence between the finding and the underlying data, and adequacy of the evidence in depth and coverage. The output is a level — high, moderate, low, or very low confidence — attached to each finding individually, with the reasons recorded.

The rating is per finding, not per review. Different findings in the same synthesis can carry different confidence levels, and the honest review displays that spread rather than settling on one overall grade. The domains also make the judgement inspectable: a reader can disagree with the adequacy call while agreeing on limitations, and the record makes that disagreement possible.

Primary sources: <https://www.biomedcentral.com/methods> and <https://jbi.global/critical-appraisal-tools>.

## Decision rules

- Rate every review finding, each on its own evidence.
- Assess the four domains per finding: methodological limitations, relevance, coherence, adequacy.
- Record the judgement and its reason for each domain, not only the overall level.
- Attach the confidence level to every finding wherever it appears.
- Keep the level separate from importance: a high-confidence finding about a minor theme is not more important.
- Downgrade transparently; any upgrade requires an explicit, argued reason.

## Procedure

1. List every finding the synthesis produced.
2. For each finding, identify its contributing studies and their appraisal profiles.
3. Judge each of the four domains with its reason.
4. Combine the domain judgements into a confidence level using the approach's rules.
5. Record level and reasons per finding.
6. Carry levels into the report and any decision-making material.

## Anti-patterns

- One confidence level for the whole review.
- Rating findings' confidence after seeing users' reactions to them.
- Omitting the domain reasons and publishing only the level.
- Treating importance as confidence or vice versa.
- Upgrading because the finding is needed for a decision.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Every finding rated individually.
- [ ] Four domains judged per finding.
- [ ] Reasons recorded for each domain.
- [ ] Levels attached to findings wherever displayed.
- [ ] Importance kept separate from confidence.
- [ ] Any upgrade argued explicitly.
