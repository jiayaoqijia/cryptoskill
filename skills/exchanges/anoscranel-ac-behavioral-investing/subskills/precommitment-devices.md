# Precommitment devices

## Standard

Willpower is a depleting resource, and the decisions that need it most arrive when it is lowest: after a loss, during a crash, at the end of a bad week. Precommitment moves the decision to a moment when judgement is intact and makes the future moment mechanical. The device is not a promise to be strong; it is an arrangement that makes weakness expensive or impossible.

The classic forms are automatic contributions, rebalancing bands with a stated trigger, cooling-off periods before any discretionary trade, and written rules about what may be changed mid-drawdown. Each converts an intention into a structure, and each is only as good as its enforcement: a rule the owner can suspend is a preference with paperwork.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.jstor.org/stable/1914185>.

## Decision rules

- Every device names the failure it prevents and the moment it acts.
- A cooling-off period is stated in days and applies to any discretionary change.
- Automatic rules are preferred over discretionary ones wherever the decision can be specified in advance.
- The suspension clause, if any, is written explicitly and limited.
- Devices are tested on paper before they are relied on.
- Devices are reviewed only at scheduled reviews, never during a drawdown.

## Procedure

1. Identify the recurring failure from the journal.
2. Specify a device that acts before the failure can.
3. Write the trigger, the action, and the enforcement.
4. Define the suspension clause and its limits.
5. Test the device against a past episode.
6. Add it to the policy and record the date.

## Anti-patterns

- A rule with no enforcement mechanism.
- A cooling-off period that can be waived in the moment.
- Adding devices after every mistake until the system is unusable.
- Writing the suspension clause so broadly it covers everything.
- Relying on a device never tested against a real episode.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Failure identified from the journal.
- [ ] Device specified with trigger and action.
- [ ] Enforcement mechanism named.
- [ ] Suspension clause written and limited.
- [ ] Device tested against a past episode.
- [ ] Device added to policy and dated.

Economic research on commitment devices is consistent on one point: arrangements that make the future action automatic are more effective than arrangements that rely on remembering the rule. Automate the trigger, not the intention.
