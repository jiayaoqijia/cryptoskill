# Narrative nonfiction

## Standard

- **The diamond shape is the long-form architecture.** An anecdotal lede, a nut graf in paragraphs 3–6 with the thesis and urgency, a systemic body, then a return to the opening subject (<https://www.poynter.org/>).
- **Every scene changes a value.** A scene is a unified event bounded by time, space or action that shifts a condition between entrance and exit (<https://www.oupress.com>). A static scene is a non-event.
- **Causal hinges, not episodes.** Connect beats with "therefore" or "but"; beats joined by "and then" mean the momentum has stalled (<https://mckeestory.com>).
- **Truth is the binding constraint.** Composite characters, fabricated quotes and assumed interiority are banned; factual and honest are separate obligations.
- **The customer's words are the asset.** Copy that works is found, not written (<https://mmsvegas.com/library/voice-of-customer-explained>). The customer has already described the problem, the fear and the outcome, and paraphrasing destroys that property.
- **The case-study arc answers the committee's questions.** Buyer profile, trigger event, decision criteria, a short approach, an outcome with before-and-after numbers, and a quote reinforcing the specific outcome (⚠️ practitioner convention).
- **Legal exposure follows the person written about.** Living persons can sue for defamation or privacy invasion even under nicknames (<https://loucheleaves.com/writing-lives-legal-considerations-in-memoir-biography-and-autobiography/>). Anonymise the mosaic, not just the name.

## Decision rules

1. One protagonist per story; a committee is not a character.
2. The trigger event gives urgency — what broke or what deadline appeared.
3. The approach section stays short: what happened in weeks one to four matters.
4. Outcome numbers carry a timeframe.
5. Distribution is part of the story: six surfaces, not one page.
6. Where memory is reconstructed, the author's note says so.
7. Damaging claims about living people route to a human for legal review.

## Procedure

1. Interview for stories, not adjectives: 30 minutes recorded, five open questions sent ahead.
2. Log every quoted line verbatim with its source and date.
3. Write the buyer profile or the subject's situation so the reader self-identifies in seconds.
4. Draft the trigger event, the obstacle, then the turn — each beat changing something.
5. Draft the outcome with numbers and a timeframe.
6. Send the draft back with quotes highlighted; approval covers the quotes only, and the loop caps at two rounds.
7. Run the connective test: therefore or but between every beat. **The human publishes.**
## Anti-patterns

- A vendor arc: the company opens, the company closes, the customer never appears.
- One story written for one role, serving a committee of six to ten.
- A composite presented as fact.
- A scene that restates its opening value at its end.
- Publishing a story and hoping the buyer finds it.

## Done checklist

- [ ] One protagonist; the reader can self-identify.
- [ ] Trigger event stated; the arc changes a value.
- [ ] Beats joined by therefore or but.
- [ ] Quotes verbatim with provenance; approval loop capped.
- [ ] Outcome carries numbers and a timeframe.
- [ ] Anonymisation and memory-reconstruction flags stated.
- [ ] **Human-gated:** published by a named person.
