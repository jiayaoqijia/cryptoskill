# Meta-aggregation

## Standard

Meta-aggregation is the least interpretive of the main approaches. It collects the findings exactly as the primary authors stated them, groups similar findings into categories on the basis of similarity in meaning, and then assembles one or more synthesised findings from those categories. The synthesist's contribution is held to the pattern that the findings themselves support: if the categories do not cohere, the synthesis stays at the category level rather than forcing a general statement.

The value of this discipline is honesty about the level of the claim. Each finding is stated as what it says, each category as what its findings share, and each synthesis as a statement of the pattern — with the count of studies and findings supporting each level attached. A synthesis that outruns its findings generalises, and the audit of what supports each statement is what prevents that.

Primary sources: <https://jbi.global/critical-appraisal-tools> and <https://www.biomedcentral.com/methods>.

## Decision rules

- Collect findings verbatim, attached to study and context.
- Group by similarity of meaning; the category label is the shared idea, not a new one.
- Assemble a synthesis statement only where categories genuinely cohere.
- Attach finding and study counts to every category and every synthesis statement.
- Record the pattern of support: a category of five findings from one study is not five studies.
- Keep the level of claim visible: what the findings said, what the category shares, what the synthesis states.

## Procedure

1. Assemble all findings from the studies into a single table with their sources.
2. Group findings with similar meanings into categories, recording which findings belong to which.
3. Draft one category label per group that states the shared meaning.
4. Assemble syntheses from cohering categories; state each as a claim about the pattern.
5. Attach study counts and finding counts to every category and synthesis.
6. State any synthesis statement that is not fully supported by its findings as an interpretation beyond aggregation, or drop it.

## Anti-patterns

- A category label that introduces a concept no finding mentions.
- A synthesis statement that goes beyond what its findings and categories jointly say.
- Presenting a category with five findings from one study as having study-level support.
- Mixing aggregation and interpretation without labelling which is which.
- Collapsing categories to produce a bolder synthesis.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Findings table with sources complete.
- [ ] Categories grounded in stated findings.
- [ ] Synthesis statements match their categories' coherence.
- [ ] Counts of studies and findings attached at every level.
- [ ] Claim level visible everywhere.
- [ ] Interpretations beyond aggregation labelled or removed.
