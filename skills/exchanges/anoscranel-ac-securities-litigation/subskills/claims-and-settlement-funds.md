# Claims and settlement funds

## Standard

A settlement creates a fund, and the fund is distributed according to a plan of allocation that the court approves. The plan typically pays each class member in proportion to a recognised loss, computed from the inflation in the share price during the class period and the number of shares held. Filing the claim form is what converts a potential entitlement into a payment.

The striking fact about these funds is how much goes unclaimed. Investors change custodians, close accounts, and never receive the notice; institutions with small positions often judge the paperwork not worth the effort. The unclaimed portion is either redistributed to claimants in a further round or returned to the defendant, and either way the holder who did not file received nothing.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the settlement amount, the approval date, and the claim deadline.
- Record the plan of allocation and the recognised-loss method it uses.
- Record the documentation required: transaction records, holdings, and account identifiers.
- Estimate the recovery from the recognised loss and the fund's ratio to total claims.
- Record whether claims administration is handled by a third party and how notice is delivered.
- Record the deadline in advance, since it is not extended for holders who missed the notice.

## Procedure

1. Record the settlement terms and the deadline.
2. Read the plan of allocation.
3. Reconstruct the recognised loss from transaction records.
4. Estimate the recovery at a range of claim ratios.
5. Assemble the documentation.
6. File and record the submission.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming notice will arrive and action is unnecessary.
- Missing the deadline because the notice went to a former custodian.
- Estimating the recovery from the settlement amount alone.
- Ignoring the recognised-loss definition in the plan.
- Filing without retaining proof of submission.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Settlement and deadline recorded.
- [ ] Plan of allocation read.
- [ ] Recognised loss reconstructed.
- [ ] Recovery estimated.
- [ ] Documentation assembled.
- [ ] Submission recorded.

The recognised-loss calculation is where most claims are won or lost in practice: it requires transaction-level records for every purchase and sale in the period, and a holder who can produce them receives a materially different amount from one who files an estimate. Reconstructing those records before the deadline, rather than after, is the difference between a filed claim and an abandoned one.
