# Open versus closed end

## Standard

An open-end fund creates and redeems units at net asset value on demand, so the share count adjusts to flows and the price stays close to the value of the assets. A closed-end fund issues a fixed number of shares that trade on an exchange, so the price is set by supply and demand and can sit at a discount or a premium to net asset value for extended periods.

The difference matters most in stress. An open-end fund facing redemptions must sell assets to meet them, which transmits selling pressure into the underlying market and can force sales at the worst prices; a closed-end fund faces no redemption pressure, so its assets stay intact while its share price absorbs the shock through a widening discount. The first structure risks the portfolio, the second risks the price.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Record the structure: open-end, closed-end, or interval.
- Record the creation and redemption mechanism and its frequency.
- Record the historical premium or discount range for a closed-end fund.
- Record the fund's holdings' liquidity, since an open-end fund must meet redemptions from them.
- Record any gate, swing pricing, or redemption fee in the open-end structure.
- Record the closed-end fund's leverage, which is often used and amplifies the discount's effect.

## Procedure

1. Identify the structure from the prospectus.
2. Record the creation and redemption terms.
3. Record the premium or discount history for a closed-end fund.
4. Assess the underlying holdings' liquidity.
5. Record any anti-dilution mechanisms.
6. Write the comparison with the stress behaviour of each stated.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a closed-end fund's discount as a mispricing without the leverage and liquidity context.
- Assuming an open-end fund can always meet redemptions without selling.
- Ignoring swing pricing, which transfers redemption costs to the leaver.
- Comparing returns across structures without the discount's movement.
- Overlooking that closed-end leverage makes the discount more volatile.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Structure recorded.
- [ ] Creation and redemption terms recorded.
- [ ] Premium or discount history recorded.
- [ ] Holdings liquidity assessed.
- [ ] Anti-dilution mechanisms recorded.
- [ ] Stress behaviour stated for both.

The interval fund sits between the two and is worth naming separately: it offers periodic repurchase at a stated percentage of shares, which limits redemption pressure but does not eliminate it, and the repurchase limits are the term that decides behaviour when flows turn. A fund that repurchases five percent a quarter cannot be exited quickly by everyone at once.
