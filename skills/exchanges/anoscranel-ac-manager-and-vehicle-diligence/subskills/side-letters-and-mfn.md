# Side letters and MFN

## Standard

A side letter grants one investor terms that differ from the fund's standard document. It may reduce fees, grant information rights, excuse a prohibited holding on religious or regulatory grounds, or adjust the liquidity terms. Every side letter means the fund has more than one set of terms, and the allocator's position depends on which set applies to them.

The most-favoured-nation provision is the mechanism intended to limit the divergence: it gives an investor the right to elect the better terms granted to another investor in defined categories. Its value depends entirely on its scope — which terms are covered, whether elections must be made within a window, and whether the manager must disclose the existence of better terms at all. An MFN without a disclosure obligation is a right the holder cannot exercise.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record whether the allocator holds a side letter and its terms.
- Record the MFN clause, the categories it covers, and the election window.
- Record whether the manager must disclose the existence of more favourable terms.
- Record any terms that are excluded from the MFN, such as regulatory accommodations.
- Record how elections are made and whether they are automatic.
- Record the fund's practice on disclosure of side letters to other investors.

## Procedure

1. Read the allocator's side letter and the standard terms.
2. Record the differences and their value.
3. Read the MFN clause and its scope.
4. Record the disclosure obligation and the election mechanics.
5. Record the exclusions.
6. Write the terms comparison.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming the standard document is the allocator's terms.
- Reading an MFN without its disclosure obligation.
- Missing an election window that has already closed.
- Treating a regulatory accommodation as a preference.
- Ignoring the value of a side letter that grants information rights.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Side letter and standard terms recorded.
- [ ] Differences and value recorded.
- [ ] MFN scope recorded.
- [ ] Disclosure obligation recorded.
- [ ] Exclusions recorded.
- [ ] Comparison written.

The MFN's practical value also depends on whether elections are made once or continuously: a clause that permits election only at the initial closing gives the first investors an advantage over later ones, while one that permits periodic election equalises them at the cost of administrative burden. Reading which applies determines whether the right is worth exercising at all.
