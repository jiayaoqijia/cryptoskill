# Litigation record

## Standard

The record states the case, the class definition and how the holding falls inside or outside it, every deadline that binds the holder, the estimated recovery under the settlement plan, and the decisions taken — whether to file, whether to opt out, and on what arithmetic. Those decisions are the ones that carry money, and they are made once, on a timetable the holder does not control.

It also states the outcome and the amount actually received, which is what allows a later review to compare the estimate with reality. Litigation recovery is a small line in most portfolios, but the process of estimating it is the same process used for larger contingent claims, and the record is where that discipline is either kept or lost.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record names the case, the court, the class period, and the security.
- The holding's position inside or outside the class is recorded with the dates tested.
- Every deadline is recorded with its consequence for missing it.
- The estimated recovery is recorded with the plan of allocation and the recognised loss.
- The filing and opt-out decisions are recorded with the arithmetic behind them.
- The outcome and amount received are recorded when known, alongside the estimate.

## Procedure

1. Record the case identifiers and class definition.
2. Test the holding against the class period.
3. Record deadlines and their consequences.
4. Record the recovery estimate and its basis.
5. Record the decisions and their arithmetic.
6. Record the outcome and compare with the estimate.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording the case without the holding dates that determine eligibility.
- Leaving a deadline implicit.
- Recording an estimate without the plan of allocation.
- Omitting the reasoning behind an opt-out decision.
- Never closing the record with the amount received.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Case and class definition recorded.
- [ ] Holding tested against the period.
- [ ] Deadlines recorded with consequences.
- [ ] Recovery estimate recorded with basis.
- [ ] Decisions recorded with arithmetic.
- [ ] Outcome recorded and compared.

The record also accumulates across cases: after several settlements, a holder can see which administrators delivered notices reliably, which plans produced recoveries close to the estimates, and where the process failed, and that history is what turns a repetitive administrative task into a managed one.
