# Net operating income

## Standard

Net operating income is rental revenue plus recoveries, less operating expenses, before debt service, capital expenditure, and tax. It is the number every property valuation is built on, and it is also the number most easily flattered: a one-off item in revenue, a deferred expense, or a recovery structure that shifts cost to tenants can each raise NOI without changing the property's economics.

The test is to rebuild it rather than accept it. Start from the rent roll, apply the contracted escalations, add recoveries at the rate the leases permit, subtract the actual expenses at a level that maintains the building, and compare the result with the reported figure. Where they differ, the difference is the part of the valuation that is an assumption rather than a contract.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.federalreserve.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Record contractual rent, escalations, and the rent roll's expiry profile.
- Record recoveries and the lease clauses that permit them.
- Record operating expenses at a level that maintains the building.
- Record any one-off revenue and exclude it from a stabilised figure.
- Record the treatment of capital expenditure, which sits below NOI.
- Reconcile your rebuilt figure with the reported one and explain the gap.

## Procedure

1. Record the rent roll and contracted escalations.
2. Record recovery income and its lease basis.
3. Record actual and normalised operating expenses.
4. Exclude one-off items and record the exclusion.
5. Rebuild NOI and compare with the reported figure.
6. Write the reconciliation with the gap explained.

## Anti-patterns

- Accepting a reported NOI without the rent roll.
- Including a one-off item in a stabilised figure.
- Treating recoveries as revenue when they offset a cost.
- Using expenses below the level needed to maintain the building.
- Comparing NOI across properties with different recovery structures.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Rent roll and escalations recorded.
- [ ] Recoveries recorded.
- [ ] Expenses normalised.
- [ ] One-offs excluded.
- [ ] NOI rebuilt and compared.
- [ ] Gap explained.

The comparison that keeps the rebuild honest is against the prior year's normalised figure rather than the reported one: a property whose normalised NOI is flat while its reported NOI grows is a property whose growth is coming from items that will not repeat, and the pattern is visible only when both series are kept.
