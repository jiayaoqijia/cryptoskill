# Bookbuilding and allocation

## Standard

A bookbuild collects indications of demand from investors across a price range, and the issuer and its bankers use that information to set a final price and to decide who receives shares. The process is an auction with discretion: the book tells the issuer where demand sits, and the allocation lets the bank favour investors it expects to hold rather than flip.

That discretion is why allocation is information. A book that is heavily oversubscribed at the top of the range prices there, and a book that is thin prices lower or is pulled; a book that is covered only by a few accounts prices but leaves a concentrated register that can sell in one block afterwards. Reading the disclosed coverage and the allocation pattern tells you more about the aftermarket than the offer price does.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.finra.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the price range, the final price, and where it sits in the range.
- Record the disclosed oversubscription and the number of accounts.
- Record the allocation policy described and any priority given to long-only holders.
- Record the free float after the issue and the size relative to the existing shares.
- Record the use of proceeds and whether it funds growth or repays debt.
- Record the syndicate structure and the stabilising manager.

## Procedure

1. Read the prospectus and the pricing announcement.
2. Record range, final price, and coverage.
3. Record allocation policy and free float.
4. Record proceeds and their use.
5. Record the syndicate and stabilising manager.
6. Write the summary with the aftermarket implications named.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating oversubscription as proof of quality.
- Ignoring the free float, which decides subsequent liquidity.
- Assuming an allocation policy is applied as described.
- Reading the offer price as the market's valuation.
- Overlooking the use of proceeds in a secondary sale.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Range and final price recorded.
- [ ] Coverage and account count recorded.
- [ ] Allocation policy recorded.
- [ ] Free float recorded.
- [ ] Proceeds and use recorded.
- [ ] Syndicate and stabilising manager recorded.

The pricing decision also carries information about the issuer's own view: an issuer that prices at the bottom of the range and still allocates to long-only accounts is signalling that it wants a stable register more than it wants the last dollar, while one that prices at the top and allocates to fast money is taking the cash and accepting the aftermarket. The disclosed outcome, read this way, is a statement of intent.
