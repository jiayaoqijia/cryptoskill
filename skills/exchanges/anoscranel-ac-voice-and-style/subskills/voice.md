# Voice

## Standard

- **Voice is invariant identity; tone is the variable on it.** Microsoft: "Though our voice is constant regardless of who we're talking to or what we're saying, we adapt our tone… to fit the context and the customer's state of mind" (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/brand-voice-above-all-simple-human). Mailchimp: "Our voice doesn't change much from day to day, but our tone changes all the time" (https://styleguide.mailchimp.com/voice-and-tone).
- **The governance ladder has three tiers.** The MLA Style Center names them: style manuals that "set standards in an entire field or industry"; in-house guides that "standardize style across… publications produced by an organization"; and per-manuscript style sheets for usage "not codified by a style manual or in-house guide" (https://style.mla.org/style-manuals-sheets-guides).
 A sheet change "may be made instantaneously"; a guide revision "may invite an entire editorial department to grapple".
- **Microsoft's three voice principles** — "Warm and relaxed", "Crisp and clear", "Ready to lend a hand" — convert into checkable tips: "Get to the point fast", "Talk like a person", "Simpler is better" (same Microsoft page).
- **Voice is setting-dependent.**
 Individual language style "changes significantly from a monolog to a conversation" (https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32301530), so identical prose in every context is mis-calibration, not consistency.
- ⚠️ No retrieved study links a measured voice metric to an evaluator's score (<https://gradingpen.com/blog/creative-writing-grading-rubric>).

## Decision rules

1. Write three to five principles, each as a do/don't pair with a rule attached. "Say *about*, not *approximately*" passes; "be conversational" fails.
2. Keep one voice; scale intensity per surface. Technical content "tends to be more technical", but the fundamentals "still apply… as appropriate for the context" (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/developer-content).
3. Split the voice only where a surface's rules force it. ⚠️ The one split/keep table available is vendor advice with no data.
4. Demote "find your voice" and "write like you talk". Voice is a distance from a template, not a discovery, and monolog and conversation have different signatures.

## Procedure

1. Collect three to five samples the owner signs as sounding right. Record the recurring moves, not the topics.
2. Draft the principles. Cut any pair a reviewer cannot apply without guessing.
3. Build one context guide per surface (developer docs, sales decks, compliance letters) naming which principles intensify and which drop.
4. Build the per-artefact sheet last: decisions neither upper tier covers. This layer closes the open field.
5. Signpost register shifts inside a document — a subheading and an explicit reorientation sentence at each pivot. An unsignalled pivot reads as "gear grinding".
6. Audit by sampling two documents per surface against the sheet. Fix the sheet when the principle was ambiguous.

## Anti-patterns

- One identical voice in an email and a compliance letter.
- Voice "bleed": one project's patterns leaking into another's text.
- A guide nobody can check: adjectives with no rule.
- Expecting a voice signature from generated text: the fingerprint does not survive generation.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Principles written as do/don't pairs, each with a rule.
- [ ] One voice, context-scoped, with a per-surface guide.
- [ ] Per-artefact sheet exists for uncovered decisions.
- [ ] Register shifts signposted in multi-venue documents.
- [ ] Sample audit run and recorded.
- [ ] **Human-gated:** a human approves publishing any voice or style guide.
