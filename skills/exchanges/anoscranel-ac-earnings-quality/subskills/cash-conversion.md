# Cash conversion

## Standard

Cash conversion measures how much of reported profit arrives as cash, usually as operating cash flow divided by net income. A ratio near one hundred percent indicates that earnings are being collected; a ratio persistently below it indicates that growth is being financed by the balance sheet, through receivables that grow faster than sales or inventory that builds ahead of demand.

The cash cycle puts the same facts in days: days sales outstanding plus days inventory less days payables. A cycle that lengthens while revenue grows is a signal that the growth is being bought with working capital, and a cycle that shortens can mean either efficiency or a business that is collecting aggressively at the expense of future sales. The record states the cycle and its trend rather than a single reading.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record operating cash flow and net income per period, and their ratio.
- Record days sales outstanding, days inventory, and days payables.
- Compute the cash cycle and record its trend.
- Record which component moved and by how much.
- Record any change in payment terms with customers or suppliers.
- Record the seasonal pattern so that a normal build is not read as a deterioration.

## Procedure

1. Pull the statements and the working-capital notes.
2. Compute the conversion ratio per period.
3. Compute each days measure and the cycle.
4. Identify the component that moved.
5. Compare with the seasonal pattern.
6. Record the questions the trend raises.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading conversion across periods with different seasonality.
- Treating a lengthening cycle as deterioration without the mix.
- Ignoring a change in payment terms.
- Comparing conversion across companies with different business models.
- Using one year's ratio as the structural level.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Conversion ratio computed per period.
- [ ] Days measures computed.
- [ ] Cycle and trend recorded.
- [ ] Component movement identified.
- [ ] Terms changes recorded.
- [ ] Seasonality accounted for.

The cycle also interacts with growth: a company growing quickly will absorb cash into working capital even when its unit economics are sound, which is why the record should compare the cycle's lengthening with the growth rate rather than reading it alone, and why a decelerating company with a still-lengthening cycle is the more informative combination.
