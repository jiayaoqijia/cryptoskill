# Behavioural review

## Standard

Outcomes are noisy, and judging decisions by outcomes teaches the wrong lessons: a good process can produce a bad result, and a bad process can produce a good one. The behavioural review separates the two by scoring the decision at the time it was made against the information then available, and asking whether the process was followed — not whether the result was pleasant.

The review is also where the bias inventory and the devices are updated. A journal entry that reveals a rule was suspended, a script that was not followed, or a decision taken under time pressure is the raw material for the next device. Without the review, the journal accumulates and teaches nothing.

Primary sources: <https://osf.io/prereg/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Decisions are scored on process, outcome, and luck separately.
- The scoring uses the journal's contemporaneous record, never memory.
- A good outcome from a broken process is recorded as luck and does not become a precedent.
- Every review produces at most two changes, to keep the system usable.
- Changes are recorded with dates and reviewed at the next cycle.
- The review is scheduled, not triggered by results.

## Procedure

1. Pull the journal entries closed since the last review.
2. Score each on process (was the rule followed) and outcome (did the expectation hold).
3. Classify: process right and outcome right, right and wrong, wrong and right, wrong and wrong.
4. Identify the recurring failure patterns.
5. Choose at most two changes; write them with dates.
6. Record the review and carry the changes to the next cycle.

## Anti-patterns

- Judging decisions by results alone.
- Changing the process after a single bad outcome.
- Making ten changes and following none.
- Reviewing from memory rather than the journal.
- Skipping the review when results are good.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Journal entries pulled for the period.
- [ ] Each scored on process and outcome separately.
- [ ] Four-way classification applied.
- [ ] Recurring failures identified.
- [ ] At most two changes written with dates.
- [ ] Review recorded and carried forward.

The review's discipline is arithmetic: count the decisions, count the ones where the process held, and compare the two across quarters. That number moves slowly and honestly, unlike a return figure, and it is the only one that tells you whether the system is working.
