# TVPI, DPI and RVPI

## Standard

Total value to paid-in capital divides a fund's net asset value plus cumulative distributions by the capital called. It is the headline multiple and it mixes realised cash with an estimate of unrealised value. DPI divides only distributions by paid-in capital, so it measures cash actually returned. RVPI divides only residual value by paid-in, so it measures what remains and is therefore entirely dependent on the valuation.

The three answer different questions and disagree over a fund's life. A young fund shows a high TVPI and a near-zero DPI, which says nothing about outcomes; a mature fund with a high TVPI and a low DPI is telling you the value is still a mark. Reading them as a triple rather than a single number is the discipline, and pairing them with the fund's age is what makes the triple interpretable.

Primary sources: <https://www.cfainstitute.org/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record all three multiples with the as-of date.
- Record paid-in capital and the cumulative distributions separately.
- Record the residual value and who produced the valuation.
- Record the fund's age and its position in the investment period.
- Note the proportion of TVPI that is unrealised.
- Where the fund reports net and gross, record which multiple uses which.

## Procedure

1. Record paid-in, distributed, and residual value.
2. Compute the three multiples and check them against the reported figures.
3. Record the unrealised share of total value.
4. Record the valuation source and date.
5. Record the fund's age.
6. Write the multiple summary with the unrealised share named.

## Anti-patterns

- Quoting TVPI as if it were cash returned.
- Comparing a young fund's DPI with a mature one's.
- Treating the residual value as a market price.
- Using multiples without the fund's age.
- Mixing gross and net multiples in one comparison.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Three multiples recorded with date.
- [ ] Components recorded.
- [ ] Unrealised share computed.
- [ ] Valuation source recorded.
- [ ] Fund age recorded.
- [ ] Summary names the unrealised share.

The ratio that is hardest to misread is distributions divided by paid-in capital, because it cannot be inflated by a mark: it is cash that has arrived. A fund with a strong TVPI and a weak DPI is a fund whose reported performance depends on valuations that have not yet been tested by a sale, and that pairing is the single most informative reading of a private-fund report.
