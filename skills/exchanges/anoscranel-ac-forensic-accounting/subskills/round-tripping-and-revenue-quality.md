# Round-tripping and revenue quality

## Standard

Revenue is recognised when goods or services transfer to a customer, and the question a forensic reader asks is who the customer is and where the cash came from. Revenue that grows without a corresponding cash inflow, or that arrives from counterparties with no visible business, or that circulates back through an investment or a loan, is revenue whose quality is unproven rather than revenue that is false.

The evidence trail is in the filings: receivables growing faster than revenue, days sales outstanding extending, customer concentration increasing, and investing cash outflows that correspond in size and timing to operating inflows. Each of those is a documented observation, and together they justify the question rather than the conclusion. The record should cite the line items and periods that produced the observation.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.nber.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record revenue and operating cash flow across periods and the relationship between them.
- Record receivables, days sales outstanding, and their trend against revenue.
- Record customer concentration and any change in its disclosure.
- Record investing outflows that correspond in timing to operating inflows.
- Record any counterparty whose business is not described.
- State the observation and the question, never the conclusion.

## Procedure

1. Record revenue and cash flow by period.
2. Compute receivables intensity and days sales outstanding.
3. Record concentration disclosures and changes.
4. Trace unusually matched investing and operating flows.
5. Record counterparties with thin descriptions.
6. Write the questions with their line items cited.

## Anti-patterns

- Concluding fraud from a pattern that has a legitimate explanation.
- Reading one period instead of a trend.
- Ignoring the investing section while examining the operating section.
- Treating concentration as evidence rather than as a question.
- Failing to cite the specific line items.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Revenue and cash flow recorded by period.
- [ ] Receivables intensity computed.
- [ ] Concentration recorded.
- [ ] Corresponding flows traced.
- [ ] Counterparties recorded.
- [ ] Questions cited to line items.

The timing correspondence is the part that is hardest to explain innocently: an operating inflow matched in size and period by an investing outflow to a counterparty that also appears as a customer suggests the money may be returning rather than being earned, and the record should present the matching as a question with both line items cited rather than as a finding.
