# Regulatory clearance

## Standard

A signed deal is not a closed deal until the required approvals are obtained, and antitrust review is the approval most able to change or kill a transaction. Merger control regimes define notification thresholds by turnover or transaction value, and closing before clearance where required can expose the parties to fines and even unwinding. The review agencies ask a competition question — will the combination reduce competition in any relevant market? — and remedies up to divestitures are the negotiated answer when it does.

Beyond competition, deals cross approvals tied to their industry and geography: foreign investment screening where a buyer's origin triggers review, sector regulators for financial, energy, telecommunications and similar businesses, and shareholder approvals on each side, which are corporate rather than regulatory but share the same function of gating the closing. The sequence matters, because some clearances cannot start until filings are made and some run in parallel while others are conditioned on earlier clearances.

Primary sources: <https://www.ftc.gov/> and <https://www.justice.gov/atr/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the notification thresholds and regimes the transaction crosses, by jurisdiction.
- Record the filing timeline and the expected review clock, including any suspended or extended periods.
- Identify sector-specific and foreign-investment approvals applicable to the businesses.
- Record the closing conditions that depend on each approval.
- Note any remedies already negotiated and the divestiture commitments they imply.
- Record the outside date and its extension mechanics, which bound how long the parties must wait.

## Procedure

1. Determine the jurisdictions' notification thresholds the deal crosses.
2. Record each regime's filing requirements, timeline, and review mechanics.
3. Identify sector and foreign-investment approvals by business and geography.
4. Map the interdependency of approvals on closing conditions.
5. Record remedy negotiations and any commitments given.
6. Write the timeline as a dated sequence with the outside date visible.

## Anti-patterns

- Closing a notifiable transaction before clearance.
- Assuming jurisdictions' timelines run independently of their remedy negotiations.
- Overseas foreign-investment screening because the competition review is clean.
- Treating the outside date as a formality rather than the deal's real deadline.
- Ignoring that divestiture remedies affect the deal model.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Notification regimes and thresholds identified.
- [ ] Filing timelines and review clocks recorded.
- [ ] Sector and FDI approvals identified.
- [ ] Closing conditions mapped to approvals.
- [ ] Remedies and commitments recorded.
- [ ] Outside date and extensions stated.
