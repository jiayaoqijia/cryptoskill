# Section Planning

## Standard

Section planning translates gathered context into an answer-first structural skeleton before any prose drafting begins. Grounded in Barbara Minto's Pyramid Principle (1987), this stage structures document sections as logical answers to the questions the reader will naturally ask in sequence, starting with the primary conclusion or recommendation. The standard requires defining strict scope boundaries, assigning one distinct outcome per section, and securing explicit author sign-off on the skeleton. ⚠️ Note: While Minto's pyramid framework is widely adopted in professional communication, empirical cognitive science studies regarding strict hierarchical reading paths remain variable across diverse digital audiences.

## Decision rules

1. Order document sections in answer-first sequence, placing the governing conclusion or recommendation at the very top.
2. Formulate each section heading as an action statement or explicit question rather than a vague topic label.
3. Define clear out-of-scope boundaries to prevent scope creep and maintain tight document focus.
4. Require author confirmation of the complete section skeleton before writing any detailed section content.
5. Ensure every section serves exactly one distinct outcome supporting the overall document purpose.

## Procedure

1. Review the gathered context parameters, reader profile, and decision mandate from Stage 1.
2. Draft an outline where sections flow logically from the executive conclusion down to supporting evidence and implementation details.
3. Annotate each section with a one-sentence summary of its core argument and target reader takeaway.
4. Present the proposed skeleton to the author for review, highlighting section headings and scope exclusions.
5. Incorporate author feedback, adjust section boundaries if necessary, and obtain formal sign-off to enter the iterative building stage.

## Anti-patterns

- Chronological or exploratory ordering that buries the main conclusion at the bottom of the document.
- Using vague noun-phrase headings like Background or Discussion instead of informative action statements.
- Skipping author review of the skeleton and diving straight into writing multi-page text blocks.
- Allowing overlapping section outcomes that duplicate arguments and confuse the reader.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Skeleton structured in answer-first order with conclusion upfront.
- [ ] Section headings framed as informative action statements.
- [ ] Out-of-scope boundaries explicitly defined.
- [ ] Author sign-off obtained on the final outline before drafting.
