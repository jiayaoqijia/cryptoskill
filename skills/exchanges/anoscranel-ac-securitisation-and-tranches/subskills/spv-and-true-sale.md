# SPV and true sale

## Standard

A securitisation works only if the assets belong to the vehicle rather than to the originator. That separation requires a true sale: a transfer structured so that a court would treat the assets as no longer the originator's property, and the vehicle as a separate legal person whose own creditors are its noteholders. Without it, an originator insolvency pulls the pool into the estate and the notes become general claims.

Bankruptcy remoteness is the second half. The vehicle is usually a shell with no other business, no employees, and restrictions on what it may do, so that it cannot itself fail for reasons unrelated to the pool. Those restrictions are covenants in the transaction documents, and they are enforceable only to the extent the documents provide.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.federalreserve.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the vehicle's legal form, jurisdiction, and ownership.
- Record the true-sale opinion and the representations supporting it.
- Record the non-consolidation opinion where one is provided.
- Record what happens to the pool if the originator becomes insolvent.
- Record the vehicle's permitted activities and its independence covenants.
- Record any repurchase or substitution obligation if assets breach representations.

## Procedure

1. Identify the issuer vehicle and its jurisdiction.
2. Record the transfer mechanism and the true-sale representations.
3. Record the non-consolidation and bankruptcy-remoteness opinions.
4. Record the originator's repurchase obligations.
5. Record the vehicle's activity restrictions.
6. Write the separation summary with its limits.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming the vehicle is remote because it is described as such.
- Ignoring the originator's repurchase obligations, which are a real exposure.
- Treating an opinion as a guarantee rather than as a legal assessment at a date.
- Overlooking the vehicle's own creditors.
- Assuming separation survives a change in the originator's jurisdiction.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Vehicle and jurisdiction recorded.
- [ ] True-sale opinion recorded.
- [ ] Non-consolidation recorded.
- [ ] Insolvency treatment recorded.
- [ ] Activity restrictions recorded.
- [ ] Repurchase obligations recorded.

The practical test is to ask what a noteholder would own if the originator failed tomorrow: a claim on a pool that a court has already recognised as someone else's property, or a general unsecured claim sharing with every other creditor. The opinions exist to answer that question, and their qualifications — usually about the facts assumed and the jurisdiction — are the part worth reading.
