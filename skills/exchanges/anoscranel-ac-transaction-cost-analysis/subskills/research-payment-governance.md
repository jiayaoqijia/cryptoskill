# Research payment governance

## Standard

Research used to be paid for out of dealing commission, which meant the investor funded it without seeing the invoice. Unbundling separates the two: the research charge becomes an explicit amount, disclosed and agreed, and the execution commission reflects execution alone. The purpose is not to reduce research spend but to make the decision about it visible to the person bearing the cost.

Governance is what makes the separation real. A research payment account needs a budget set in advance, a stated methodology for how the charge is calculated, disclosure of the total, and a process for deciding what is bought. Without those, the arrangement reverts to an implicit subsidy with extra administration, and the record should document each element rather than asserting that the policy exists.

Primary sources: <https://www.finra.org/> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the budget, who set it, and the period it covers.
- Record the methodology for calculating the research charge.
- Record what is eligible as research and what is not.
- Record the disclosure made to the person bearing the cost.
- Record the process for approving spend and for reviewing it.
- Record any residual commission arrangement and its justification.

## Procedure

1. Record the budget and its approval.
2. Record the charge methodology.
3. Record eligibility criteria for research.
4. Record the disclosure provided.
5. Record the approval and review process.
6. Write the governance summary with its evidence.

## Anti-patterns

- Describing a policy without the budget or the methodology.
- Treating all broker services as research.
- Setting a budget that is never reviewed against spend.
- Leaving the disclosure to the annual report alone.
- Assuming unbundling removes the conflict rather than exposing it.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Budget and approval recorded.
- [ ] Methodology recorded.
- [ ] Eligibility recorded.
- [ ] Disclosure recorded.
- [ ] Review process recorded.
- [ ] Residual arrangements recorded.

The governance question that is hardest to answer honestly is what happens when the budget is not spent: an unspent research budget that reverts to the manager creates an incentive to spend it, while one that reverts to the client creates an incentive to underspend on research that would have improved decisions, and the policy should state which it chose.

The disclosure that makes the arrangement auditable is the total charge expressed as a share of assets under management, because that is the figure a client can compare across managers, and a per-broker list without the aggregate leaves the cost invisible at the level where it is actually borne.
