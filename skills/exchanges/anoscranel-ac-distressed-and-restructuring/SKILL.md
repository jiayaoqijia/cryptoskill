# Distressed and restructuring

## Overview

A distressed capital structure is a queue, and the work is locating yourself in it. Every claim sits somewhere in a priority waterfall, and the question that determines value is not what the enterprise is worth in the abstract but which layer of the queue that value reaches. Once the fulcrum sits between two layers, the instruments above it are money-good and the instruments below it are an option on a recovery that may not exist.

This skill covers the mechanics of that process: how priority and absolute priority constrain outcomes, how a company buys time with DIP financing, what the automatic stay does to claims and to trade counterparties, how claims trade while the process runs, and how a plan is negotiated, voted, and confirmed. The arithmetic is recovery per claim; the judgment is about process risk, which is not the same as credit risk.

Primary sources: <https://www.uscourts.gov/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## When to use

- When analysing a capital structure where the fulcrum security is in question.
- When a borrower is negotiating an exchange or entering a court process.
- When a DIP facility or a plan of reorganisation is announced.
- When claims are trading and the settlement mechanics matter.

## When NOT to use

- For ordinary credit quality and coverage ratio analysis (use ac-bank-and-credit-analysis).
- For spread, duration, and bond pricing mechanics (use ac-fixed-income-and-rates).
- For convertible bond parity and bond floor (use ac-convertible-and-hybrid-capital).

## Status and honest limits

Restructuring outcomes are decided by negotiation, court rulings, and financing markets at a specific moment, none of which a model can anticipate. The arithmetic here is straightforward; the uncertainty is in valuation disputes, litigation risk, and the availability of exit financing. Nothing here recommends a distressed security, and any recovery estimate should carry its valuation assumption and the date it was made, because in this process the enterprise value input is the argument.
