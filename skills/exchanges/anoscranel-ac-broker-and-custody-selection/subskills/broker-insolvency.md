# Broker insolvency

## Standard

When a broker fails, the question is not whether the assets exist but whether they are identifiable as the client's. Segregated assets are returned through an administrator; unsegregated ones are claims in a queue. The difference between those two paths is measured in months rather than days, and the investor's portfolio is frozen throughout, which is a real cost even when the assets are eventually recovered in full.

Compensation schemes sit behind the segregation. They typically cover a capped amount per client and only certain shortfalls, so they are a backstop rather than a guarantee. Reading the cap, the scope, and the claim process before choosing a provider is the whole point: a large account spread across two providers may be better protected than one concentrated at a single firm.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Record the segregation arrangement and the administrator process in the applicable jurisdiction.
- Record the compensation scheme's cap, scope, and claim route.
- Compare the account size with the cap; where it exceeds it, record the concentration.
- Record the expected timeline from comparable failures if available.
- Note whether any assets are held outside the segregated perimeter.
- Where the jurisdiction's rules are unclear, record the question rather than a conclusion.

## Procedure

1. Establish the jurisdiction and the relevant insolvency regime.
2. Record the segregation and the administrator process.
3. Record the compensation cap and what it does not cover.
4. Compare account size with the cap.
5. Record any assets outside the protected perimeter.
6. Write the assessment with its timeline assumptions.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a compensation scheme as insurance for the whole balance.
- Ignoring the frozen period as a cost.
- Assuming all assets in the account are inside the perimeter.
- Spreading assets without checking whether the second provider is comparably protected.
- Relying on a marketing statement about safety.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Jurisdiction and regime recorded.
- [ ] Segregation and administrator process recorded.
- [ ] Compensation cap and scope recorded.
- [ ] Account size compared with the cap.
- [ ] Assets outside the perimeter identified.
- [ ] Timeline assumption stated.

The frozen period is the cost that is easiest to forget: assets that are returned in full after a year are still assets that could not be sold during the fall that made the failure likely, and a record that names that risk is a record that can size the position it justifies.
