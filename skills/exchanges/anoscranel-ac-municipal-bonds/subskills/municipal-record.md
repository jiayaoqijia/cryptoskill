# Municipal record

## Standard

The municipal record states the pledge, the tax status, the call schedule, and the credit findings, with the dates of the disclosure each rests on. Those four things decide the return, and each of them is easy to state imprecisely: a pledge described loosely hides the difference between a revenue bond and a general obligation, and a tax status described without the investor's bracket is not a comparison at all.

Its second function is to hold the pension arithmetic. The unfunded liability is the part of municipal credit that is least visible and most consequential, and it lives in the notes to the financial statements rather than on the cover. Recording the funded ratio, the discount rate, and the contribution trend at entry means a later review can see whether the obligation improved or deteriorated without rereading a hundred pages.

Primary sources: <https://emma.msrb.org/> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the issuer, the bond, and the official statement date.
- The pledge is named precisely, with the revenue source for a revenue bond.
- The tax-equivalent yield is computed against the holder's marginal rate.
- The call schedule and the call-adjusted yield are recorded.
- The pension and OPEB findings are recorded with their assumptions.
- Review triggers are dated: annual disclosure, rating action, call date, material event notice.

## Procedure

1. Record the issuer, the bond, and the disclosure dates.
2. State the pledge and the revenue source.
3. Compute the tax-equivalent yield.
4. Record the call schedule and the lowest yield.
5. Record the pension findings and assumptions.
6. Set the review triggers and amend at each.

## Anti-patterns

- Describing the pledge without the revenue source.
- Recording yield to maturity without the call adjustment.
- Omitting the pension findings, which are the hidden leverage.
- Using an undated disclosure.
- No trigger for the annual filing, so the credit is never revisited.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Issuer, bond, and disclosure dates recorded.
- [ ] Pledge and revenue source stated.
- [ ] Tax-equivalent yield computed.
- [ ] Call schedule and adjusted yield recorded.
- [ ] Pension findings recorded.
- [ ] Triggers dated.

Recording the credit's direction rather than only its level is what makes the record useful: a funded ratio that fell two points while the contribution stayed flat is a different finding from one that fell two points while the contribution doubled, and stating both numbers with their trend turns an annual filing into a signal about which way the issuer is moving.
