# Inventory and receivables finance

## Standard

A borrowing base facility lends against inventory and receivables, with the available amount determined by the value of the assets and a set of eligibility rules. The lender's protection is not the assets themselves but the control over them: the right to inspect, to require the proceeds of receivables to be paid into a controlled account, and to exclude ineligible items from the base.

The eligibility rules do the work. Inventory that is slow-moving, stored at a third party, or subject to retention of title may be excluded, and receivables from a concentrated customer or beyond a stated age are typically excluded too. A facility whose borrowing base includes assets the lender could not actually realise in a default is a facility secured on arithmetic.

The dilution rate deserves a number rather than an assumption: it reflects the historical proportion of invoices that are disputed, credited, or never paid, and a rate set below the borrower's own experience means the borrowing base is overstated from the first draw. Computing it from the borrower's own receivables history, not from an industry average, is what keeps the base honest.

The discretionary reserve is the provision that makes the facility's headline size least reliable: a lender that may impose reserves against slow-moving inventory or a deteriorating receivables pool can reduce availability precisely when the borrower needs it, and the facility agreement usually permits this without consent. Recording the reserve language, and the conditions under which it can be applied, is what shows the facility's true capacity in stress rather than in calm.

Primary sources: <https://www.bis.org/> and <https://www.federalreserve.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the borrowing base formula and each eligibility criterion.
- Record the dilution rate applied to receivables and how it is calculated.
- Record the concentration limits by customer and by inventory type.
- Record the control accounts and the direction of payment instructions.
- Record the inspection rights and their frequency.
- Record the reserves the lender may impose at its discretion.

## Procedure

1. Record the base formula and eligibility rules.
2. Record dilution and concentration limits.
3. Record the control account arrangements.
4. Record inspection rights and practice.
5. Record discretionary reserves.
6. Write the facility summary with what is excluded.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading the facility size as the available amount.
- Ignoring eligibility rules that exclude the borrower's main inventory.
- Overlooking a concentration limit that cuts the base sharply.
- Assuming control accounts are in place when only promised.
- Treating a discretionary reserve as unlikely.
- Using an industry-average dilution rate instead of the borrower's own history.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Base formula and eligibility criteria recorded.
- [ ] Dilution rate computed from the borrower's history.
- [ ] Concentration limits recorded.
- [ ] Control accounts and payment direction recorded.
- [ ] Inspection rights recorded.
- [ ] Discretionary reserve language recorded with its conditions.
- [ ] Excluded assets named.
