# Access and transfer

## Standard

An account's value includes the ability to get the money out. Withdrawal limits, settlement delays, identity re-verification, and transfer procedures determine how quickly assets can be reached, and they are tested at exactly the moments when speed matters: an emergency, a move to another provider, or the discovery that a better option exists.

Transfer friction is the quiet version of a lock-in. A provider that charges an exit fee, requires paper forms, or takes weeks to release positions imposes a cost that never appears in a fee comparison. Recording that friction before opening the account is cheaper than discovering it afterwards, and it is the practical test of whether the provider's incentives favour keeping the assets over serving the holder.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record withdrawal limits, settlement timing, and any holding periods.
- Record the transfer process for each asset type, including the forms and the expected duration.
- Record exit and transfer fees, and any minimum balance before closure.
- Record jurisdiction restrictions on the account or on specific assets.
- Record what identity or documentation is required to release funds in an emergency.
- Record the friction as a cost, quantified if possible, alongside the fees.

## Procedure

1. Read the withdrawal and transfer sections of the terms.
2. Record limits, delays, and documentation requirements.
3. Record the transfer process and its expected duration.
4. Record exit fees and closure conditions.
5. Identify jurisdiction restrictions.
6. Write the friction assessment with its costs.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming an online account can be emptied online.
- Ignoring the paperwork required to transfer positions.
- Comparing providers on cost without comparing exit friction.
- Discovering a jurisdiction restriction after funding the account.
- Treating a delay as a temporary glitch when it is a term.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Withdrawal limits and timing recorded.
- [ ] Transfer process recorded per asset type.
- [ ] Exit fees and closure conditions recorded.
- [ ] Jurisdiction restrictions recorded.
- [ ] Emergency release requirements recorded.
- [ ] Friction quantified alongside fees.

A useful test before funding an account is to walk through the exit: find the transfer form, note the fee, and estimate the weeks. If the process cannot be described without calling support, that is the friction the record should capture, and it is a reason to keep the balance where the exit is known.
