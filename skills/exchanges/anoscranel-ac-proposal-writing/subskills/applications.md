# Applications

## Standard

- **The selector judges a person, not an offer.** A grader reads one essay against hundreds, so the essay shows the applicant, not a restated CV (<https://ainativestudent.com/blog/how-to-use-ai-college-essay>).
- **Fraud has a named line.** The Common App policy treats passing off an AI platform's substantive output as your own as application fraud. It is punishable by revocation or expulsion.
- **The trusted-adult test.** Would it be ethical if a trusted adult did this task? A teacher fixing a comma is fine; a teacher writing the paragraph is fraud.
- **A 17-year-old may sound 17.** Unbalanced sentences beat over-polished correctness; the read-aloud test decides.
- **Detectors are evidence-poor.** About 40% of US colleges use them, but Turnitin flags roughly 4% of human sentences (⚠️ vendor-domain sources). Guilt by proxy is unavailable.
- **Disclose as the venue asks.** Caltech asks directly and says disclosure will not hurt the decision. About 68% of tracked schools have no mechanism (⚠️ vendor-domain sources).

## Decision rules

1. Freewrite first: never open the assistant before the applicant writes a word.
2. Grammar checks, question brainstorming and process research are allowed; outlining, drafting, copied output and, at Caltech, AI translation are not.
3. One specific memory beats five achievements.
4. Trade the universal lesson ("I learned perseverance") for one quirky specific one.
5. Prefer sensory detail over vocabulary: the ozone and the centrifuge hum, not "the lab".
6. Subvert the challenge → eureka → success arc; small and ambiguous wins read as true.
7. Where a venue names a disclosure, the applicant writes it and signs it.

## Procedure

1. Run a memory interview: 20 questions across origin, family, work, body, turning points; record verbatim answers.
2. Pick one memory. Write the scene: who was there, what was said, what it cost.
3. Bridge the scene to the programme after the scene, not before it.
4. Draft in the applicant's own voice; uneven sentences stay.
5. Audit for authenticity: a skeptical reader flags the three most clichéd sentences, and the applicant rewrites them.
6. Verify every fact-checkable claim against a record.
7. Read it aloud; cut what sounds like someone else. **The applicant signs and submits.**

## Anti-patterns

- Thesaurus overload standing in for evidence.
- Uniform paragraph lengths and rhythmic sameness.
- Tell-don't-show flatness: emotion asserted, never staged.
- An ending about changing the world.
- Submitting a draft the applicant did not write.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Freewriting preceded any tool use.
- [ ] One scene, with names, objects and dialogue.
- [ ] Clichés found and rewritten by the applicant.
- [ ] Every fact-checkable claim verified.
- [ ] Read-aloud test passed in the applicant's voice.
- [ ] Disclosure present where the venue requires it.
- [ ] **Human-gated:** signed and submitted by the applicant.
