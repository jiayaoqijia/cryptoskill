# Manager and vehicle diligence

## Overview

An allocation to a manager is a decision about a counterparty as much as about a strategy. The strategy may be sound and the manager may still be the reason the money is not returned: because the fees consumed the return, because the key person left, because the vehicle's gates prevented an exit, or because the terms permitted something the allocator did not read.

This skill covers that counterparty assessment. It deals with what a due diligence questionnaire can establish and what it cannot, how to test operations rather than describe them, how to read a fee stack and the offsets that reduce it, how side letters create different investors in the same fund, and how key-person, governance, and liquidity terms allocate the risk between the manager and the allocator.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## When to use

- When evaluating a manager before an allocation.
- When comparing the terms of two vehicles offering similar strategies.
- When a side letter, fee offset, or key-person clause needs reading.
- When an existing relationship's terms are being reviewed or renegotiated.

## When NOT to use

- For fund and ETF vehicle comparison including tracking (use ac-fund-selection).
- For fund-level financing and continuation vehicles (use ac-portfolio-financing).
- For the asset allocation decision itself (use ac-alternatives-and-real-assets).

## Status and honest limits

Diligence establishes what a manager has disclosed, not what will happen. Terms differ by jurisdiction and by vehicle, and a clause that is standard in one structure may be unusual in another. The evidence available to an outside allocator is limited to documents and meetings, and both are produced by the manager. Nothing here recommends a manager, and a decision taken on a questionnaire alone is a decision taken on a document the manager wrote about itself.
