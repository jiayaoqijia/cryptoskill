# Index methodology

## Standard

An index is a rule set, and the rule set determines what the fund owns. Capitalisation weighting concentrates in the largest names; equal weighting diversifies but adds turnover; factor and thematic indices select on characteristics that can be backtested into existence. The methodology document is the primary source, and reading it is the difference between owning an exposure and owning a label.

Two features deserve particular attention. Rebalancing rules determine turnover, and turnover determines cost and, in taxable accounts, tax. Concentration limits — or their absence — determine whether a fund that looks diversified has become a bet on a handful of names after a long bull run.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Read the index methodology and record its weighting scheme, selection rules, and rebalancing frequency.
- Record the current top holdings as a percentage, not just a count.
- Note concentration limits and whether they bind.
- Record turnover and the estimated cost it implies.
- For factor or thematic indices, record the selection criteria and the risk of overlap with existing holdings.
- Where the methodology has changed, record the date and the effect.

## Procedure

1. Obtain the methodology document and record its key rules.
2. Pull current holdings and compute concentration.
3. Note the rebalancing schedule and any buffer rules that reduce turnover.
4. Estimate turnover from history if disclosed.
5. Check overlap with other holdings in the portfolio.
6. Record the findings with the methodology's date.

## Anti-patterns

- Buying an index fund without reading the methodology.
- Assuming a name describes the holdings.
- Ignoring concentration drift.
- Treating a backtested factor as evidence of future returns.
- Overlooking overlap between two thematic funds.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Methodology rules recorded.
- [ ] Holdings concentration computed.
- [ ] Rebalancing schedule and buffers noted.
- [ ] Turnover estimated.
- [ ] Overlap with existing holdings checked.
- [ ] Findings dated.

The methodology's buffer rules matter more than its headline weighting for turnover: a fund that only adds or removes names at the boundary of a size band trades far less than one that ranks purely by size. Reading those rules turns an estimate of cost into a fact about the rule set.

Where a fund follows an index an investor cannot inspect — a bespoke or licensed one — that opacity is itself a finding, and the record should say so rather than treating the fund's description as the methodology.
