# Continuation conflicts

## Standard

In a continuation transaction the sponsor is on both sides: it manages the selling fund and controls the buying one, and it may also roll its own interest into the new vehicle. The conflict is structural rather than personal, and the question is not whether it exists but whether the process around it produced a price a willing buyer would have paid.

The process elements that matter are the independence of the valuation, the identity of the party that negotiated on behalf of the selling fund, the advisory committee's access to its own advisers, and the disclosure of the sponsor's rollover. Each of these can be present formally and absent substantively, which is why reading the documents rather than the summary is the only way to judge.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the valuation provider and who engaged it.
- Record whether the selling fund had independent advisers and who paid them.
- Record the advisory committee's role and its access to information.
- Record the sponsor's rollover and its size relative to the new vehicle.
- Record the alternative options considered, including a sale to a third party.
- Record what was disclosed to investors and when.

## Procedure

1. Record the valuation and its engagement.
2. Record independent advice on the sell side.
3. Record the committee's role and access.
4. Record the sponsor's rollover.
5. Record alternatives considered.
6. Write the conflict assessment.

## Anti-patterns

- Accepting a fairness opinion as proof of a fair price.
- Ignoring who commissioned and paid the valuer.
- Assuming committee approval implies independent scrutiny.
- Overlooking the sponsor's own rollover as a signal.
- Missing the alternatives that were not explored.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Valuation and engagement recorded.
- [ ] Sell-side advice recorded.
- [ ] Committee role recorded.
- [ ] Sponsor rollover recorded.
- [ ] Alternatives recorded.
- [ ] Assessment written.

The signal worth recording is the sponsor's own rollover: a sponsor that commits a substantial share of its own capital into the new vehicle has aligned its economics with the asset's continued performance, while one that rolls a nominal amount and takes its carried interest from the transaction has monetised the position. The size of that commitment, relative to the sponsor's stake in the old fund, is the most legible evidence available.
