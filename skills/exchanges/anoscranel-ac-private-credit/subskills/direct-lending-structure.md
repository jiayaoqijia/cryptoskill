# Direct lending structure

## Standard

A direct loan is originated by one lender or a small group and held, which changes the economics and the incentives. The originator negotiates the terms with the borrower and the sponsor, sets the covenants, and prices the spread; because it intends to hold, it bears the credit outcome, which aligns its interest with the lender's in a way that an originate-to-distribute model does not.

The agreement is where the protection lives. It defines the borrower, the guarantors, the collateral, the negative pledge, the permitted baskets for debt and liens, and the reporting obligations. Those clauses decide what a lender can do when the borrower deteriorates, and they are the part of the document that a summary never reproduces.

Primary sources: <https://www.federalreserve.gov/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the borrower, the guarantor group, and the collateral.
- Record the facility size, the maturity, the spread, and the reference rate.
- Record the negative pledge and the permitted lien baskets.
- Record the reporting obligations and their frequency.
- Record the lender group and whether transfer requires consent.
- Record the origination channel and whether the originator holds the position.

## Procedure

1. Identify the borrower entity and the guarantor structure.
2. Record the facility terms and pricing.
3. Record the negative pledge and baskets.
4. Record the reporting package and deadlines.
5. Record the lender group and transfer restrictions.
6. Write the structure summary.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading a marketing summary instead of the credit agreement.
- Ignoring the guarantor structure when assessing collateral.
- Missing the permitted baskets, which allow the protection to be eroded.
- Assuming the originator holds the loan.
- Overlooking consent requirements that make an exit impossible.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Borrower and guarantors recorded.
- [ ] Facility terms recorded.
- [ ] Negative pledge and baskets recorded.
- [ ] Reporting obligations recorded.
- [ ] Lender group and consents recorded.
- [ ] Structure summary written.

The reporting package deserves attention beyond its frequency: a monthly covenant certificate carries management's arithmetic, and a lender who reads it against the audited statements each year is the one who notices when the definitions have drifted. That reconciliation, not the certificate alone, is what provides early warning in a market without prices.
