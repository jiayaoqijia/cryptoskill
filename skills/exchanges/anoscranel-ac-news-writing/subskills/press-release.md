# Press release

## Standard

- **The inverted pyramid is the operating shape.** Essential facts upfront, because editors cut from the bottom up (<https://newsx.io/2026/06/12/best-ai-press-release-writing-tools-for-startups>). AP style, a dateline, and a close with the boilerplate and contact details (PRSA guidance, https://jobs.prsa.org/career-resources/finding-talent-9/how-to-write-a-press-release-that-actually-gets-published-388 ⚠️ careers article, not a PRSA standard).
- **Short is measured, not stylistic.** 68% of journalists prefer releases under 400 words and decide in 5–10 seconds. 70% stop at 200 words (Cision 2025 State of the Media Report, 3,000+ journalists, 19 markets).
- **The assistant structures supplied facts; it never supplies them.** Never let a model invent an executive quote — write or approve the quote verbatim first.
- **The release is a regulated disclosure surface.** When an issuer discloses material nonpublic information to covered persons, public disclosure must be simultaneous for an intentional disclosure. For an unintentional one it must be prompt (17 CFR 243.100(a), 243.101(d)–(e), https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-243). "Promptly" means no later than 24 hours or the start of the next day's NYSE trading. A widely disseminated release counts as broad, non-exclusionary distribution.
- **Non-GAAP figures carry their own rule.** A public non-GAAP measure must not contain an untrue statement or omit a material fact (17 CFR 244.100, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-17/chapter-II/part-244/section-244.100). The comparable GAAP measure plus a reconciliation is required.
- **Fabricated praise is banned.** Fake or false consumer reviews, bought reviews, insider reviews and company-controlled review sites are all banned (16 CFR 465, effective 21 October 2024, https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-465).

## Decision rules

1. One announcement per release. A release carrying three stories carries none.
2. The headline states the news, not the brand.
3. Every number, title and date is verified against a source record before drafting.
4. Executive quotes are approved verbatim by the speaker.
5. Earnings releases on a completed period trigger filing duties; route those to a human with counsel.
6. A listed issuer treats the release as a market disclosure, not marketing copy.

## Procedure

1. Assemble the facts document: announcement type, date, names, titles, numbers, approved quote.
2. Write the headline as the news sentence.
3. Write the lede: what happened, when, who, and why it matters — in one sentence.
4. Add the body: context, then the quote, then supporting detail in descending order.
5. Add the boilerplate and the named media contact with a phone number and email.
6. Verify every figure, title and date against the facts document; log the check.
7. Run the legal pass: material information, non-GAAP measures, filing triggers, endorsement claims. **The human issues the release.**

## Anti-patterns

- A release that opens with company history.
- An invented executive quote or a quote no speaker approved.
- A non-GAAP figure with no comparable measure and no reconciliation.
- A fabricated reviewer quoted as proof.

## Done checklist

- [ ] One announcement; headline states the news.
- [ ] Facts document verified figure by figure.
- [ ] Quote approved verbatim by its speaker.
- [ ] Under 400 words; the news survives the 200-word cut.
- [ ] Boilerplate and named contact present.
- [ ] Legal pass complete: materiality, non-GAAP, filing triggers.
- [ ] **Human-gated:** issued by a named person.
