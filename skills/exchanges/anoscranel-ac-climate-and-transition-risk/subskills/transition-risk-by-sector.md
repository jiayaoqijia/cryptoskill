# Transition risk by sector

## Standard

Transition risk is not uniform: it lands where the cost of a carbon price is large relative to the unit economics, where substitution is technically possible, and where the asset lives long enough to be exposed to a policy that has not yet been written. A cement plant, an airline, and a software company face the same carbon price and experience it very differently, because their ability to pass it through and their alternatives differ.

The horizon matters as much as the sector. A company that expects to recover its capital in three years is exposed to three years of policy; one whose assets last forty years is exposed to forty, and its terminal value depends on a policy environment nobody can forecast. The useful framing is the asset life against the policy horizon, which converts a sector label into an estimate of exposure.

Primary sources: <https://www.bis.org/> and <https://www.eia.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the sector's emissions intensity per unit of output.
- Record the ability to pass cost through, and the elasticity of demand.
- Record the technical substitution options and their cost.
- Record the asset life against the policy horizon.
- Record the regulatory exposure in the jurisdictions where assets sit.
- Record the capital already committed to assets with a long life.

## Procedure

1. Record the emissions intensity and its main source.
2. Record pass-through ability and demand elasticity.
3. Record substitution options and cost.
4. Compare asset life with policy horizon.
5. Record jurisdictional exposure.
6. Write the transition assessment with the horizon stated.

## Anti-patterns

- Applying a sector-level carbon cost without the intensity.
- Assuming pass-through is possible in a competitive market.
- Ignoring asset life in the exposure estimate.
- Treating a global policy as the relevant one for a local asset.
- Using a sector score as a substitute for the arithmetic.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Intensity recorded.
- [ ] Pass-through recorded.
- [ ] Substitution recorded.
- [ ] Asset life versus horizon recorded.
- [ ] Jurisdictions recorded.
- [ ] Assessment written with horizon.

The comparison that avoids sector labels is to ask how many dollars of capital are committed per dollar of annual margin: an asset-intensive business with a thin margin has little room for a carbon cost, while a service business with high margins absorbs the same price with no change in economics. That ratio, not the industry classification, is what determines whether the transition is an inconvenience or an existential question.
