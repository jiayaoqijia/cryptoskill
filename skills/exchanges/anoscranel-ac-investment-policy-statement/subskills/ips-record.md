# IPS record

## Standard

The policy statement is a document with an owner, a date, and a version. It contains the goals and their priorities, the binding risk constraint, the constraints register, the allocation table with ranges, the rebalancing rule, and the monitoring and review cadence. Its purpose is to be read at the moment of temptation and to settle the question without a new debate.

Versioning is what makes it a policy rather than a preference. Each amendment records what changed, why, and on whose authority; the prior version is retained. A policy that is edited silently cannot be audited, and an unauditable policy cannot tell you whether the portfolio is behaving as intended.

Primary sources: <https://www.cfainstitute.org/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- The statement names its owner and the date it takes effect.
- Every section exists or is explicitly marked as not applicable, with a reason.
- Amendments are versioned, dated, and retained; the previous version is never deleted.
- The binding risk constraint is stated in one sentence.
- The sign-off records who accepted the policy and when.
- The statement is stored where it will be found at a review, not where it was written.

## Procedure

1. Assemble the sections from the six leaves.
2. State the owner, effective date, and version.
3. Mark any non-applicable section with its reason.
4. Write the binding-constraint sentence.
5. Sign and date.
6. File the statement and record its location in the monitoring log.

## Anti-patterns

- Drafting the statement after the portfolio is built, to match it.
- Editing the file in place with no version record.
- Omitting a section silently.
- Leaving ownership unstated.
- Filing it where it will not be found at review time.

## Done checklist

- [ ] All sections assembled or marked non-applicable with reason.
- [ ] Owner, effective date, and version stated.
- [ ] Binding constraint stated in one sentence.
- [ ] Sign-off and date recorded.
- [ ] Statement filed and location logged.
- [ ] Amendment process stated.

The statement's authority comes from being read before the decision, not after. Keeping it where a review can find it — and referring to it in the monitoring log — is what turns a document into a constraint on behaviour. A policy nobody opens is a policy nobody follows, and the first symptom is a portfolio that drifts without a decision being recorded anywhere.
