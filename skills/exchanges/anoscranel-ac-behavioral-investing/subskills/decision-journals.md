# Decision journals

## Standard

A decision journal is written at the moment of decision, before the outcome exists, and it records what was decided, why, what was expected, what would show it wrong, and what was felt. Its function is to make hindsight honest: without a contemporaneous record, every outcome becomes evidence for whatever the decision-maker now believes they thought.

The evidence base for this is not anecdotal. Research on investor behaviour has repeatedly found that the disposition effect — selling winners and holding losers — is visible in account data, and that overconfidence shows up as excessive trading with worse returns. A journal is the cheapest instrument that lets an individual see those patterns in their own record.

Primary sources: <https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/0022-1082.00072> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The journal entry is written before the order, not after the fill.
- Each entry states: decision, reasoning, expected outcome with a horizon, falsifier, and emotional state.
- Confidence is recorded as a number so calibration can be measured later.
- Entries are never edited after the outcome; subsequent thoughts are appended as dated notes.
- Every entry names the review date when it will be scored.
- Decisions taken under time pressure are flagged as such.

## Procedure

1. Open the entry with the date, the instrument, and the decision.
2. Write the reasoning in full sentences, including the case against.
3. State the expected outcome and its horizon.
4. State the falsifier: what evidence would show the decision wrong.
5. Record the emotional state and any time pressure.
6. Score the entry at the review date: outcome versus expectation, process versus luck.

## Anti-patterns

- Writing entries after the outcome to look prescient.
- Recording the decision without the reasoning.
- Omitting the emotional state because it feels irrelevant.
- Never scoring the entries, which makes the journal decoration.
- Editing an entry when the thesis fails.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Entry written before the order.
- [ ] Decision, reasoning, expectation, and horizon recorded.
- [ ] Falsifier stated.
- [ ] Confidence as a number.
- [ ] Emotional state and time pressure noted.
- [ ] Review date set and scoring completed.

A journal's value appears after about a year, when the first entries are scored and the pattern is visible: the same rule suspended, the same asset chased, the same moment of panic. Until then it is an expense; after then it is the most useful document the investor owns.
