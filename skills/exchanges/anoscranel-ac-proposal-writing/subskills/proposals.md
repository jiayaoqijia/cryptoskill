# Proposals & RFPs

## Standard

- **The buyer's criteria are the structure.** A losing submission opens with background and buries the recommendation. The winning one states the answer first, groups arguments without overlap, and puts evidence beneath (Minto's pyramid).
- **Action titles.** Each heading states the point the section proves; the headings alone must reconstruct the case. Win themes are named claims, repeated in headings, each tied to a criterion.
- **The summary carries everything** because unassigned evaluators score without reading the body.
- **Open with the client's problem, not your credentials.** Demonstrate understanding before offering. Specify deliverables, timeline and success criteria. Pre-empt objections. Close with a clear call to action (<https://datafield.dev/working-with-ai-tools-effectively/part-04/chapter-27/> ⚠️ practitioner).
- **The claims carry legal duties.** An endorsement must reflect the endorser's honest opinion (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255). A material connection must be disclosed "clearly and conspicuously" — 16 CFR 255, revised, effective 26 July 2023. Fake or AI-generated reviews are banned outright — 16 CFR 465, effective 21 October 2024 (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-465). Content that promotes benefits yet is not readily identifiable as advertising is deceptive under FTC Act §5, even where its claims are true (https://www.ftc.gov/tips-advice/business-center/guidance/native-advertising-guide-businesses).
- **Case studies follow the typical-results rule.** Where an endorsement features exceptional results without substantiated typical results, the generally expected results must be disclosed (16 CFR 255.2; FTC staff guidance, https://www.ftc.gov/tips-advice/business-center/guidance/ftcs-endorsement-guides-what-people-are-asking).

## Decision rules

1. Build the compliance matrix first: every requirement, its section, its owner. An unanswered requirement is a scoring failure.
2. Answer the buyer's criteria in the buyer's order and wording.
3. Every claim needs a substantiation basis before the proposal ships; performance claims need competent evidence, not a customer quote.
4. Testimonials and case studies: honest opinion, disclosed connection, typical results alongside the exceptional one.
5. Price framing presents the number plainly. ⚠️ Practitioner rule; no measured dosing.
6. If a requirement cannot be met, say so with the workaround. Silence reads as an evasion the buyer will find.

## Procedure

1. Extract every requirement into a matrix. Map each to a section and an owner.
2. Write the one-sentence answer: why this buyer chooses this offer.
3. Draft headings as full claims, one per criterion. The heading list alone must carry the bid.
4. Open with the client's problem in their words, then demonstrate understanding.
5. Write each section: topic sentence, claim, evidence, objection pre-empted.
6. Add the compliance matrix as the final section, mapped line by line.
7. Check every claim and testimonial against its substantiation record.
8. Hand the human the draft plus the claim record. **The human submits.**

## Anti-patterns

- Opening with company history while the buyer's problem goes unstated.
- Topic-label headings ("Our Approach") that carry no claim.
- A case study with one 300% result and no typical-results context.
- Submitting with a matrix row nobody owns.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Compliance matrix complete, every row owned and mapped.
- [ ] One-sentence answer placed first.
- [ ] Headings are claims; the list alone carries the bid.
- [ ] Client's problem opens the document.
- [ ] Every claim substantiated; testimonials checked for connection and typicality.
- [ ] **Human-gated:** submitted by a named person.
