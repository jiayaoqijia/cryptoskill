# Steelman Discipline

## Standard

- **Galef, _The Scout Mindset_ (2021)**: the scout reports the terrain as it is; the soldier defends. Steelmanning is scouting applied to a view you dislike. ⚠️ cited without URL.
- **Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Fallacies"**: the straw man substitutes a distorted version of a position; the defect is the substitution. <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/fallacies/>
- **Shackel, "The Vacuity of Postmodernist Methodology"** (2005): named the motte-and-bailey move — an ambitious claim is defended by retreating to a modest one, then carrying the modest victory back. ⚠️ cited without URL.
- **Socratic method** (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Socrates"): the elenchus tests a claim at its strength; the aim is truth, not victory. <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/socrates/>

## Decision rules

1. If you cannot restate the position to its holder's satisfaction, you are not ready to attack it.
2. If the version you are attacking is weaker than the version stated, that is a straw man; return to the stated version.
3. If a claim retreats to a defensible core when pressed, name the retreat: "you first argued X; now Y — which is your position?"
4. If the critique targets the person — motive, competence, tribe — drop it and argue the proposition.
5. If you apply a standard to their claim that you would not accept for your own, stop and equalise.
6. If you are winning but not learning, you are soldiering; switch to asking what would change your own mind.
7. If the strongest version survives your attack, say so plainly; that is a finding, not a defeat.

## Procedure

1. Ask for the strongest version: "state it the way you would want it attacked."
2. Paraphrase it back; ask "is that your view?" until confirmed.
3. Steelman it further: add the best evidence and the most charitable reading.
4. Attack that version, hardest mechanism first. Write the attack without softening it.
5. Check the attack lands on the claim, not the person.
6. If the claim retreats when pressed, run the motte check and hold both versions side by side.
7. Record the outcome: which version survived, which arguments moved you, what you now believe and at what confidence.
8. State your own prior before the exchange, so any update is visible.

## Anti-patterns

- **Politeness theater.** The steelman is announced, then a strawman is attacked. Fix: paraphrase-and-confirm before attacking.
- **Unchallenged motte retreat.** The safe claim wins today, the brave one returns tomorrow. Fix: name both versions.
- **Tone policing as dismissal.** Brisk delivery used to discard content. Fix: extract the argument and answer it.
- **"You are just saying X".** Reframing words into something easier to beat. Fix: quote the actual words.
- **One-way steelman.** Charity for your own side only. Fix: apply the same standard in both directions.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Strongest version obtained and confirmed by its holder.
- [ ] Paraphrase confirmed before any attack.
- [ ] Attack aimed at the strongest version, mechanism first.
- [ ] Person and proposition separated.
- [ ] Retreats named; both versions on the table.
- [ ] Outcome recorded: what moved, what survived.
