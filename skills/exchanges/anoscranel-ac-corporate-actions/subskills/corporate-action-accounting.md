# Corporate action accounting

## Standard

Every corporate action creates an accounting question: what basis the new position carries, what counts as proceeds, and whether the event is income, a return of capital, or a reorganisation. The answers are jurisdiction-specific, and the record's job is to preserve the facts each answer depends on rather than to decide the answer.

The facts are consistent across jurisdictions: the original basis and purchase date, the terms of the action, the ratio or consideration, the fair value at the relevant date, and the amounts received. A record containing those can be answered by a professional; one missing them requires reconstruction, and reconstruction understates basis as often as it overstates it.

Primary sources: <https://www.irs.gov/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Original basis and purchase date are preserved for every position, however it was created.
- The terms and ratios are recorded from the issuer's document, not from memory.
- Fair value at the relevant date is recorded where an allocation depends on it.
- Amounts received — cash, shares, rights — are recorded separately by type.
- Treatment questions are written as questions for a professional, not answered in the record.
- Where the treatment is unsettled, the record says so and dates the uncertainty.

## Procedure

1. Carry the original basis and date into the record.
2. Record the terms and ratios from the source document.
3. Record the fair values used in any allocation.
4. Record each amount received by type.
5. Write the open treatment questions.
6. File the record with the tax pack inputs.

## Anti-patterns

- Losing the original basis after a reorganisation.
- Using a remembered ratio instead of the document.
- Deciding the treatment in the record.
- Mixing cash and share amounts in one line.
- Treating an unsettled area as settled.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Original basis and date preserved.
- [ ] Terms and ratios recorded from source.
- [ ] Fair values recorded where used.
- [ ] Amounts received recorded by type.
- [ ] Open questions written.
- [ ] Record filed with the tax inputs.

The value of carrying the original basis forward is that every later question can be answered from it: the gain on a sale, the allocation in a spin-off, the treatment of a return of capital. Losing it after a reorganisation is the single most common basis error, and it is unrecoverable without the original broker records.
