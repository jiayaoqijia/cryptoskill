# Settlement accounting

## Standard

A buy-out settles the defined-benefit obligation, and settlement is an accounting event: the obligation is removed from the balance sheet, the assets are derecognised, and the difference between them is recognised. Whether that difference is a gain or a loss depends on the funding position at the transaction date relative to the assumptions the obligation was carried at, not relative to the price the insurer quoted.

The reported consequence is therefore not a measure of whether the transaction was well priced. A settlement loss can appear when the insurer's price is above the book liability even though the transaction removed risk the sponsor was being paid nothing to hold, and a settlement gain can appear when the book liability was carried on conservative assumptions. Reading the settlement line requires knowing which basis produced it.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.fasb.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the obligation and the assets derecognised, on the accounting basis used.
- Record the settlement gain or loss and its calculation basis.
- Record the assumptions the obligation was carried at, particularly the discount rate.
- Record the insurer quote against that book liability, not against a market basis.
- Record any remaining liability after a partial transfer.
- Record the effect on future service cost and on the sponsor's cash contributions.

## Procedure

1. Read the pension note for the obligation and the asset values.
2. Record the accounting basis and discount rate.
3. Record the settlement gain or loss.
4. Compare the insurer quote with the book liability.
5. Record the residual liability after a partial transfer.
6. Write the analysis with the reported and economic effects distinguished.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading the settlement gain or loss as a verdict on the transaction.
- Comparing the insurer quote with a market-consistent liability rather than the book figure.
- Ignoring the assumptions behind the book liability.
- Overlooking the residual liability after a partial transfer.
- Treating the accounting effect as a cash effect.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Obligation and assets recorded.
- [ ] Settlement gain or loss recorded.
- [ ] Discount rate recorded.
- [ ] Quote compared with book liability.
- [ ] Residual liability recorded.
- [ ] Cash and accounting effects distinguished.

The disclosure should also be read for what it does not say: a settlement line without the discount rate and the assumptions behind the obligation is a number without a meaning, and the same transaction can produce a gain or a loss depending on the basis, which is why the note rather than the headline is the source.
