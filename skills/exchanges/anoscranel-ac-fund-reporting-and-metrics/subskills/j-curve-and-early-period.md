# J-curve and early period

## Standard

A private fund typically reports negative returns in its first years. Fees are charged from the first close, investments are marked at or near cost, and the gains arrive later when positions are sold. That shape is the J-curve, and it is structural rather than a sign of poor management, which is why an early IRR from a fund in its investment period carries almost no information.

The distinction that matters is between a J-curve and a flat or falling curve. A fund whose value is still below cost after most of its capital has been deployed and whose marks have not risen is not simply early; the vintage is telling you something. The record should compare the fund's shape with the expected shape for its strategy and vintage, rather than treating any negative number as normal.

Primary sources: <https://www.cfainstitute.org/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the fund's age and the proportion of capital deployed.
- Record the reported return at each reporting date, not only the latest.
- Compare the shape with the typical shape for the strategy and vintage.
- Record the fee load in the early years and its effect on the curve.
- Record whether marks are at cost, marked up, or written down.
- Distinguish an early-stage negative from a late-stage negative.

## Procedure

1. Record the fund's age and deployment percentage.
2. Collect the reported returns across reporting dates.
3. Plot or tabulate the shape.
4. Compare with a comparable vintage's typical shape.
5. Record the fee drag and the mark basis.
6. Write the curve assessment with its stage.

## Anti-patterns

- Judging a first-year fund on its IRR.
- Treating all negative returns as the J-curve.
- Ignoring the fee drag in the early years.
- Assuming marks at cost represent value.
- Comparing shapes across strategies with different durations.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Age and deployment recorded.
- [ ] Return history recorded.
- [ ] Shape compared with vintage norm.
- [ ] Fee drag recorded.
- [ ] Mark basis recorded.
- [ ] Stage assessment written.

The warning sign is not the depth of the dip but the failure to climb: a fund whose marks remain flat through the years when its companies should be maturing is telling you that the marks are stale rather than early, and the difference between a curve that is behind schedule and one that is not moving at all is the difference between patience and a problem.
