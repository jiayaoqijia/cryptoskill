# Evidence Dial

## Standard

- **Popper on falsification** (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, "Karl Popper"): the line runs between claims that rule something out and claims that rule nothing out. <https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/popper/>
- **Tetlock & Gardner, _Superforecasting_ (2015)**: frequent small updates beat confident verbal verdicts; probability, not certainty, tracks reality. ⚠️ cited without URL.
- **Duke, _Thinking in Bets_ (2018)**: separate decision quality from outcome quality; good decisions can lose. ⚠️ cited without URL.
- **Galef, _The Scout Mindset_ (2021)**: the test of a belief is what you expect to see if you are wrong. ⚠️ cited without URL.

## Decision rules

1. If the author cannot answer "what would change your mind?", the claim is unfalsifiable; treat it as a belief, label it, and test the decision without it.
2. If evidence was collected only on one side, the search is half done; look for the disconfirming case before the confirming one.
3. If the decision is one-way or expensive, more research earns its cost; if it is two-way and cheap, decide now and let the result teach.
4. If confidence arrives as an adjective, convert it to a number and a range, then ask what last moved it.
5. If the newest evidence is one anecdote, check the reference class before updating much.
6. If research continues while the choice stops changing, stop: research has become avoidance.
7. If a past decision is judged by its outcome alone, that is resulting; judge what was knowable at the time.

## Procedure

1. List the claims the decision rests on. Mark each as evidence-needed or evidence-exists.
2. For each evidence-needed claim, write the falsification test: the observation that, if seen, kills it. No candidate observation means unfalsifiable.
3. Set the gate: compare the cost of more research with the cost of being wrong. One-way door pushes toward research; two-way door pushes toward deciding.
4. Seek disconfirmation on purpose: ask who would have found the case against.
5. Grade the evidence: direct measurement first, then repeated observation, then single case, then analogy, then assertion.
6. Set confidence as a percentage with a range; write the two facts that would move it most.
7. If the gate says decide, decide now and record the confidence for later scoring.

## Anti-patterns

- **Research as avoidance.** Another week of reading postpones a choice that will not change. Fix: name the threshold in advance; stop at it.
- **Confirmation shopping.** Gathering only what supports the plan. Fix: run a deliberate search for the opposite case.
- **Unfalsifiable claims in evidence clothing.** "The market wants this" cannot be wrong, so it cannot guide. Fix: demand the observation that would kill it.
- **Adjective confidence.** "Pretty sure" carries no information. Fix: number, range, reason.
- **Resulting.** Judging the call by how it happened to land. Fix: score the decision on what was knowable then.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Claims listed and marked evidence-needed or evidence-exists.
- [ ] Falsification test written for each major claim.
- [ ] Research gate applied with a stated reason.
- [ ] Disconfirming search run before concluding.
- [ ] Confidence recorded as number, range, and reason.
