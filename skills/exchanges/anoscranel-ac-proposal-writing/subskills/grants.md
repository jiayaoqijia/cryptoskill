# Grants

## Standard

- **The Aims page is the decision.** Reviewers form a first-pass impression on the Aims page in about 60–120 seconds, then read the rest to confirm (<https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_process_theory> ⚠️ consulting sources). Non-assigned panelists skim the Aims page and two lines of the Approach. That page carries significance, capability and feasibility before any detail.
- **Plainness is the strongest single-document writing effect.** Needless complexity depresses judgements of the author's intelligence and of acceptance, robust across domains and independent of the essay's quality (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/acp.1178).
- **Risk beats strength in scoring.** As perceived risk rises, strengths predict less; one serious flaw outweighs several strengths. Pre-empt the killer objection in the body, not the appendix (same GA-01 file §2, §8).
- **Reliability bounds what writing can do.** Two reviewers of the same proposal agree at r about 0.26. Near the payline roughly a third of outcomes flip with score noise. Aim at the range: leaving the triaged half beats a point of impact score (<https://www.niaid.nih.gov/research/understand-paylines-percentiles>).
- **The originality rule is the strictest AI constraint.** NIH NOT-OD-25-132: applications "substantially developed by AI, or contain[ing] sections substantially developed by AI" are not original ideas of applicants (https://grants.nih.gov/grants/guide/notice-files/NOT-OD-25-132.html). They "will not be considered", and disclosure does not cure it. NSF asks proposers to "indicate the extent and manner of use" (https://nsf.gov/policies/ai/merit-review).

## Decision rules

1. Answer the funder's criteria in the funder's order and wording; where weights are unstated, the reviewer's weighting becomes the rubric.
2. Frame significance against the funder's gap wording; never substitute "innovative" for evidence.
3. Pre-empt the predictable methodological attack before it is raised; anchor on one solid, well-labelled result, not the biggest number.
5. Criterion-responsive, not gaming. The test is defensibility under calibration talk. If a panelist could say "your comments are meaner than your score" and be right, the claim comes off.
6. **Human-gated:** every attestation and submission is made by a named person.
7. No AI-written section may be presented as original at an originality-testing agency; log and disclose every AI touch.

## Procedure

1. Extract criteria, weights and page limits into a section map before drafting.
2. Draft the Aims page first: significance, capability, feasibility, one paragraph each.
3. Write need around the funder's significance wording, with the gap named.
4. Write method as the criterion that carries the overall score: approach, alternatives, failure mode and contingency.
5. Write the budget justification so every line maps to a method step.
6. Run the plainness pass (short sentences, one claim per paragraph, jargon defined once), then verify every citation, number and claim against its source. Log the check.
7. Hand the human the draft, the criteria map and the verification record. **The human submits.**

## Anti-patterns

- An aims page opening with background while significance goes unstated.
- "Innovative" standing in for evidence.
- Risks buried optimistically or absent.
- AI-drafted sections presented as original.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Criteria map complete; sections in the funder's order.
- [ ] Aims page carries significance, capability, feasibility in order.
- [ ] Killer objection pre-empted in the body.
- [ ] Budget lines map to method steps.
- [ ] Plainness pass run; every claim verified and logged.
- [ ] **Human-gated** submission and attestation by a named person.
