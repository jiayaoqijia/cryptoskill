# Behavioural investing

## Overview

The portfolio is the easy part; the investor is the hard part. Most long-horizon outcomes are damaged by decisions made under emotional load — selling at the bottom, chasing at the top, adding complexity after a loss — rather than by any mistake in security selection. This skill manages the decision environment so that good policy survives bad moods.

It works through structure rather than willpower. Willpower is a resource that depletes exactly when it is needed; a written rule, a cooling-off period, and a recorded decision do not deplete. Every leaf here converts a good intention into an arrangement that holds when the intention is absent.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.jstor.org/stable/1914185>.

## When to use

- When a decision is being made under time pressure or emotional load.
- When designing the rules that govern checking frequency and information intake.
- After a drawdown, before any change to the portfolio.
- When reviewing past decisions to learn from them.

## When NOT to use

- For the portfolio's written policy (use ac-investment-policy-statement).
- For per-trade risk rails on a trading desk (use ac-crypto-trading-discipline).
- For company or token research (use ac-equity-research, ac-token-diligence).

## Status and honest limits

Behavioural rules reduce the frequency and cost of bad decisions; they do not eliminate them. A journal can be written dishonestly, a rule can be suspended by the person it constrains, and knowing about a bias does not immunise against it — the evidence on debiasing is thin and the evidence on structure is better. Build the structure, record the decisions, and expect to need the script.
