# Line edit

## Standard

⚠️ The primary standard is paywalled; these points come from secondary sources and the public summary.

- **The line pass, defined by trade.** "Detailed editing of a manuscript—line by line … but not necessarily correcting all fine points of grammar, punctuation, or style, which is the task of copyediting" (CMOS Q&A: https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/ManuscriptPreparation/faq0218.html). A line edit may change emphasis and rhythm; a copyedit may not.
- **CMOS itself concedes the boundary is crossed in practice.** "A copyedit will typically involve some line editing also—for example, to fix an awkward sentence—a thorough line edit is best done as a separate step at an earlier stage".
- **Countable sentence targets (GOV.UK).** Split sentences over 25 words. Paragraphs hold no more than five sentences. Use the short word: "buy" not "purchase", "help" not "assist", "about" not "approximately". Word-endings "-ion" and "-ment" lengthen sentences (https://guidance.publishing.service.gov.uk/writing-to-gov-uk-standards/writing-guidelines/clear-language/).
- **Active voice is the default with two named exceptions**: where the outcome matters more than the agent, and where the writing is user-centred.
- **Microsoft's mechanical tips.** Contractions, verb-first statements, cut "you can", avoid "there is / there are" (https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/style-guide/top-10-tips-style-voice).
- **Measured effect of style recasting.** One step along a traditional→accessible continuum gave readability +2.91% (CI 1.79–4.03) and 1.08× odds of correct understanding (Frey, *Front Psychol* 2021, https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.714321/full).

## Decision rules

1. One idea per sentence. Two ideas means two sentences.
2. The sentence start carries the subject; the sentence end carries the point. Move emphasis by moving the clause.
3. Prefer the strong verb. Cut the nominalisation ("implement a review" → "review").
4. A paragraph is one unit of thought, at most five sentences, with its point in the first sentence.
5. Break the 25-word line, unless the sentence carries a legal condition that must not be split.
6. Rhythm needs variety. A page of identical short sentences reads as hard as one long one.
7. Fixing a sentence by changing what it says is not a line edit; that work belongs to subskills/revision-structural.md.

## Procedure

1. Read the section aloud, or by text-to-speech. Ears catch what eyes pass over.
2. Mark, do not repair, on the first pass: long lines, buried points, weak verbs, throat-clearing openings.
3. Cut openers that carry no content ("It is important to note that", "As previously mentioned").
4. Rewrite marked sentences. Keep the claim's strength and its qualifiers exactly as set (the claim-evidence grammar is out of scope here).
5. Break every sentence over 25 words at its natural joint, not at a random comma.
6. Re-read once aloud. If you stumble, the sentence is still wrong.
7. Hand the section on. Mechanics and style-sheet items belong to subskills/copyedit.md.

## Anti-patterns

- Cutting a hedge to make the sentence shorter.
- Uniform sentence length for a "clean" rhythm.
- Transitions bolted on ("Furthermore", "Moreover") instead of real logical joints.
- Passive voice in a technical document where the agent matters.
- A line edit that quietly changes what the section claims.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Read aloud or by text-to-speech.
- [ ] No sentence over 25 words without a reason.
- [ ] One idea per sentence; point at start or end.
- [ ] Nominalisations replaced by verbs.
- [ ] Paragraphs within five sentences.
- [ ] Claim strength and qualifiers unchanged.
- [ ] Rhythm varies across the page.
