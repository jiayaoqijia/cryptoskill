# Restructuring process

## Standard

A sovereign restructuring follows a recognisable sequence even though no two are identical. The debtor's position deteriorates until market access closes; a programme with the official sector is negotiated, which sets macroeconomic targets and often sets aside funds for the debtor's own obligations; private creditors are then approached, usually through an exchange offer; and the process ends with new instruments, a restored access, and a period during which the debtor's compliance with the programme is monitored.

The sequence matters because each step changes the creditors' position. A programme that treats the private sector's claims as subordinate to official lending changes the recovery arithmetic; an exchange offer launched before the programme is agreed may be withdrawn; and the timing of the offer relative to the debtor's reserves determines how much leverage the creditors retain.

Primary sources: <https://www.imf.org/> and <https://www.bis.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the stage the process has reached, with dates.
- Record the official-sector involvement and the terms attached.
- Record the exchange offer's terms, including the new instruments and the haircut.
- Record the participation threshold and whether it was met.
- Record the treatment of official versus private creditors.
- Record the conditions that must be met for the process to conclude.

## Procedure

1. Establish the current stage and the preceding events.
2. Record official-sector participation and its terms.
3. Record the offer's consideration and haircut.
4. Record the participation threshold and result.
5. Record the creditor treatment differential.
6. Write the process summary with the next milestone named.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating an exchange offer as the whole process.
- Ignoring the official sector's seniority in practice.
- Reading the haircut without the new instruments' terms.
- Assuming the programme's targets will be met.
- Overlooking the conditions that could reopen the negotiation.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Stage and dates recorded.
- [ ] Official involvement recorded.
- [ ] Offer terms and haircut recorded.
- [ ] Participation threshold recorded.
- [ ] Creditor treatment recorded.
- [ ] Next milestone named.

The programme's fiscal targets are also a constraint on what the private sector can recover: a target that requires a primary surplus the debtor cannot achieve politically will be missed, and a missed target typically reopens the negotiation rather than ending it. Recording the target and its political feasibility is part of reading the process rather than an aside about politics.
