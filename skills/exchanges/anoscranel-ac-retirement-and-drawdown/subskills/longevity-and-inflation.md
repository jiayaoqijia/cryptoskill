# Longevity and inflation

## Standard

Two uncertainties dominate decumulation and neither can be resolved in advance. Longevity is a distribution, not a date: planning to an average lifespan leaves half the outcomes unplanned, and the tail is where the money runs out. Inflation compounds against a fixed nominal spending plan, and the relevant measure for a household is not the headline index but the basket it actually buys.

The response is structural rather than predictive. A plan that survives to a long horizon with a stress-tested inflation assumption is more robust than one calibrated to an expected lifespan. Inflation-protected assets and real-terms spending rules handle the second risk directly; the first is handled by a plan that does not depend on dying on schedule.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://fred.stlouisfed.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Plan to a horizon beyond the average lifespan, and state the percentile used.
- Express spending in real terms; a nominal plan is not a plan.
- State the inflation assumption with its source, and test a higher one.
- Record which assets provide inflation protection and what kind.
- Healthcare and long-term care costs are recorded separately, with their growth assumption.
- The plan's sensitivity to living longer than planned is stated.

## Procedure

1. Record the planning horizon and the percentile behind it.
2. Express spending in real terms with the inflation assumption.
3. Identify the inflation-protected portion of the portfolio.
4. Add healthcare costs with an explicit growth rate.
5. Test the plan at a longer horizon and a higher inflation rate.
6. Record the sensitivities in the drawdown policy.

## Anti-patterns

- Planning to the average lifespan.
- Using nominal spending in a long-horizon plan.
- Assuming headline inflation matches household inflation.
- Omitting healthcare cost growth.
- Treating inflation protection as complete when it covers one index.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Horizon stated with the percentile used.

- [ ] Inflation-protected holdings identified.

Both risks are often underestimated for the same reason: they are slow. A percentage point of missed inflation compounds for decades, and a plan built to the average lifespan has a fifty percent chance of being too short. Writing the percentile and the inflation assumption down makes those choices visible rather than implied.
