# Marketing copy

## Standard

- **The buyer's words carry the copy.** The story is written in the customer's voice, so it reads as their account, not the vendor's pitch (<https://resources.rework.com/libraries/strategic-management/jobs-to-be-done>).
- **Named frameworks are delivery formats, not evidence.** StoryBrand SB7, SUCCESs, Golden Circle, PASTOR and AIDA have no controlled evaluation. Each is convention, never proof of effect.
- **Claims carry legal duties, and the label does not dilute them.** An endorsement must reflect the endorser's honest opinion. A material connection must be disclosed clearly and conspicuously — 16 CFR 255, effective 26 July 2023 (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-255). Fake or AI-generated reviews and bought followers are banned — 16 CFR 465 (https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/part-465).
- **An educational format inherits the ad's substantiation duty.** Health claims need "competent and reliable scientific evidence", generally randomised human trials (<https://ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Health-Products-Compliance-Guidance.pdf:>). Earnings claims trigger the Business Opportunity Rule disclosure artefact.
- **Editorial clothing is the format-deception line.** Content that reads as independent but is paid for is deceptive even where its claims are true. The FTC policy statement sits at 81 FR 22596; the EU makes the advertorial unfair in all circumstances.
- **Urgency must be real.** Reset countdown timers are a live enforcement pattern (FTC Act §5). Scarcity that is not a fact is a false statement of fact.

## Decision rules

1. Every performance claim needs a substantiation basis existing before the copy ships.
2. Testimonials need honest opinion, a disclosed connection and typical results beside the exceptional one.
3. Countdown timers and "last chance" lines describe reality or they come out.
4. Sponsored content carries an unambiguous label ("Ad"/"Advertisement") at first contact, before the click.
5. Feed 5–10 real samples to the voice model before drafting, and draft 20+ variants; about 15% of AI outputs carry inaccurate product claims.

## Procedure

1. Harvest the customer's words from reviews, calls and tickets; log each quote verbatim with its source.
2. Write the job story: when [situation], I want [motivation], so I can [outcome].
3. Choose one framework shape for the goal, and record it as a convention.
4. Draft the hero: the problem in the customer's words, the mechanism, the proof, one call to action.
5. Stack proof near the claim it supports: named testimonial, real number, role.
6. Place each rebuttal where the doubt fires — price at the price, effort at the form, trust at the call to action.
7. Run the claims audit; regulated-class claims route to a human. **The human publishes.**

## Anti-patterns

- A vendor arc: the company opens, the company closes, the customer never appears.
- One 300% result with no typical-results context.
- Three calls to action in one resolve.
- Invented urgency or fake social proof.
- A landing-page-only disclosure on an advertorial.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Customer language harvested and logged verbatim.
- [ ] Job story written before copy.
- [ ] Every claim substantiated before publication.
- [ ] Testimonials checked for honesty, connection and typicality.
- [ ] No invented urgency; no fake social proof.
- [ ] Paid content labelled unambiguously at first contact.
- [ ] **Human-gated:** published by a named person.
