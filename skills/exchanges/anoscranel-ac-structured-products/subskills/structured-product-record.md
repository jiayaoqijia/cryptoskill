# Structured product record

## Standard

The product record documents the payoff as a diagram, the issuer as a credit, the cost as a number, and the decision as a statement of what must happen for the note to be worth holding. It is written before purchase, and it is the document that makes a later review a comparison rather than a reconstruction of what the marketing said.

Its most important page is the one that states the bad case. A note's loss is produced by a specific path — a barrier breached near maturity, an underlying that converts, an issuer whose credit deteriorates — and recording that path in advance turns an abstract risk into a scenario the holder has already considered. The record also carries the alternative that was rejected and why.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record contains the payoff diagram, the terms reference, and the three-case summary.
- The issuer assessment is dated and referenced, not duplicated.
- The cost is stated as a percentage of the issue price, with its basis.
- The bad case is written as a path, with the loss in currency.
- The rejected alternative and the reason for rejecting it are recorded.
- Review triggers are dated: barrier proximity, issuer rating change, autocall observation, secondary-market spread.

## Procedure

1. Attach the payoff diagram and the terms reference.
2. Record the three-case summary and the maximum loss.
3. Reference the dated issuer assessment.
4. Record the cost and its basis.
5. Record the rejected alternative and the reason.
6. Set the review triggers with dates.

## Anti-patterns

- A record that repeats the marketing summary.
- Recording the cost only as a fee when the issue-price gap is the larger number.
- Omitting the rejected alternative.
- Setting no triggers, so the position is never reviewed.
- Editing the record after the outcome is known.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Payoff diagram and terms reference attached.
- [ ] Three-case summary written.
- [ ] Issuer assessment referenced with date.
- [ ] Cost recorded with basis.
- [ ] Rejected alternative recorded.
- [ ] Review triggers dated.

A record kept this way also settles disputes: the diagram, the terms reference, and the bad-case path are exactly the evidence a complaint or a review needs, and having written them before the outcome removes the argument about what was understood at the time.
