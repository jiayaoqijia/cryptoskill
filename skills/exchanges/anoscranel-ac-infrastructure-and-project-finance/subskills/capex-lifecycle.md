# Capex lifecycle

## Standard

Infrastructure assets consume capital in a pattern that is not linear. Early years require modest maintenance; the middle of the life is where major components reach the end of their service life and require renewal; and the final years before handback require work to meet the return condition. That middle trough is the point at which a project that looked comfortable on a straight-line capital assumption finds its coverage squeezed.

The analytical error is treating capital expenditure as a smooth annual allowance. A renewal cycle that spends nothing for eight years and then replaces a system is not equivalent to the same total spread evenly, because the concentrated spend coincides with a lock-up test and consumes the reserve that was meant for debt. The record should place the capital expenditure in the years it occurs.

Primary sources: <https://www.worldbank.org/> and <https://www.eib.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the maintenance allowance assumed and how it was derived.
- Record the renewal schedule and the years in which major capital falls.
- Record the capital expenditure as a percentage of revenue in each period.
- Record the funding source for major renewals: reserve, additional debt, or equity.
- Record the interaction between a renewal year and the coverage tests.
- Record any deferred maintenance disclosed, since it is a future obligation.

## Procedure

1. Read the technical and financial models for the capital assumptions.
2. Record the maintenance allowance and the renewal schedule.
3. Place the capital expenditure by year.
4. Record the funding source for each major item.
5. Test the coverage ratio in a renewal year.
6. Write the profile with the trough identified.

## Anti-patterns

- Spreading renewal capital evenly when the asset does not.
- Ignoring the interaction between a renewal year and the lock-up test.
- Assuming additional debt will be available for renewals.
- Treating deferred maintenance as avoided cost.
- Using the concession's early years as the basis for the whole term.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Maintenance allowance recorded.
- [ ] Renewal schedule recorded by year.
- [ ] Capital as a percentage of revenue computed.
- [ ] Funding sources recorded.
- [ ] Coverage tested in a renewal year.
- [ ] Deferred maintenance recorded.

The trough is also where refinancing usually happens: a project refinances after construction and before the renewal cycle, which means the lenders who take the refinancing risk are underwriting a cash flow that will later be consumed by capital the first lenders did not have to fund. The record should note which lender bears the renewal.
