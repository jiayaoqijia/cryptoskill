# Suitability and mis-selling

## Standard

Structured products are sold to buyers who often cannot price them, which makes the sales process the main protection. Suitability therefore turns on three questions: does the buyer understand the payoff, can the buyer bear the loss the payoff permits, and is the buyer paying a cost that can be explained. A product that cannot be explained in plain sentences fails the third question regardless of how it performs.

The recurring failures are documented. Payoffs described by their upside while the barrier removes protection; "capital protected" used without the level or the issuer's credit; backtested participation presented as expected return; and coupons annualised over a term the product is unlikely to survive. Each is detectable by asking for the terms and drawing the diagram, which is why the record keeps those artefacts.

Primary sources: <https://www.finra.org/> and <https://www.esma.europa.eu/>.

## Decision rules

- State in one paragraph what the product pays in the good, flat, and bad cases.
- Record the maximum loss the payoff permits, in currency.
- Record the buyer's capacity to bear that loss, and the basis for the judgement.
- Record the total cost, including the issue-price gap and the secondary spread.
- Record every claim from the marketing material that is not in the terms.
- Where the buyer cannot explain the payoff back, record that and treat it as a fail.

## Procedure

1. Write the three-case summary from the terms.
2. State the maximum loss and the conditions that produce it.
3. Compare the loss with the buyer's stated capacity.
4. Compute or bound the total cost.
5. List marketing claims absent from the terms.
6. Record the suitability conclusion and its basis.

## Anti-patterns

- Accepting a "moderate risk" label as the assessment.
- Relying on the distributor's suitability questionnaire alone.
- Letting backtested performance stand as expected return.
- Recording the upside without the maximum loss.
- Treating a complaint as proof of mis-selling, or a signature as proof of suitability.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Three-case summary written from the terms.
- [ ] Maximum loss stated in currency.
- [ ] Capacity to bear the loss recorded with basis.
- [ ] Total cost bounded.
- [ ] Marketing claims absent from terms listed.
- [ ] Conclusion recorded with its basis.
