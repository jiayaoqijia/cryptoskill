# Capex and deferred maintenance

## Standard

Capital expenditure sits below net operating income, which means a property can show a strong NOI while consuming its own substance: a roof not replaced, a façade not repaired, and a plant not upgraded. Those items do not reduce current income, and they do reduce the value a buyer will pay, because the buyer will fund them.

The distinction that matters is between maintenance needed to sustain the current income and expenditure intended to grow it. A deferred roof is a liability that has been subtracted from neither NOI nor the capitalisation rate, so a valuation that ignores it is overstated by the cost of the roof. The record should state the capital plan, the deferred items with their estimated cost, and which of the two categories each belongs to.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.federalreserve.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the capital plan for the holding period and its cost.
- Record deferred maintenance items with estimated cost and timing.
- Separate sustaining expenditure from growth expenditure.
- Record the reserve per unit or per square metre assumed in the valuation.
- Record which items are the landlord's obligation under the leases.
- Record the effect on NOI of any expenditure that changes rent or occupancy.

## Procedure

1. Record the capital plan and its annual cost.
2. Record deferred items with cost estimates.
3. Separate sustaining from growth expenditure.
4. Record the reserve assumption in the valuation.
5. Record the lease allocation of obligations.
6. Write the capital summary with the deferred total.

## Anti-patterns

- Valuing a property without the deferred maintenance total.
- Treating a reserve as adequate without testing it against the item list.
- Classifying a replacement as growth expenditure.
- Assuming the tenant pays for structural items under a net lease.
- Ignoring capital expenditure in the equity return calculation.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Capital plan recorded.
- [ ] Deferred items costed.
- [ ] Categories separated.
- [ ] Reserve assumption recorded.
- [ ] Lease allocation recorded.
- [ ] NOI effect recorded.

The treatment worth recording explicitly is whether the deferred items were priced by an engineer or estimated by the seller: a schedule prepared by the party selling the asset tends to be optimistic on both scope and cost, and a buyer who has not had the items inspected has accepted an estimate rather than a measurement.
