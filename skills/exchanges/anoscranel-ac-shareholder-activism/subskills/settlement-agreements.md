# Settlement agreements

## Standard

Most campaigns end in a settlement rather than a vote. The typical package gives the activist a number of board seats, sometimes with a committee assignment, and the company receives a standstill: an agreement that the activist will not increase its stake beyond a level, will not run a proxy contest, and will not disparage the company for a stated period. Both sides give up something, and the terms decide how much.

The details that matter are the standstill length, the ownership cap, the voting commitment for the seats granted, and the confidentiality provisions. A short standstill returns the activist to the table quickly; a long one buys the company time. Nominees who must be approved by the board, or who are allocated to a committee with no authority, may be seats in name only.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the number of board seats and whether they are board-approved nominees.
- Record the standstill duration and the ownership cap it sets.
- Record any committee assignments and their powers.
- Record the voting commitment and whether the activist must support the board slate.
- Record the confidentiality and non-disparagement provisions.
- Record any operational commitments the company made in exchange.

## Procedure

1. Read the settlement agreement as filed.
2. Record the seats and their conditions.
3. Record the standstill terms.
4. Record committee assignments and voting commitments.
5. Record any operational commitments.
6. Write the settlement summary with what each side gave up.

## Anti-patterns

- Counting board seats without reading their conditions.
- Ignoring the standstill length, which determines the next round.
- Treating a committee seat as influence without checking its powers.
- Missing confidentiality provisions that suppress later disclosure.
- Assuming the settlement resolves the campaign's demands.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Seats and conditions recorded.
- [ ] Standstill terms recorded.
- [ ] Committee assignments recorded.
- [ ] Voting commitments recorded.
- [ ] Confidentiality provisions recorded.
- [ ] Summary written.

The settlement's durability also depends on what happens after the standstill expires: an activist whose seats remain but whose agreement has lapsed can resume public pressure with board information it obtained in the interim, which is why companies sometimes extend a standstill rather than face a second campaign. Reading the terms with the expiry date in mind is part of assessing the outcome.
