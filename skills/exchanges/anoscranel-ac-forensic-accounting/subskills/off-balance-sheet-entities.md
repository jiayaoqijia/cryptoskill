# Off-balance-sheet entities

## Standard

A company consolidates entities it controls, and the definition of control is principles-based: it turns on variable interests and decision-making authority rather than on a simple ownership percentage. That means a company can have significant exposure to an entity it does not consolidate, and the exposure appears in the notes rather than on the balance sheet.

The questions to document are the ones the boundary raises. What is the company's maximum exposure to an unconsolidated entity, and is it disclosed? Does the company provide liquidity support, guarantees, or management services? Has the entity's consolidation status changed between periods, and if so, what did that change do to the reported figures? Each is answerable from the filings, and each bears on whether the leverage in the group is visible.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.nber.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record each unconsolidated entity named and the company's interest in it.
- Record the maximum exposure to loss where disclosed.
- Record any guarantee, liquidity support, or commitment provided.
- Record changes in consolidation status between periods and their effect.
- Record the accounting policy for consolidation and any judgement disclosed.
- Record transfers of assets to entities where recourse remains.

## Procedure

1. Collect the consolidation policy and the variable-interest note.
2. Record unconsolidated entities and the interest held.
3. Record maximum exposure and support arrangements.
4. Record consolidation changes and their reported effect.
5. Record asset transfers with retained recourse.
6. Write the questions with their document references.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating non-consolidation as non-exposure.
- Ignoring guarantees because they are contingent.
- Missing a consolidation change that flattered a ratio.
- Overlooking management or servicing relationships.
- Reading the balance sheet without the variable-interest note.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Policy recorded.
- [ ] Entities and interests recorded.
- [ ] Maximum exposure recorded.
- [ ] Consolidation changes recorded.
- [ ] Asset transfers recorded.
- [ ] Questions documented.

The figure that deserves the most attention is the difference between the company's stated maximum exposure and the assets the entity holds, because a large gap between them is where a guarantee or a support arrangement usually sits, and it is the part of an unconsolidated structure that reaches back to the parent in a stress.

The arrangement that most often escapes notice is the ordinary commercial one rather than the exotic structure: a supplier finance programme, a receivables sale with recourse, or a joint venture that the company manages but does not control can each keep obligations outside the reported debt, and each is disclosed in a note whose title does not mention leverage.
