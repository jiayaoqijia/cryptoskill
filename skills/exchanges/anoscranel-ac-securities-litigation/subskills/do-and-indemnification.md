# D&O cover and indemnification

## Standard

A securities claim is usually brought against the company and its directors and officers, and who pays depends on the interaction of two mechanisms. Indemnification is the company's promise to reimburse its directors and officers for liabilities incurred in their role, subject to statutory limits and to the company's own financial capacity. Insurance is a policy that responds to covered claims, above a retention, up to a limit.

The layers matter because each has its own boundary. An indemnification obligation that the company cannot fund is worth little, which is why a financially distressed issuer's directors care about the insurance. A policy with a large retention leaves the first layer with the company, and a policy with an aggregate limit can be exhausted by one large claim before the second arrives.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the policy's limit, retention, and the insuring clauses that apply.
- Record what the policy excludes, particularly prior-known-circumstances and conduct exclusions.
- Record the indemnification provisions and any statutory limits on them.
- Record whether the company's indemnification obligation is funded or unfunded.
- Record the aggregate limit and any other claims that may exhaust it.
- Record the side-A cover that responds when the company cannot indemnify.

## Procedure

1. Record the policy terms and the retention.
2. Record the exclusions and their scope.
3. Record indemnification provisions and limits.
4. Record whether side-A cover exists.
5. Record the aggregate and competing claims.
6. Write the allocation summary.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming the policy pays before the retention is met.
- Ignoring conduct exclusions that remove coverage.
- Treating indemnification as equivalent to insurance.
- Overlooking an aggregate limit shared with other claims.
- Assuming side-A cover exists when only side-B and side-C were purchased.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Policy limit and retention recorded.
- [ ] Exclusions recorded.
- [ ] Indemnification provisions recorded.
- [ ] Funding status recorded.
- [ ] Aggregate and competing claims recorded.
- [ ] Allocation summary written.

The question that decides practical recovery is the company's own capacity: where the issuer is solvent and insured, the claim is effectively funded from outside the enterprise, and where it is not, the claim competes with other creditors for the same assets. Recording the company's financial position beside the policy terms is what shows whether the insurance is the real source of payment.
