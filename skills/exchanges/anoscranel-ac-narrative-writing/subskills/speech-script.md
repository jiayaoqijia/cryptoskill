# Speech script

## Standard

- **Announce the shape, out loud, early.** Advance organisers move application at 0.77 while barely touching factual recall at 0.09, and aural organisers beat written ones (<https://doi.org/10.3102/00028312017002211>). Spoken cues raised noted points by 39% and details by 35%.
- **Load-bearing content does not go in the opening.** In a 1982 lecture study, first-part material was recalled significantly worse (F = 19.52, p <.0001) (⚠️ single study, unreplicated). The best-remembered material fell between minutes 15 and 30.
- **The opening is an impression slot, not a memory slot.** Engagement ratings become highly predictive of the final evaluation within the first 10 seconds (⚠️ author-hosted PDF, venue unconfirmed). The effect is faster when nonverbal only.
- **No study contrasts opening typologies or talk length.**
 No trial compares question, anecdote, statistic or provocation openings; any ranked list is ⚠️ unsourced.
- **Build causal chains, not parallel lists.** Events with many causal connections are recalled more. Three factors explained 32–65% of variance in event recall, while the connective word itself is worth about 1.5%. ⚠️ Source not cited inline; check the Sources list before relying on this figure.
- **Speaking rate is a reading ceiling, not a published standard.** BBC subtitle guidelines set news subtitling "up to a subtitling speed of around 160-180wpm" (https://www.bbc.co.uk/accessibility/forproducts/guides/subtitles/). The working script norm is about 150 words per minute conversational (⚠️ no broadcaster publishes a normative rate).

## Decision rules

1. Write the close first: the one thing the room should retain is the last sentence.
2. Name the four chunks aloud at the start, then keep the promise.
3. Replace "moving on" and "another point" with the actual relation; they cost the listener a repair.
4. Repeat the point, not the sentence.
5. Budget by chunk count: one or two major parts in 10 minutes, four to six in 60.
6. Keep the pace under your control: the modality advantage holds only when system-paced.
7. Every fact, quote and number is verified before the script is final.

## Procedure

1. Write the one-sentence takeaway the room should keep.
2. Draft the closing line around that sentence.
3. List and name the four chunks; write the spoken signpost.
4. Draft each chunk as a causal chain: each beat causes or resists the next.
5. Read aloud at about 150 words per minute; cut anything unhearable in one breath.
6. Insert a verbal referent for every visual the audience cannot see.
7. Verify every fact, quote and figure against its source; log the check.
8. Rehearse and cut by ear, not by eye. **The human delivers.**

## Anti-patterns

- Load-bearing content in the first minutes.
- A chunk list announced, then broken.
- "Moving on" and "another point" as connective tissue.
- A minute-count prescription quoted as a standard.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Takeaway sentence written; the close is that sentence.
- [ ] Four named chunks, announced aloud.
- [ ] Causal chains between beats, no parallel lists.
- [ ] Non-causal connectives removed.
- [ ] Paced at about 150 words per minute; breath-tested aloud.
- [ ] Every fact and quote verified.
- [ ] **Human-gated:** delivered by the named speaker.
