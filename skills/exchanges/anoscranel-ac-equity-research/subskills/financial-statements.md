# Financial statements

## Standard

The three statements are one system. Profit on the income statement must show up somewhere on the balance sheet or the cash-flow statement; if it does not, either the business model or the accounting needs explaining. The link is not a formality: it is the cheapest test of whether reported earnings are real.

The cash-flow statement is the auditor of the income statement. Earnings can be managed within the rules; cash arriving from customers is harder to fake. A company that reports years of profit while its operating cash flow lags is telling two stories, and the research job is to find out which is true.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/>.

## Decision rules

- Read the cash-flow statement before the income statement; it anchors the rest.
- Check that retained earnings movements reconcile with net income and dividends.
- Compare operating cash flow with net income across at least three years.
- Treat a widening gap between reported profit and cash as a finding, not a detail.
- Working-capital swings are explained, not waved away; receivables or inventory growing faster than revenue is a flag.
- One-off items are recorded with the line and the year, never netted silently.

## Procedure

1. Extract the three statements for the last three to five years.
2. Verify the articulation: net income to retained earnings, cash changes to balance-sheet cash.
3. Compute operating cash flow minus net income for each year.
4. Examine working-capital components: receivables, inventory, payables, relative to revenue.
5. Note every accounting-policy change and its effect.
6. Write the reconciliation findings with line references.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading only the income statement.
- Accepting "adjusted" earnings as the headline number.
- Ignoring working capital because it is "operational".
- Explaining an earnings-cash gap with a story instead of a line item.
- Comparing statements across different fiscal-year conventions without noting it.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Three statements extracted for three to five years.
- [ ] Articulation verified both ways.
- [ ] Operating cash flow versus net income computed per year.
- [ ] Working-capital components compared with revenue.
- [ ] Accounting-policy changes noted with effect.
- [ ] Findings written with line references.
