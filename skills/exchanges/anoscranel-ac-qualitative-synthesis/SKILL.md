# Qualitative synthesis

## Overview

Qualitative studies produce findings in words, and pooling them statistically would destroy the one thing they carry: meaning in context. A qualitative synthesis instead works from the findings upward — coding them, grouping them, and building descriptive and analytical themes or translations that answer a review question without flattening what the original authors saw. The choice of method decides what the output looks like: aggregated findings, developed themes, translated concepts, or an account of disagreement.

This skill covers that work and its choices: which synthesis approach fits which question, how coding proceeds honestly, how disagreement between studies is handled rather than averaged away, how study quality is appraised with a named tool, and how confidence in a finding is rated for evidence that has no confidence interval. The conduct of the search and screening is shared with systematic review conduct; this is what happens after the studies qualify.

Primary sources: <https://bmcmedresmethodol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2288-8-45> and <https://casp-uk.net/casp-tools-checklists/>.

## When to use

- When synthesising qualitative or mixed-method studies' qualitative arms.
- When choosing between aggregation, thematic development, or translation.
- When disagreement between primary studies must be synthesised, not discarded.
- When rating confidence in qualitative findings.

## When NOT to use

- For the review's search, screening, and protocol machinery (use ac-systematic-review-conduct).
- For statistical pooling of quantitative estimates (use ac-meta-analysis-and-synthesis).
- For writing the synthesis as a document (use ac-academic-writing).

## Status and honest limits

Qualitative synthesis inherits its limits from the primary studies: thick description cannot be created where the studies are thin, and a theme that appears in twenty shallow studies is not deeper than one. Appraisal tools judge conduct, not importance; confidence ratings judge the finding's support, not whether the experience matters. Nothing here certifies what participants meant — it assembles what studies reported they meant, with each assembly step recorded so a reader can disagree with the assembly rather than discover it.
