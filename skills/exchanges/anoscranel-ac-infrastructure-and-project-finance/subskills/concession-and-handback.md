# Concession and handback

## Standard

An infrastructure concession grants the right to operate an asset for a fixed term, and at the end the asset returns to the grantor in a condition the contract specifies. The handback standard is therefore an obligation priced into the last years of the concession: an asset that must be returned in a defined condition requires capital expenditure before expiry, and that expenditure competes with the equity's cash flow exactly when the equity is trying to recover its return.

The term also determines the asset's terminal value, which for most concessions is zero. That is why the equity return is front-loaded and why the discount rate applied to the later years matters more than the early ones. A concession with ten years remaining behaves very differently from the same asset with thirty, and the difference is not a matter of preference but of the arithmetic of a finite claim.

Primary sources: <https://www.worldbank.org/> and <https://www.eib.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the concession start, end, and any extension mechanism.
- Record the handback standard and the maintenance obligation it implies.
- Record the estimated handback capital expenditure and its timing.
- Record the asset's residual value to the equity, which is usually zero.
- Record change-of-law and termination compensation provisions.
- Record the grantor's step-in rights and any performance regime.

## Procedure

1. Read the concession agreement's term and handback clauses.
2. Record the dates and any extension options.
3. Record the handback standard and the works it requires.
4. Estimate the handback expenditure and place it in the cash flow.
5. Record the residual value assumption.
6. Write the analysis with the terminal obligation stated.

## Anti-patterns

- Modelling the cash flow to the end of the term without the handback cost.
- Assuming an extension will be granted.
- Treating the terminal value as the asset's market value.
- Ignoring change-of-law and termination provisions.
- Discounting later years at the same rate as early ones without noting the concentration.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Term and extension recorded.
- [ ] Handback standard recorded.
- [ ] Handback expenditure estimated.
- [ ] Residual value recorded.
- [ ] Change-of-law and termination recorded.
- [ ] Terminal obligation stated in the cash flow.

The handback standard is also a negotiating position: a grantor that will not accept a worn asset can require expenditure the concessionaire disputes, and the dispute is settled after the concession has ended, when the equity has already been distributed. Reserving for it at entry is the conservative treatment and the one that survives an adverse interpretation.
