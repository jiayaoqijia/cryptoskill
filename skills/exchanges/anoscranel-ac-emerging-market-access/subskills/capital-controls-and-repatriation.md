# Capital controls and repatriation

## Standard

Capital controls determine whether an investment can be exited in the currency it was made in, and they operate on two flows. Inflow controls restrict bringing money in, often through registration and approval; outflow controls restrict taking it out, through limits, delays, or approval requirements. A market can be open to entry and closed to exit, which is the configuration that traps capital.

The relevant tests are the repatriation rules for capital and for income, separately, because they often differ. Dividends may be repatriable while principal is not, or the reverse; proceeds from a sale may require a certificate obtained at entry; and the process for approval may take weeks or months, during which the currency can move. Recording the rules and the expected duration converts a policy risk into an operational one.

Primary sources: <https://www.imf.org/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the inflow registration or approval required at entry.
- Record the repatriation rules for capital, dividends, and interest separately.
- Record any limit, delay, or approval required to repatriate.
- Record the documents required at exit and whether they depend on documents obtained at entry.
- Record the historical use of controls: imposition, tightening, loosening, with dates.
- Record the expected duration of the approval process and its variability.

## Procedure

1. Record entry requirements and the certificates obtained.
2. Record the repatriation rules by flow type.
3. Record limits, delays, and approvals.
4. Record the conditions that could change the rules.
5. Record the market's history of using controls.
6. Write the repatriation assessment with the timeline.

## Anti-patterns

- Checking the entry rules and not the exit rules.
- Assuming dividends and capital follow the same repatriation rules.
- Ignoring the certificate obtained at entry which the exit requires.
- Treating current openness as a permanent condition.
- Underestimating the approval's duration in a stress period.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Entry requirements recorded.
- [ ] Repatriation rules by flow recorded.
- [ ] Limits and approvals recorded.
- [ ] Exit documents recorded.
- [ ] Control history recorded.
- [ ] Timeline written.

The asymmetry between entry and exit is the central fact of this risk: a market that invites foreign capital through a registered channel can close the exit without closing the entrance, and by then the position is already held. Recording the exit rules at the point of entry, and dating them, is the only defence available before the policy changes.
