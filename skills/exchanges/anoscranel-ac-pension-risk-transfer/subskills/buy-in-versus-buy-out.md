# Buy-in versus buy-out

## Standard

In a buy-in the insurer holds assets in its own name and pays the scheme an income stream matching the benefits, so the scheme keeps its assets and its liabilities and holds an insurance policy against them. In a buy-out the scheme transfers the assets and the liabilities to the insurer, and the members become policyholders of the insurer rather than beneficiaries of the scheme.

The difference is who bears the residual risk. A buy-in leaves the scheme with the obligations still on its books and the insurer's payments as the matching asset, so trustee responsibility continues and the scheme remains exposed to the insurer's credit. A buy-out extinguishes the scheme's obligation, which is why it is the final step, and why it usually follows a buy-in once the scheme is fully funded on the insurer's basis.

Primary sources: <https://www.fca.org.uk/> and <https://www.bis.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record which transaction is contemplated and what transfers: assets, liabilities, or both.
- Record the scheme's funding position on the insurer's basis, not only the actuarial basis.
- Record the insurer's credit standing, since a buy-in leaves that exposure in place.
- Record the trustee and sponsor responsibilities that continue after the transaction.
- Record the conditions that must be met before a buy-out can follow.
- Record the treatment of members and any change in their legal status.

## Procedure

1. Record the transaction type and what transfers.
2. Record the funding position on the pricing basis.
3. Record the insurer and its credit standing.
4. Record continuing responsibilities.
5. Record the path to a buy-out if a buy-in is chosen.
6. Write the comparison with the residual risks named.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a buy-in as if it removed the liability from the sponsor's balance sheet.
- Comparing prices without comparing what is transferred.
- Ignoring the insurer's credit in a buy-in.
- Assuming a buy-out is available whenever a buy-in is priced.
- Overlooking the member communication and consent requirements.
