# Sovereign versus corporate

## Standard

A corporate borrower can be liquidated and its assets distributed, which gives its creditors a terminal remedy. A sovereign cannot, so its creditors hold a claim that can be serviced, renegotiated, or ignored, and the remedy is a negotiation rather than a recovery. That difference is why sovereign spreads behave differently from corporate spreads at the same rating.

The leverage that replaces liquidation is market access. A sovereign that defaults loses the ability to borrow, and the cost of that loss is the calculation its officials make against the cost of austerity. The creditor's leverage is the same calculation in reverse, which is why the process turns on the debtor's willingness to impose domestic costs rather than on its capacity to pay in an accounting sense.

Primary sources: <https://www.imf.org/> and <https://www.bis.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the issuer's borrowing capacity: reserves, external position, and market access.
- Record the composition of the debt: local-law, foreign-law, official, and multilateral.
- Record the currency composition, since foreign-currency debt cannot be inflated away.
- Record the primary balance required to stabilise the debt ratio.
- Record the political constraints on expenditure adjustment.
- Record the official-sector exposure, which changes the negotiation.

## Procedure

1. Record the debt stock and its composition.
2. Record reserves and the external position.
3. Compute the primary balance needed for stabilisation.
4. Record the currency split.
5. Record official and multilateral exposure.
6. Write the assessment with the adjustment burden stated.

## Anti-patterns

- Applying a corporate recovery framework to a sovereign.
- Ignoring the currency composition, which determines the adjustment's difficulty.
- Treating willingness to pay as a moral question rather than a political calculation.
- Overlooking official-sector exposure, which can subordinate private creditors.
- Assuming market access returns on a schedule.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Debt stock and composition recorded.
- [ ] Reserves and external position recorded.
- [ ] Stabilising primary balance computed.
- [ ] Currency split recorded.
- [ ] Official exposure recorded.
- [ ] Adjustment burden stated.

The comparison that clarifies the difference is the treatment of a missed payment: a company that misses one enters a process with a court and a timetable, while a sovereign that misses one begins a negotiation whose length is set by its own willingness to endure the consequences. That is why the analysis is about sustainability and politics rather than about assets.
