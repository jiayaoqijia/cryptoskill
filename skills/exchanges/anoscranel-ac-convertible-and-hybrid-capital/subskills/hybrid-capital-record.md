# Hybrid capital record

## Standard

The record for a convertible or hybrid states all three exposures on one page: the equity exposure through parity and premium, the credit exposure through the floor and subordination, and the structural exposure through calls, puts, triggers and coupon discretion. Any one of them can become dominant without a trade, which is why the record names which one dominates today.

It also carries the dates that decide outcomes. The call schedule, the put dates, the trigger reference, the coupon payment dates that can be cancelled, and the maturity or perpetuity all belong in one place, because a review that reads only the price cannot see that the instrument's character changed when credit widened or the stock fell through the conversion price.

Primary sources: <https://www.cfainstitute.org/> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the conversion ratio, the conversion price, and parity with a date.
- The floor is recorded with its discount rate and spread inputs.
- Call and put schedules are recorded with prices and notice periods.
- Trigger type and level are recorded for contingent capital, with the loss at trigger.
- The dominant exposure is named explicitly, with the evidence for that judgement.
- Review triggers are dated: stock level against conversion price, spread level, trigger proximity.

## Procedure

1. Record the instrument terms and the conversion mechanics.
2. Record parity, premium, and floor with their inputs.
3. Record the call, put, and trigger schedules.
4. Name the dominant exposure and the evidence.
5. Set the review triggers with levels and dates.
6. File the record and review at each trigger.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording a convertible as an equity position or a bond position but not both.
- Omitting the call schedule from an analysis of yield.
- Leaving the trigger undefined for a contingent capital instrument.
- Naming a dominant exposure without the evidence.
- Reviewing only the price and not the character of the instrument.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Terms and conversion mechanics recorded.
- [ ] Parity, premium, floor recorded with inputs.
- [ ] Call, put, trigger schedules recorded.
- [ ] Dominant exposure named with evidence.
- [ ] Review triggers dated.
- [ ] Record filed and reviewed.

The record's value compounds across a portfolio: with several converts and hybrids recorded in the same format, the aggregate exposure to a single credit, to a sector's volatility, and to call dates in the same quarter becomes visible, and those concentrations are the ones that surprise a book assembled one instrument at a time.
