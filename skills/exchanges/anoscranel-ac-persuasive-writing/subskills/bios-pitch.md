# Bios & pitch

## Standard

- **The investor reads the memo, not the deck.** Build for the memo: weight filings over informal sources, surface contradictions, link claims to evidence and keep figures consistent across documents (<https://aiadvisoryboard.me/blog/fundraising-data-room-ai-prep>).
- **Numbers reconcile to audited sources.** An assistant promotes small nice-sounding facts to headlines unless told what stays small. The scale of every figure is fixed before drafting.
- **Generic output registers as sameness.** A generic deck reads as sameness in about 30 seconds. The founder hand-writes the thesis-fit hook line, because fully drafted outreach reads smooth but generic. ⚠️ Source not cited inline; check the Sources list before relying on this figure.
- **The narrative follows a fixed spine.** A two-page executive summary carries problem, solution, model and unit economics, traction, team, market and why now. It states the raise and the use of proceeds.
- **The founder story follows the customer story rules.** The protagonist is a real customer or founder, and the story opens on the problem in the customer's own words (<https://blueoceansolutions.ai/blog/b2b-case-studies-that-convert-social-proof>). Proof is shown rather than asserted.
- **A bio is a routing document, not a life.** One person, one relevance claim, one verifiable credential; named authors and real-entity signals signal authority.

## Decision rules

1. Fix the figure scale first: which numbers may be headlines, which stay in the appendix.
2. The human writes the hook line and the thesis; the assistant drafts the structure.
3. Every claim links to a document: a filing, a bank statement, a signed contract, a reproducible log.
4. Cross-document figures must agree; a contradiction is flagged, never smoothed.
5. The deck carries no more than 12 slides, one claim per slide.
6. Counsel drafts the cap table, contracts, intellectual-property items and risk factors.
7. Finance and legal review gates run before anything leaves the building.

## Procedure

1. Build the claim ledger: each claim, its document, its location, its verifier.
2. Reconcile every figure across deck, summary and data room; log mismatches by severity.
3. Draft the two-page summary on the spine, then cut to the one sentence the investor should repeat.
4. Draft the deck slide by slide, one claim per slide, with proof beneath.
5. Write the outreach: subject, hook line, traction paragraph, deck attached, follow-up date.
6. Run the skeptical-investor pass: every number, name and date mismatch flagged.
7. Hand the human the draft, ledger and mismatch log. **The human sends.**

## Anti-patterns

- A headline number with no document behind it.
- Figures that differ between the deck and the data room.
- An invented traction claim or a fabricated reference customer.
- A biography that lists every award instead of the one relevant credential.
- Sending a data room before the finance and legal gates.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Claim ledger complete, with a document per claim.
- [ ] Figures reconciled across every document; mismatches flagged.
- [ ] One sentence the reader can repeat, drafted and tested.
- [ ] Deck at or under 12 slides, one claim each.
- [ ] Hook line hand-written by the principal.
- [ ] Finance and legal gates cleared.
- [ ] **Human-gated:** sent or published by a named person.
