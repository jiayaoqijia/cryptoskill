# Risk capacity and tolerance

## Standard

Capacity and tolerance are different constraints and they are frequently confused. Capacity is objective: how large a loss the plan can absorb without failing a goal, given the horizon, the liquidity needs, and the other resources available. Tolerance is subjective: how large a loss the owner can watch without abandoning the policy. The binding constraint is the smaller of the two, and the policy must state which one it is.

The reason this matters is behavioural. A policy written to capacity but beyond tolerance will be abandoned in the first drawdown, at the worst moment. A policy written only to tolerance may be too conservative to fund the goals. Naming both, and the gap between them, is what lets the owner make an informed choice.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Capacity is computed: the maximum drawdown the plan survives without failing a prioritised goal.
- Tolerance is elicited, not assumed: a stated maximum tolerable loss, in currency and percentage, and a stated reaction to a hypothetical.
- The policy adopts the smaller of the two and says which bound it.
- Where tolerance is below capacity, record the gap and the cost of the conservatism.
- Tolerance is tested against a scenario, not a preference statement.
- Both figures are revisited when goals, horizon, or resources change.

## Procedure

1. Compute capacity from goals, horizon, and liquidity needs; show the arithmetic.
2. Elicit tolerance with a concrete scenario and a currency figure.
3. Compare and record the smaller as the binding constraint.
4. Write the gap and its cost where tolerance binds.
5. Record how the figure was elicited, so it can be revisited.
6. Add both to the policy statement.

## Anti-patterns

- Using a questionnaire score as the sole measure of tolerance.
- Assuming high capacity implies high tolerance.
- Adopting tolerance without checking capacity.
- Revising tolerance after a loss, when the revision is really a reaction.
- Stating risk in percentages only, with no currency figure.
