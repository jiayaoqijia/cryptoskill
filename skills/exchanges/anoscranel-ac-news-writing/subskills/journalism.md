# Journalism

## Standard

- **The lede carries the story, not the chronology.** News structure follows the inverted pyramid — the most newsworthy facts first — because editors cut from the bottom up (<https://www.the-efa.org/>).
- **The diamond shape governs long-form.** An anecdotal lede, a nut graf in paragraphs 3–6 carrying the thesis and the urgency, a body of systemic evidence, then a return to the opening subject.
- **Pace by the triad.** Scene about 50%, summary about 30%, exposition about 20%, cycling up and down the ladder of abstraction every 2–3 paragraphs. ⚠️ Source not cited inline; check the Sources list before relying on this figure.
- **Reconstruction has hard rules.** Composite characters, fabricated quotes and assumed interiority are banned. Interiority needs contemporaneous journals or sworn testimony, and physical scenes need two independent witnesses.
- **Attribution is a ladder with ground rules struck first.** On the record, on background, deep background, off the record — never published. The rules are agreed before the disclosure.
- **Fact-checking is institutional, not personal.** Independent checkers corroborate against documents and read back factual assertions rather than whole drafts.
- **Libel elements are stated, and fault standards differ.** Publication of an unprivileged false factual assertion causing reputational injury to an identifiable party. Public figures must prove actual malice; most states require only negligence for private figures.

## Decision rules

1. Every proper noun, date, coordinate and number is checked against a document, not a memory.
2. Quotes are verbatim; context is not narrowed by ellipses.
3. Anonymous sourcing needs a named reason and an editor's approval.
4. Absolute and relative figures are separated; a base rate is stated with a percentage.
5. Damaging allegations get a written breakdown and a response window before publication.
6. Fact-versus-opinion boundaries are marked; opinion is labelled.

## Procedure

1. Fix the claim: one sentence stating what the story shows and why it matters now.
2. Build the evidence map, tier by tier: dockets and filings, agency records, memos, trade press, single-source tips.
3. Interview with the funnel: baseline facts with open questions, locked denials, then the documents.
4. Draft the lede on the strongest verified fact; the nut graf states the stakes.
5. Structure the body in scenes, each entering late and leaving early.
6. Check every fact against its document; log the source for each.
7. Run the legal pass: privilege, opinion boundary, corroboration, response window. **The human publishes.**

## Anti-patterns

- A chronology in place of a lede.
- A single-source allegation stated as fact.
- Interiority written from assumption.
- A response window skipped on a damaging allegation.
- A percentage quoted without its base rate.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Claim stated in one sentence before drafting.
- [ ] Evidence map tiered, with documents for every load-bearing fact.
- [ ] Quotes verbatim, context intact.
- [ ] Attribution ladder respected; ground rules pre-agreed.
- [ ] Legal pass complete: privilege, opinion boundary, corroboration.
- [ ] Response window given and documented.
- [ ] **Human-gated:** published by a named person.
