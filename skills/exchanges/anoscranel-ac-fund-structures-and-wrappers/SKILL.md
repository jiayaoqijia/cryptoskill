# Fund structures and wrappers

## Overview

Two funds can hold identical assets and behave differently because of the legal vehicle that carries them. The wrapper decides who may invest, how often units can be created or redeemed, what leverage is permitted, how the vehicle is taxed, and what happens when too many holders want out at once. Those are not administrative details; they are the terms on which the exposure is offered.

This skill covers the wrapper landscape: open-end and closed-end construction, the European passport and its requirements, the US registered fund framework and its diversification and leverage limits, unit trusts and their equivalents, exchange-traded mechanics including the creation and redemption process, and business development companies. Fund cost and tracking belong to a different skill, and so does the manager.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.esma.europa.eu/>.

## When to use

- When comparing funds that hold the same assets under different wrappers.
- When an investor's eligibility or residence determines what can be bought.
- When a closed-end fund's discount or a BDC's structure needs reading.
- When an ETF's creation mechanism and its stress behaviour matter.

## When NOT to use

- For expense ratios, tracking difference, and fund liquidity (use ac-fund-selection).
- For evaluating the manager and the terms of a private vehicle (use ac-manager-and-vehicle-diligence).
- For subscription lines and fund-level borrowing on private vehicles (use ac-portfolio-financing).

## Status and honest limits

Fund regulation changes, and the rules that determine eligibility can differ by the investor's residence rather than by the fund's domicile. This skill describes the frameworks, not the current text of any rule, and it does not give legal or tax advice. Nothing here recommends a fund, and a wrapper that looks equivalent across two jurisdictions may carry different investor protections, which is precisely the comparison the record exists to make.
