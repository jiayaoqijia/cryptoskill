# Account ordering

## Standard

Accounts differ in when they can be accessed, how withdrawals are taxed, and what happens to what remains. Ordering withdrawals means choosing which of those constraints to accept first. The common frames are taxable-first, tax-deferred-first, and proportional, and each has a rationale that depends on the investor's bracket, age, and the rules of the jurisdiction.

This leaf does not compute tax. It prepares the facts and the questions: the account types, their access rules, their tax treatment, and the ordering implications, so that a professional — or a careful reader of the jurisdiction's rules — can decide with the full picture rather than a rule of thumb.

Primary sources: <https://www.irs.gov/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Every account is classified by access rule and tax treatment, with the jurisdiction stated.
- Required minimum distributions and age-based access rules are recorded as facts, not estimates.
- The ordering candidates are written with their rationale, and the chosen order is justified.
- The interaction with other income is noted as a question for the professional.
- Beneficiary and estate implications are recorded where they affect the order.
- The order is revisited when rules, brackets, or health change.

## Procedure

1. List accounts with their types, access rules, and tax treatment.
2. Record any required distributions or age thresholds.
3. Write the candidate orderings and their rationales.
4. Note the income interactions that need professional input.
5. Record the chosen order and its assumptions.
6. Set the review triggers.

## Anti-patterns

- Applying a rule of thumb from another jurisdiction.
- Ignoring required distributions.
- Ordering on tax alone while ignoring access or estate effects.
- Treating the order as permanent as rules change.
- Computing a liability in the record.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Accounts classified with rules and treatment.
- [ ] Required distributions and thresholds recorded.
- [ ] Candidate orderings and rationales written.
- [ ] Income interactions flagged for professional input.
- [ ] Chosen order recorded with assumptions.
- [ ] Review triggers set.

## Sources

- Tax administration guidance on retirement accounts: <https://www.irs.gov/>
- Investor education on account types: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>

The ordering question changes as retirement progresses: early years are often about access and bracket management, later years about required distributions and what remains for beneficiaries. Recording the order as a staged plan rather than a single rule keeps it honest when the stage changes.
