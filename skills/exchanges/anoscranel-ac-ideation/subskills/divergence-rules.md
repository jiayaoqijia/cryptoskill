# Divergence Rules

## Standard

- **Osborn, _Applied Imagination_ (1953)**: four rules — go for quantity, defer judgment, welcome wild ideas, and combine and improve ideas. <https://www.creativeeducationfoundation.org/>
- **Diehl & Stroebe (1987)**: nominal groups (people working alone, then pooled) produce more and more original ideas than interacting groups; production blocking and evaluation apprehension explain most of the loss. ⚠️ Lab evidence from face-to-face groups; treat as directional, not law. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.53.3.497>
- **Mullen, Johnson & Salas (1991)**: a meta-analysis of brainstorming studies finds interacting groups consistently lose to nominal groups on quantity and quality. ⚠️ Same lab-evidence caveat. <https://doi.org/10.1207/s15324834basp1201_1>
- Generation and evaluation are different cognitive tasks and must not share a meeting.

## Decision rules

1. Defer all judgment during generation; a "no" or "but" closes a thread and the next three.
2. If the room keeps producing variants of the first idea, impose a quota or switch the prompt.
3. If one person dominates, switch to silence-then-share: write alone first, then collect.
4. Wild ideas are allowed and recorded, not defended.
5. Generation and evaluation happen in separate time blocks or separate artifacts; never interleaved.

## Procedure

1. State a quota and a time-box up front (for example, 30 ideas in 15 minutes).
2. Generate silently first: each person writes their own ideas for 5 to 10 minutes.
3. Collect round-robin, one idea per person per turn, recorded verbatim, no commentary.
4. Build on others: "and" before "but"; combine and extend.
5. When the room stalls, change the angle: invert the goal, borrow from another field, or add or remove one constraint.
6. Stop at the quota or at diminishing returns, then move to evaluation as a separate pass.

## Anti-patterns

- **Judging mid-stream.** "That won't work" kills the thread and the next three. Fix: defer.
- **First idea anchors.** Everyone elaborates the first idea. Fix: silence-then-share or a random speaking order.
- **Loudest wins.** One voice fills the list. Fix: write-alone first, then round-robin.
- **Quota absent.** The session stops at five ideas because it "feels done". Fix: state a number.
- **Evaluation dressed as generation.** "We need ideas" becomes "we need the right idea". Fix: split the passes.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Judgment was deferred through the whole generation pass.
- [ ] A quota was stated and met or exceeded.
- [ ] Silence-then-share or round-robin was used, so one voice did not dominate.
- [ ] Ideas were recorded as generated, not filtered live.
- [ ] Generation and evaluation stayed in separate passes.
