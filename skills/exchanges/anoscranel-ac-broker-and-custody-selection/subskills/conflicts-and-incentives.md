# Conflicts and incentives

## Standard

A provider earns in several ways, and each creates a preference. Commission and spread reward activity. Payment for order flow rewards routing to particular venues. Margin lending rewards leverage. Securities lending rewards holding assets in accounts that permit it. Proprietary products reward distribution of the firm's own funds. None of these is misconduct, and all of them shape what the provider is likely to recommend.

The record lists the revenue lines, then asks what each one makes the provider prefer, then checks whether the advice received aligns with that preference. A recommendation that happens to increase the provider's revenue is not thereby wrong, but it deserves the extra question: would the same recommendation be made if the provider earned the same either way.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.finra.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record each revenue line the provider discloses, and any it does not.
- Record what each line makes the provider prefer in products, activity, or routing.
- Record whether the advice received is compensated by commission, by fee, or by neither.
- Where a proprietary product is recommended, record the conflict explicitly.
- Record whether the provider lends securities and whether the client receives the revenue.
- Where disclosure is thin, record the gap rather than assuming neutrality.

## Procedure

1. Collect the provider's fee and revenue disclosures.
2. List the revenue lines and the behaviour each rewards.
3. Identify the compensation model of the advice received.
4. Check recommendations against the incentives.
5. Record the conflicts and the questions they raise.
6. Record the gaps in disclosure.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a disclosed conflict as a resolved one.
- Assuming a fee-based model removes all conflicts.
- Ignoring securities lending revenue when the client receives none of it.
- Reading a conflict as proof of bad advice.
- Overlooking the incentive created by margin lending.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Revenue lines listed.
- [ ] Behaviour each rewards recorded.
- [ ] Advice compensation model recorded.
- [ ] Proprietary-product conflicts flagged.
- [ ] Lending revenue treatment recorded.
- [ ] Disclosure gaps recorded.

The question to carry into any recommendation is not whether a conflict exists, since one always does, but whether the recommendation would survive if the revenue were the same either way. That test is answerable, and it is more useful than a general suspicion.
