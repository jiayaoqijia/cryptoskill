# Sanctions and secondary reach

## Standard

Sanctions take several forms: lists of designated persons and entities whose assets are frozen and with whom dealings are prohibited, sectoral measures that restrict specific activities with a country rather than a person, and secondary measures that reach non-domestic parties who deal with a designated target. The third is the one that catches investors by surprise, because it makes a transaction unlawful even when neither party is named.

Ownership is the mechanism that extends reach. A designated party's majority-owned entities are typically treated as designated themselves, and an entity that is fifty percent or more owned by designated persons can be caught even if it is not on the list. Tracing that requires reading the ownership chain, not the name, which is why the record should state the ownership analysis rather than asserting that a counterparty is clean.

Primary sources: <https://www.imf.org/> and <https://www.wto.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the regime and the type of measure: list, sectoral, or secondary.
- Record the ownership threshold the regime applies and how it aggregates.
- Record the ownership chain for any counterparty above the threshold.
- Record the payment and shipping routes involved, since banks and carriers apply their own screening.
- Record the exposure if a route is blocked, not only if a party is named.
- Record the screening process and its frequency.

## Procedure

1. Record the regime and measure type.
2. Record the ownership threshold and aggregation rule.
3. Trace ownership for each counterparty in the chain.
4. Record payment and logistics routes.
5. Record the blocked-route scenario.
6. Write the reach summary with the chain traced.

## Anti-patterns

- Checking only the named party and not its owners.
- Assuming a non-domestic party is outside the regime's reach.
- Ignoring the bank's own screening, which can block a lawful payment.
- Treating a licence as a permanent permission.
- Relying on a screening tool without reading its date.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Regime and measure type recorded.
- [ ] Ownership threshold recorded.
- [ ] Ownership chain traced.
- [ ] Payment and shipping routes recorded.
- [ ] Blocked-route scenario written.
- [ ] Screening process recorded.

The changing part of the analysis is the ownership chain: a designated party's holdings can change without the list changing, so a counterparty cleared last quarter may be caught this quarter by an acquisition upstream, and the record should date the ownership analysis rather than the screening result.
