# Resource nationalism

## Standard

Resource nationalism is the set of measures a host government can take to capture more of the value from an extractive or strategic asset: higher royalties or taxes, mandatory state participation, export quotas or bans, local-content requirements, or outright renegotiation and expropriation. The measures are usually legal under domestic law and often legal under international investment treaties, which makes the risk structural rather than a breach of contract.

The exposure is not binary. Most cases are renegotiations rather than expropriations, and the investor keeps the asset on worse terms. That means the relevant estimate is the change in the present value under a plausible renegotiation, not the probability of losing everything, and the record should state the mechanism and the expected magnitude rather than a headline fear.

Primary sources: <https://www.imf.org/> and <https://www.wto.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the measures available to the host government under its own law.
- Record the fiscal terms and how they compare with comparable jurisdictions.
- Record any stabilisation clause or investment treaty protection.
- Record the state's existing participation and its direction of travel.
- Record the export restrictions in force or proposed.
- Estimate the value effect of a plausible renegotiation, not only of expropriation.

## Procedure

1. Record the fiscal and regulatory terms.
2. Record the measures available and their legal basis.
3. Record treaty and contract protections.
4. Record the state's participation and history.
5. Estimate the value change under a renegotiation.
6. Write the assessment with its mechanism and magnitude.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating expropriation as the only risk.
- Ignoring that a renegotiation can be more costly than a taking.
- Assuming a stabilisation clause is enforceable in practice.
- Comparing fiscal terms without the broader cost base.
- Reading history as a guarantee of stability.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Fiscal terms recorded.
- [ ] Measures and legal basis recorded.
- [ ] Protections recorded.
- [ ] State participation recorded.
- [ ] Renegotiation value effect estimated.
- [ ] Mechanism and magnitude written.

The measure that is most often underestimated is the export quota, because it does not change the fiscal terms and therefore does not appear in a comparison of tax regimes, yet it can prevent a producer from selling at the price that made the investment attractive, which is a revenue restriction rather than a cost increase.
