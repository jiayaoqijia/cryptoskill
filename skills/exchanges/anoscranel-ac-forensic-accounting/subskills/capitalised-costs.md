# Capitalised costs

## Standard

A cost that is capitalised is recorded as an asset and charged to expense over time rather than immediately. The policy that governs this is disclosed, and the choice within the permitted range is a judgement: a company that capitalises more of its development or acquisition costs reports higher earnings now and lower earnings later than one that expenses.

The observable pattern is a capitalised balance growing faster than the revenue it supports, or a capitalisation rate that rises when results weaken. Both are disclosed in the notes and both raise the same question: whether the costs would have been capitalised under the policy in a stronger year. The record should state the policy, the balance, the amortisation period, and the trend relative to revenue.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.nber.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the capitalisation policy and the categories it covers.
- Record the capitalised balance and its growth by period.
- Record the amortisation period and whether it has changed.
- Record capitalised costs as a share of revenue and of capital expenditure.
- Record any change in policy or in the rate of capitalisation.
- Record impairments and their timing relative to the capitalisation.

## Procedure

1. Record the policy and its categories.
2. Record balances and growth by period.
3. Record amortisation periods and changes.
4. Compute intensity against revenue and capital expenditure.
5. Record policy changes and impairments.
6. Write the questions with their figures cited.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a permitted policy as a misstatement.
- Ignoring a lengthening amortisation period.
- Comparing intensity across companies with different business models.
- Overlooking an impairment that follows a period of heavy capitalisation.
- Reading the balance without the policy note.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Policy recorded.
- [ ] Balances and growth recorded.
- [ ] Amortisation periods recorded.
- [ ] Intensity computed.
- [ ] Changes and impairments recorded.
- [ ] Questions cited.

The comparison that exposes most of the pattern is the capitalisation rate against the prior three years: a company that capitalises a rising share of the same cost categories while revenue growth slows is one whose reported margin is being supported by a policy choice rather than by the business, and the two series together show that where either alone would not.

A second, quieter signal is the amortisation period itself: extending the life over which a balance is written off reduces the annual charge without changing the asset, and a company that lengthens the period in a year when results are weak has changed its reported profit through an estimate rather than through its operations.
