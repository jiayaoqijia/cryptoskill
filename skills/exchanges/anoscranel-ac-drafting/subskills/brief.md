# Brief

## Standard

- **ISO 24495-1:2023, clause 5.1 (relevant)** (https://www.iso.org/standard/78907.html ⚠️ text paywalled; clause titles read from public previews):
 identify the readers, their purpose, the reading context, the document type, and the content they need, before writing.
- **Reader modelling has measured effect sizes.** Recasting abstracts on a traditional→accessible continuum raised readability 44.4%→66.3% and understanding 47.9%→57.9% on 170 undergraduates (Frey, *Front Psychol* 2021, https://frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.714321/full). Same corpus: high-familiarity readers skipped over-simplified summaries, including the limitations (https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3613904.3642289).
- **The plain-language rule**: "write for your audience"; "most federal writing has no place for literary flair" (digital.gov, successor to plainlanguage.gov: https://digital.gov/guides/plain-language/principles).
- A brief is a short decision record. It names reader, decision, use, constraints and success test. A task verb is not a brief.

## Decision rules

1. Name one primary reader and the decision they hold. "Who signs this?" If nobody decides, the piece is not needed.
2. Purpose is one sentence with a verb: "persuade the vendor to approve a pilot in Q3."
3. Capture venue rules before prose: length, structure, style guide, citation style, deadline, approval chain, legal review.
4. Write the success test as a reader action, not a feeling: approve, fund, reply, attend, buy.
5. Missing brief → write the brief first. Never draft from the task verb alone.
6. Split readers → split the document, or write for the primary reader and summary the rest.

## Procedure

1. Write the brief in this shape:
   ```markdown
   Reader (one person, their decision):
   Purpose (one sentence, verb):
   Use (where it is read, on what device, in what context):
   Success test (what the reader does next):
   Venue rules (limits, structure, style, citations, deadline, approvers):
   Known constraints (facts that cannot be claimed):
   ```
2. List the three questions the reader will ask first. Those become the outline's first sections (subskills/outline-structure.md).
3. Mark any claim the brief cannot support yet. It becomes a research task (out of scope for this leaf: finding and judging sources).
4. Send the brief back to the requester when reader or success test is missing. Do not guess both.
5. Keep the brief beside the draft. Re-read it after the first full draft.

## Anti-patterns

- Drafting the document because a verb was given.
- Audience written as "everyone" or "stakeholders".
- Success test written as "the reader understands it".
- Venue rules discovered after the draft is complete.
- No brief, and no request for one.

## Done checklist

- [ ] One named primary reader and their decision.
- [ ] Purpose in one sentence with a verb.
- [ ] Reading context and use recorded.
- [ ] Success test states a reader action.
- [ ] Venue rules captured: length, structure, style, citations, deadline, approver.
- [ ] Unsupported claims marked for research.
- [ ] Brief confirmed with the requester.
