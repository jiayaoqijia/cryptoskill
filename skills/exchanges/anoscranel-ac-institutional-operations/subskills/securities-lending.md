# Securities lending

## Standard

Securities lending transfers title to the borrower against collateral and a fee. The lender receives a rebate or a fee, the borrower gains the securities to deliver or to sell short, and the collateral protects the lender if the borrower fails. The arrangement is economically a secured loan, and its risks are the collateral's quality and the lender's ability to get the securities back.

Recall is the risk that matters for anyone relying on the lent position. The lender can demand the securities back, and the borrower must return them, which for a short seller means buying them in the market. That is why a lending programme's terms — who may recall, on what notice, and how the collateral is marked — determine whether an apparently available position is actually available.

Primary sources: <https://www.bis.org/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the lent securities, the borrower, and the collateral posted.
- Record the collateral type, haircut, and marking frequency.
- Record the rebate or fee and the revenue split between lender and agent.
- Record the recall terms and the notice period.
- Record what happens if the borrower fails and the collateral must be sold.
- Record the lending programme's scope: which assets are eligible and any limits.

## Procedure

1. Record the lending arrangement and parties.
2. Record collateral and its terms.
3. Record the fee and revenue split.
4. Record recall terms.
5. Record the borrower-default path.
6. Write the summary with the recall risk named.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating lent securities as still available.
- Ignoring the collateral's own market risk.
- Assuming recall is immediate.
- Overlooking the revenue split when assessing the programme's value.
- Reading the programme summary rather than the lending agreement.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Arrangement and parties recorded.
- [ ] Collateral and marks recorded.
- [ ] Fee and split recorded.
- [ ] Recall terms recorded.
- [ ] Default path recorded.
- [ ] Recall risk named.

The programme's economics are also a function of the borrower's demand: lending revenue rises when short interest is high and borrow is scarce, which means the revenue is largest precisely when the recall risk is greatest, and a programme evaluated on its recent revenue will look best at the moment it is most fragile.
