# Tone

## Standard

- **Footing is what the reader actually scores.**
 The reader reads tone from three surfaces: address forms, hedges and modality, and who is named as agent.
 "You may want to re-check that number" is advice from a peer, a correction from a superior, or sarcasm from a subordinate. Same words, different act.
 With no prosody and no negotiation, footing is read once and cannot be repaired (<https://doi.org/10.5040/9781350023857.0012>).
- **Face threat sets the dose.** Brown and Levinson: every adult claims positive face (an approved self-image) and negative face (freedom from imposition).
 Requests threaten the reader's negative face, criticism their positive face, apologies your own.
 Weightiness Wx = social distance + the reader's power over you + the imposition's rank (<https://www.historicalpoliteness.net/bibliography/>).
 Redress runs from bald on-record through positive and negative politeness to off-record.
- **Hedging is insurance, not a competence penalty.** Sah, Moore and MacCoun (n = 184): when wrong, confident advisors scored 2.23/5 against 2.84 for hedged (https://escholarship.org/content/qt1ww5g76g/qt1ww5g76g.pdf). When right it inverted — 4.00 versus 3.67.
- **Warmth is the weakest axis.** No corpus dimension and no measured outcome exist for warmth. "Be warm" is advice, not a finding. Formality is corpus-anchored in Biber's Dimension 1.

## Decision rules

1. Hedge claims you might be wrong about; never those you are right about. Never hedge a recommendation or a promise — the act, not the confidence, is scored.
2. Choose footing before diction. A petitioner opening ("we respectfully submit") installs the register before content arrives.
3. Choose the redress rung by weightiness: more distance, reader power, or a bigger ask moves you toward deference.
4. Politeness is a cost lower-status speakers keep paying: polite Wikipedia editors won more elections and turned less polite once elevated (https://arxiv.org/abs/1306.6078). In any ask you are the lower-status party.
5. ⚠️ No study manipulates tone inside a graded document and measures the score; these are mechanism rules, not dose-response.

## Procedure

1. Rate the reader's power over you, the distance, and the size of the ask. That sum picks the rung.
2. Bad news: the fact in sentence one, the reason in sentence two, the path forward in sentence three.
3. Refusal: the decision once, the reason, the alternative or boundary. Do not re-argue in the close.
4. Apology: name the act and the impact; take responsibility without confessing intent. Never "sorry if anyone was offended".
5. Requests: the ask, the deadline, and what happens next — one line each.
6. Criticism of another's text runs reader-side: "readers may find this contradictory", not "this is confusing" (out of scope for this leaf: the full editorial-comment style).
7. Read the opening clause aloud: it installs footing, so revise it first.

## Anti-patterns

- Hedging everything, including the recommendation.
- An apology stack in front of bad news.
- Cheerfulness performed on a serious message.
- Over-formal inflation ("utilize" for "use") as a substitute for respect.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Footing set in the opening clause.
- [ ] Hedges match accuracy; recommendations unhedged.
- [ ] Bad news, refusal and apology follow the three-part shape.
- [ ] Register matches the reader's power and the ask's size.
- [ ] No warmth token traded against precision.
