# Local content and transfer pricing

## Standard

Local-content rules require a share of a product's value to be produced domestically, often as a condition of market access or of a subsidy. They change a company's cost base rather than its revenue: a firm that must source locally may pay more for inputs, and one that cannot meet the threshold may lose access to a market entirely. The margin effect is a function of the cost differential at the required percentage.

Transfer pricing is the other side. A multinational's reported profit in any jurisdiction depends on the prices it sets on intra-group transactions, and tax authorities require those prices to be arm's length. A change in the rules, or a dispute about the method, moves profit between jurisdictions without any change in the underlying business, which changes the after-tax return on an investment in a particular entity.

Primary sources: <https://www.imf.org/> and <https://www.wto.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the local-content requirement and the proportion it demands.
- Record the cost differential between domestic and imported inputs.
- Record the market access or subsidy that depends on compliance.
- Record the transfer-pricing method used and the jurisdictions involved.
- Record any audit, dispute, or advance ruling in progress.
- Record the effective tax rate by jurisdiction and its sensitivity to a method change.

## Procedure

1. Record the localisation rule and its threshold.
2. Compute the cost differential at the required share.
3. Record the access or subsidy at stake.
4. Record the transfer-pricing method and jurisdictions.
5. Record disputes and rulings.
6. Write the summary with the margin effect at both ends.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating local content as a revenue rule rather than a cost rule.
- Ignoring the cost differential when the requirement is met.
- Assuming the transfer-pricing method is settled because it has been used.
- Comparing effective tax rates across entities without the method.
- Overlooking a pending dispute disclosed in the filings.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Localisation rule and threshold recorded.
- [ ] Cost differential computed.
- [ ] Access or subsidy recorded.
- [ ] Pricing method and jurisdictions recorded.
- [ ] Disputes recorded.
- [ ] Margin effect written.

The interaction between the two is where the margin actually moves: a company that satisfies local content by sourcing from a related party at a transfer price is doing both at once, and a change in the pricing method can undo the cost advantage that the localisation was meant to create, so the two should be modelled together rather than as separate adjustments.
