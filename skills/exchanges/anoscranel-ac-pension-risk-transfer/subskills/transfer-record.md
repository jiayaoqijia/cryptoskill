# Transfer record

## Standard

The transfer record states the instrument, the price and its drivers, the risks that remain after the transaction, and the timeline. It is written before the decision, so that a later review can distinguish a transaction that was well judged from one that happened to complete in a favourable market, which are different conclusions.

The residual section is the part that earns its place. No transfer removes every risk: a buy-in leaves insurer credit and trustee responsibility, a longevity swap leaves basis and counterparty exposure, and a partial transfer leaves the remainder to be managed. Naming those residuals at the outset prevents the transaction from being treated as an ending when it is a step.

Primary sources: <https://www.fca.org.uk/> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the instrument and what transfers: assets, liabilities, or both.
- The price is recorded with its date, its basis, and the drivers identified.
- The insurer or counterparty is recorded with its credit standing and a date.
- The residuals are named with their measurement where possible.
- The timeline records quotation, approval, execution, and completion dates.
- Review triggers are dated: funding level, market yields, insurer rating, member experience.

## Procedure

1. Record the instrument and the scope of the transfer.
2. Record the price, basis, and drivers.
3. Record the counterparty and its credit.
4. Record the residuals and their measurement.
5. Record the timeline and the approvals required.
6. Set review triggers and amend at each.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording the price without the basis it was struck on.
- Omitting the residuals, which is the risk the transaction did not remove.
- Leaving the counterparty's credit out of a buy-in record.
- No timeline, so the decision's conditions are lost.
- Treating completion as the end of the analysis.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Instrument and scope recorded.
- [ ] Price, basis, and drivers recorded.
- [ ] Counterparty and credit recorded.
- [ ] Residuals named with measurement.
- [ ] Timeline and approvals recorded.
- [ ] Triggers dated.

The record's most useful test is whether it would let a new trustee understand the decision a year later: the instrument, the price and its drivers, the residuals, and the timeline should be sufficient to reconstruct why the transaction was done, and if they are not, the record is describing an outcome rather than a decision.
