# Revenue recognition

## Standard

Revenue is recognised when control of the good or service transfers, and the judgement is in deciding when that happened. A contract with several obligations, a long delivery period, or a right of return gives management discretion over the period in which revenue appears, and the discretion is legitimate within the standard. It becomes a quality question when the timing consistently favours the reporting period.

The balance sheet is the witness. Revenue recognised before cash arrives shows up as receivables; revenue recognised before the good ships shows up as inventory that has not moved; revenue recognised subject to returns shows up as a refund liability that grows. Reading the revenue line beside those balances is what turns a growth story into a set of testable questions.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the revenue recognition policy from the accounting note.
- Record receivables growth against revenue growth over the same periods.
- Record inventory days and any build ahead of reported sales.
- Record refund and return liabilities and their trend.
- Record any change to recognition policy, with the period and the effect disclosed.
- Record related-party revenue and its share where disclosed.

## Procedure

1. Read the revenue recognition note.
2. Compare receivables growth with revenue growth.
3. Compare inventory days with the sales trend.
4. Track refund and return liabilities.
5. Note policy changes and their effect.
6. Write the questions the ratios raise.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading revenue growth without the receivables trend.
- Ignoring a change in recognition policy.
- Treating a seasonal inventory build as a signal.
- Assuming related-party revenue is improper.
- Concluding misconduct from a timing judgement the standard permits.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Policy recorded.
- [ ] Receivables growth compared.
- [ ] Inventory days compared.
- [ ] Refund liabilities tracked.
- [ ] Policy changes recorded.
- [ ] Questions written.

The useful comparison is between revenue growth and the cash collected from customers, which the cash flow statement discloses separately: when revenue grows faster than collections across several periods, the difference is sitting in receivables, and the question of whether those receivables will be collected is answerable from the ageing note rather than from the revenue line.


The revenue question is ultimately a question about whether the reported growth will be collected, and every balance-sheet witness in this leaf is a way of asking that same question from a different angle, which is why the series across periods answers it and a single quarter's figures do not.
