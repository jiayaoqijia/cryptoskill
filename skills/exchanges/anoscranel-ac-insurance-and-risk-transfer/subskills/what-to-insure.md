# What to insure

## Standard

The case for insurance rests on a loss that is severe, infrequent, and unaffordable if it lands. A catastrophic liability, a house fire, a long disability: each is rare enough that the premium is affordable and large enough that self-funding would be ruinous. The case against insurance rests on the opposite: small, frequent losses that the premium's load makes more expensive than simply paying.

The test is arithmetic and behavioural. Arithmetically, the premium must be small relative to the loss it protects and the expected loss must be low, or the transfer is a purchase of convenience rather than protection. Behaviourally, a retained risk must be one the household will actually fund when it occurs, which is why a reserve is part of the decision rather than an afterthought.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.naic.org/>.

## Decision rules

- List each risk with its severity, its frequency, and the cost of self-funding.
- Transfer risks whose loss would be unaffordable and whose frequency is low.
- Retain risks whose loss is small relative to the premium, backed by a stated reserve.
- Record the premium as a percentage of the coverage limit.
- Record which risks are legally mandated or contractually required.
- Reassess when the household, the assets, or the prices change.

## Procedure

1. List the risks: liability, property, health, disability, mortality, and any contractual requirements.
2. For each, estimate severity and frequency, with sources.
3. Compare the premium with the coverage and with the reserve needed to self-insure.
4. Classify each risk as transfer, retain, or mandatory.
5. Record the reserve for the retained risks.
6. Write the classification with its rationale.

## Anti-patterns

- Insuring small losses because the premium feels small.
- Retaining a catastrophic risk without a reserve.
- Ignoring contractual or legal requirements.
- Comparing premiums across policies with different limits.
- Treating insurance as an investment product.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Risks listed and estimated.
- [ ] Premium compared with coverage and with self-insurance.
- [ ] Each risk classified.
- [ ] Reserve for retained risks stated.
- [ ] Mandatory coverages recorded.
- [ ] Classification and rationale written.

The classification is a decision record, not a shopping list: recording why a risk was retained makes the retained exposure visible, which is what allows the reserve to be sized against it rather than against a general sense of caution.
