# Goals and horizon

## Standard

A policy begins with what the money is for. The statement that survives scrutiny names each goal, its amount, its date, and its priority, because those four facts determine everything downstream: the horizon sets how much volatility the capital can absorb, the priority sets what gets funded first when returns disappoint, and the amount sets whether the goal is plausible at all.

Vague objectives produce vague policy. "Growth" is not a goal; "fund a stated expenditure in year eight without drawing on the reserve" is. The discipline is to convert each intention into a claim that can be judged later, and to write down the ones that cannot be quantified as qualitative goals with their reasoning.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Every goal has an amount, a date, and a priority; a goal missing any of the three is recorded as unquantified and flagged.
- Goals are separated by horizon: short (funds needed within a few years), medium, and long.
- Short-horizon goals constrain the policy more than any return target; they set the floor of low-volatility assets.
- Priorities are ranked explicitly, so a shortfall has a stated order of sacrifice.
- Liquidity needs are recorded as amounts and dates, not as a percentage.
- If the goals are not plausible given the capital, record the gap rather than adjusting the assumptions to close it.

## Procedure

1. List every goal with amount, date, and priority.
2. Group by horizon and mark the unquantified ones.
3. Record liquidity needs as dated amounts.
4. Test plausibility: required return implied by each goal, shown as arithmetic.
5. Record the gap where the implied return is implausible.
6. Write the goals section with priorities and the plausibility note.

## Anti-patterns

- Stating "capital growth" as a goal.
- Omitting the date because it feels flexible.
- Setting a return target first and inventing goals to justify it.
- Ignoring a short-horizon need when setting allocation.
- Closing a plausibility gap by assuming a higher return.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Every goal has amount, date, and priority.
- [ ] Goals grouped by horizon; unquantified ones flagged.
- [ ] Liquidity needs recorded as dated amounts.
- [ ] Implied returns computed and shown.
- [ ] Plausibility gap recorded where present.
- [ ] Goals section written with priorities.
