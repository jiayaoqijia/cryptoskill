# Manager record

## Standard

The record states the manager, the vehicle, the terms agreed, the tests applied in diligence, and the evidence each test produced. Separating the test from the conclusion is what makes the record reviewable: a later reader can see whether the administrator reconciliation was obtained or assumed, and whether the fee stack was modelled or quoted.

It also carries the conditions of the relationship. The reporting the allocator will receive, the information rights it holds, the review cycle, and the triggers that would cause a re-underwriting — a key-person departure, a fee change, a gate being invoked, a change of control — belong in the record before the allocation, because after it the allocator's leverage is reduced.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record names the manager, the vehicle, the strategy, and the allocation size.
- The fee stack is recorded as a modelled all-in cost at stated return levels.
- Each diligence test is recorded with its evidence and its result.
- Side letter terms and MFN rights are recorded.
- Liquidity terms are recorded as a stress exit timeline.
- Review triggers are dated, and the re-underwriting conditions are stated.

## Procedure

1. Record the manager, vehicle, and allocation.
2. Record the modelled fee stack and its assumptions.
3. Record each test with evidence and result.
4. Record side letter and MFN terms.
5. Record the liquidity timeline.
6. Set review triggers and file the record.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording conclusions without the tests behind them.
- Quoting a fee rather than modelling it.
- Omitting the side letter from the terms summary.
- Leaving the liquidity timeline as a list of provisions.
- Setting no triggers, so the relationship is reviewed only at fundraising.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Manager and vehicle recorded.
- [ ] Fee stack modelled.
- [ ] Tests recorded with evidence.
- [ ] Side letter and MFN recorded.
- [ ] Liquidity timeline recorded.
- [ ] Triggers and conditions set.

The record also serves as the baseline for the next diligence cycle: comparing the terms and tests recorded at allocation with those found at review shows whether the relationship drifted, and drift in governance and liquidity terms is far easier to detect against a written baseline than from memory of a negotiation that happened years earlier.
