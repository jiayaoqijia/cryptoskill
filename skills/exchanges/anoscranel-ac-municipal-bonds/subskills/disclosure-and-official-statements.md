# Disclosure and official statements

## Standard

The official statement is the municipal market's prospectus, and it is filed at issuance rather than continuously. After that, the issuer commits to continuing disclosure — annual financial information and notices of material events — and the quality of that commitment varies enormously between issuers and between sectors.

Reading it means locating the parts that decide the credit rather than the parts that describe the project. The financial statements and their notes, the pension and OPEB disclosure, the debt schedule with its maturity profile, the rate covenant, and the litigation and contingent liability section are the substance. The cover page, the project description, and the rating summary are the parts written to be read, and they are the parts most likely to omit the finding.

Primary sources: <https://emma.msrb.org/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the official statement date and the fiscal year of the financials it contains.
- Record the continuing disclosure undertaking and whether filings have been made on time.
- Record the audited financial statements' opinion and any qualifications.
- Record the pension and OPEB disclosure with its funding ratio and assumptions.
- Record the debt schedule and the maturity profile.
- Record any material event notices filed since issuance.

## Procedure

1. Obtain the official statement and the most recent continuing disclosure.
2. Record the filing dates and the fiscal periods covered.
3. Read the financial statements and their notes.
4. Record the pension and OPEB disclosure.
5. Record the debt schedule and any recent event notices.
6. Write the findings with the gaps named.

## Anti-patterns

- Relying on the cover page and the rating summary.
- Assuming continuing disclosure has been filed on time.
- Reading financials without their fiscal year.
- Ignoring the notes, where the contingent liabilities sit.
- Treating an unqualified opinion as a credit assessment.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Statement date and fiscal year recorded.
- [ ] Continuing disclosure status recorded.
- [ ] Audit opinion recorded.
- [ ] Pension and OPEB figures recorded.
- [ ] Debt schedule recorded.
- [ ] Material event notices recorded.

A further check is the tone of the document: an official statement that spends pages on the project and one paragraph on the debt service coverage is telling you where the issuer's attention was. The proportion of disclosure devoted to the pledge and the financials, rather than to the description of the asset, is a crude but reliable signal of how carefully the credit was presented.
