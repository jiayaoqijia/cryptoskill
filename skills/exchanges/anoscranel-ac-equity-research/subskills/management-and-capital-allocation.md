# Management and capital allocation

## Standard

Management's job with the cash the business generates is to allocate it: reinvest, acquire, pay down debt, buy back shares, or pay dividends. The record of those choices, over years, is the most objective evidence available about competence and intent. A team that repeatedly buys back stock at high prices and issues it at low prices has told you something that no strategy presentation can undo.

Alignment is the second question. Insider ownership, compensation structure, and the history of share issuance are all disclosed, and together they describe whether the people running the company are paid for the same outcomes as the owners.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Build the capital-allocation table for at least five years: cash generated, and where it went.
- Buybacks are judged against the price paid relative to later performance, not against the announcement.
- Share issuance history is dilution history; record the share count over time.
- Insider ownership and sales are read from ownership filings, not from news.
- Compensation is compared with the metrics it rewards; if the metrics are not capital returns, note it.
- A change of chief financial officer or auditor is recorded and explained.

## Procedure

1. Extract cash from operations, capex, acquisitions, buybacks, dividends, and debt changes for five years.
2. Compute the split of capital deployed across those categories.
3. Chart the share count over the same period.
4. Pull insider ownership and transactions from ownership filings.
5. Read the compensation disclosure and identify the metrics that pay.
6. Write the record: what the team did, at what prices, and how it was paid.

## Anti-patterns

- Judging capital allocation by a single year.
- Accepting a buyback announcement as evidence of good allocation.
- Ignoring dilution across a decade because this year's share count looks stable.
- Reading insider activity through news summaries.
- Treating a charismatic presentation as evidence of competence.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Five-year capital-allocation table built.
- [ ] Split of capital deployed computed.
- [ ] Share count charted over the period.
- [ ] Insider ownership and transactions pulled from filings.
- [ ] Compensation metrics identified.
- [ ] Record written with prices and outcomes.
