# Fund record

## Standard

The fund record documents why a vehicle was chosen over the alternatives available on the same day. It carries the exposure the vehicle provides, the total cost, the tracking evidence, the structure and counterparty position, the liquidity assessment, and the review triggers that will bring the decision back onto the table.

Its value appears at the next review: a fund that has since cut its fee, changed its index, or grown too large for its market can be compared against the original record and the change made deliberately. Without the record, every review starts from the fund's current marketing, which is exactly what the selection was supposed to look past.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the exposure and the alternatives considered, with their costs.
- Every figure carries a date; fund documents change.
- The structure and counterparty position is recorded in one paragraph, not implied.
- Review triggers are dated: fee changes, index changes, size thresholds, tracking drift.
- The record is append-only; corrections are dated amendments.
- A note records which questions were left for a tax or investment professional.

## Procedure

1. State the exposure required and the alternatives compared.
2. Record the cost stack for each.
3. Record tracking difference and error for the chosen fund.
4. Summarise structure, counterparty, and liquidity.
5. Set review triggers with dates.
6. File the record and reference it from the allocation document.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording the chosen fund without the alternatives.
- Leaving out the cost stack.
- Setting no review triggers.
- Using undated figures.
- Editing the record after a fund change instead of amending it.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Exposure and alternatives recorded.
- [ ] Cost stack for each alternative.
- [ ] Tracking evidence for the chosen fund.
- [ ] Structure, counterparty, liquidity summarised.
- [ ] Review triggers dated.
- [ ] Record filed and referenced.

The alternatives section is what makes the record a decision rather than a preference. Recording the funds that were rejected, with the specific reason and the date, means that a later review can see whether the reason still holds or whether the chosen fund has drifted into the rejected category.

A record that is one page long and dated is more likely to be used than a thorough one that is not; the review reads the page, and the detail exists to be consulted when a figure is challenged.
