# Trade policy and restrictions

## Overview

Trade policy is a set of rules that change which goods may move, at what cost, and between whom. For an investor the transmission is specific: a tariff changes a margin at a named point in a supply chain, an export control removes access to a component, a sanction blocks a payment route, and an investment-screening rule changes who may own an asset. Each is a rule with a defined scope, and each reaches an investment through a channel that can be traced.

This skill covers that tracing: who actually bears a tariff, how export controls propagate through suppliers, how sanctions reach beyond their named targets, how screening and ownership limits affect valuation and liquidity, and how local-content and transfer-pricing rules alter a subsidiary's economics. It maps policy into cash flow; it does not predict policy.

Primary sources: <https://www.wto.org/> and <https://www.imf.org/>.

## When to use

- When a tariff, control, or sanction could change a company's cost or access.
- When foreign ownership limits or screening affect an investment's transferability.
- When local-content or transfer-pricing rules change a subsidiary's economics.
- When a portfolio's exposure to a policy channel needs quantifying.

## When NOT to use

- For the commodity price and curve mechanics (use ac-commodities-trading).
- For access routes into an emerging market (use ac-emerging-market-access).
- For the investment case on a company (use ac-equity-research).

## Status and honest limits

Policy is announced, amended, and enforced with discretion, and the enforcement of a rule can matter more than its text. Nothing here is legal advice, and a sanctions or export-control question about a specific transaction belongs with counsel. The record should state the rule, its date, and the channel it reaches the investment through, rather than a general view about which way policy is going.
