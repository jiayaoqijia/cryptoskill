# What a factor is

## Standard

A factor is a characteristic on which securities can be ranked cross-sectionally and which has been associated with a return premium across many securities and periods. That definition has three parts, and all three matter: the characteristic must be observable for every security in the universe, the ranking must produce a meaningful spread, and the premium must be documented across samples rather than found in one.

A style label is not a factor. Style classifications group securities by characteristics, but they do not require a return premium, a construction method, or a cross-sectional test. The practical difference shows up in evaluation: a factor can be tested for whether it delivered its premium, while a style can only be described. The record should state which of the two it is holding.

Primary sources: <https://www.nber.org/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Record the characteristic, how it is measured, and the universe it applies to.
- Record the evidence for a premium: samples, periods, and whether they overlap.
- Record whether the claim is a factor (testable) or a style (descriptive).
- Record the mechanism offered, if any, and whether it is testable.
- Record the definition's stability: does the characteristic require restatement of past data.
- Record what would falsify the premium, beyond a bad year.

## Procedure

1. Define the characteristic precisely.
2. Record its measurement and the universe.
3. Collect the evidence and its sample structure.
4. Classify the claim as factor or style.
5. Record the mechanism and its testability.
6. Write the definition with its falsifier.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a style label as a factor.
- Accepting a premium from one sample without overlap analysis.
- Ignoring restatement and point-in-time data issues.
- Describing a mechanism that cannot be tested.
- Falsifying only with a bad year rather than a stated condition.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Characteristic and measurement recorded.
- [ ] Universe recorded.
- [ ] Evidence and sample structure recorded.
- [ ] Factor or style classified.
- [ ] Mechanism recorded.
- [ ] Falsifier stated.

The other distinction worth recording is between a risk premium and a mispricing: a premium that exists because holders are compensated for bearing an uncomfortable exposure behaves differently from one that exists because prices are wrong, and the first tends to survive publication while the second tends to disappear as capital arrives. Which explanation is being assumed determines what should be expected after the factor becomes widely known.
