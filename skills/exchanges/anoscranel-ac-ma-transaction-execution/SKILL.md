# M&A transaction execution

## Overview

A merger is a negotiation wrapped in arithmetic, and the arithmetic decides most of the negotiation. What the buyer can pay in stock without diluting itself, what the allocation of purchase price says about future earnings, how much of the price is protected by working capital adjustments and how much is at risk in indemnities — each of these is a modelling problem before it is a legal one. The lawyers paper the structure the model justified.

This skill covers the execution mechanics of a transaction after the strategic decision: consideration structure and its arithmetic, the accretion/dilution test, the post-closing purchase price allocation, the reps and warranties architecture that allocates risk, the working capital peg and closing adjustment, the regulatory clearance sequence, and the deal record. The decision to acquire belongs to strategy and is not made here; trading the spread belongs to a trading skill.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://www.law.cornell.edu/>.

## When to use

- When modelling or evaluating a proposed merger or acquisition.
- When structuring consideration, preparing closing adjustments, or drafting reps and warranties.
- When regulatory clearance sequencing affects deal timing or feasibility.
- When a buyer's post-closing accounting must reflect the deal.

## When NOT to use

- For trading the merger spread and break risk (use ac-special-situations).
- For primary equity issuance to the market (use ac-equity-capital-markets).
- For the credit or equity assessment of the combined company (use ac-bank-and-credit-analysis).

## Status and honest limits

Deal terms are jurisdictional and the accounting rules differ across regimes; this skill states the mechanics and names the questions rather than giving legal advice. Regulatory outcomes and financing markets change; every assumption in a deal model carries its date and is re-validated at signing
and again at closing. Nothing here recommends a transaction or judges a price; a well-executed
deal at a bad price remains a bad deal.
