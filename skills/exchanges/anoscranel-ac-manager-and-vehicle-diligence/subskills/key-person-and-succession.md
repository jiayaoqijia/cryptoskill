# Key person and succession

## Standard

A key-person clause suspends or alters the manager's rights if a named individual ceases to be involved. It is the allocator's protection against the concentration of the strategy in one person, and its value depends on how the clause defines departure, what it triggers, and whether the trigger is proportionate to the loss.

The clause is also an admission. A fund that names two people as key persons has told you where its process lives, and the succession arrangements show whether that dependence has been addressed or merely documented. A clause that pauses new investment but leaves the portfolio to be managed by whoever remains is a different protection from one that triggers a wind-down or a fee reduction.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the named key persons and the definition of their departure.
- Record what the clause triggers: a pause, a wind-down, a fee change, or a notice.
- Record whether the trigger is automatic or subject to a vote of investors.
- Record the succession plan and whether a successor has been identified.
- Record the ownership and economics of any successor, since incentives determine retention.
- Record the notification obligation and its timing.

## Procedure

1. Record the named persons and the departure definition.
2. Record the trigger and its consequences.
3. Record whether it is automatic.
4. Record the succession arrangements.
5. Record successor ownership and economics.
6. Write the clause assessment.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading the clause as a guarantee of continuity.
- Ignoring the trigger's proportionality.
- Assuming a named successor is a retained successor.
- Overlooking the notification timing.
- Treating the clause as covering departure by all senior staff.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Named persons and definition recorded.
- [ ] Trigger and consequences recorded.
- [ ] Automatic status recorded.
- [ ] Succession recorded.
- [ ] Incentives recorded.
- [ ] Assessment written.

The clause's value also depends on what it does to fees: a trigger that pauses new investment but leaves the management fee accruing transfers the cost of the disruption to the allocator, while one that reduces the fee during the pause shares it. Recording the fee consequence alongside the trigger converts a governance provision into an economic term.

A further test is whether the named persons know they are named: a clause listing individuals who have not agreed to the restriction, or who have already signalled an intention to leave, is a clause that protects the form rather than the substance, and the question is answerable in a meeting even when the document does not answer it.
