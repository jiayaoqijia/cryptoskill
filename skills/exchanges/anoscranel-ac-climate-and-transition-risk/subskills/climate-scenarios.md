# Climate scenarios

## Standard

A climate scenario is a conditional path: given this level of policy stringency, this technology cost curve, and this carbon price, here is what happens to a portfolio or an asset. It is not a forecast and it carries no probability. The common scenarios — an orderly transition, a disorderly one, and a hot-house world — differ in the timing of the policy and the physical damage, and those differences change which assets lose.

Reading one means reading its assumptions. The carbon price path, the technology cost assumptions, the discount rate, and the demand elasticity determine the answer, and a scenario that assumes a smooth carbon price is describing a world in which the transition has no political friction. Comparing a portfolio's loss under two scenarios without comparing their assumptions compares two different models rather than two futures.

Primary sources: <https://www.bis.org/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the scenario's source, its policy assumptions, and its stated horizon.
- Record the carbon price path used and its starting level.
- Record the technology assumptions, particularly renewable and storage costs.
- Record the physical damage assumptions and their source.
- Record the discount rate applied.
- Treat the output as a conditional path, and never assign it a probability.

## Procedure

1. Record the scenario source and vintage.
2. Record policy and carbon price assumptions.
3. Record technology and damage assumptions.
4. Apply the scenario to the exposure and record the loss.
5. Compare assumptions with an alternative scenario.
6. Write the analysis with the assumptions foregrounded.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a scenario output as a forecast.
- Averaging two scenarios into an expected value.
- Comparing losses across scenarios with different assumptions.
- Using a smooth carbon path without noting the political assumption.
- Applying a global scenario to a local asset without translation.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Scenario source and horizon recorded.
- [ ] Carbon price path recorded.
- [ ] Technology assumptions recorded.
- [ ] Damage assumptions recorded.
- [ ] Loss computed and conditional status stated.
- [ ] Comparison made on assumptions.

A useful discipline is to ask what the scenario would have to assume for the exposure to be harmless: if the answer requires a carbon price far below any currently legislated level, the scenario is describing a world in which nothing changes, and its output measures the analyst's optimism rather than the asset's risk. Running that inverse case alongside the forward one is what turns a scenario into a test.
