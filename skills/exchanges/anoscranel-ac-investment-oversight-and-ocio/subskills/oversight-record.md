# Oversight record

## Standard

The oversight record demonstrates that the function operated: what was monitored, what was found, what was decided, and what followed. It is written for a reader who was not present and who may need to establish, later, that the delegator did its job. Minutes that record attendance and topics do not satisfy that test; a record of decisions and their follow-through does.

Its second purpose is continuity. Oversight is exercised by people who change, and the record is what allows a successor to see what was already questioned, what was accepted, and what remains open. A record that resets with each change of personnel is a record that has to be rebuilt every few years, and the questions already answered get asked again.

Primary sources: <https://www.cfainstitute.org/> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- Record each monitoring activity, its date, and its result.
- Record each decision, the reasoning, and the responsible party.
- Record each breach with its cause, remedy, and closure.
- Record the open items and their owners.
- Record the evidence held for each decision.
- Record the review cycle and the next scheduled test of each control.

## Procedure

1. Record the monitoring calendar and its results.
2. Record decisions with reasoning and ownership.
3. Record breaches and their closure.
4. Record open items with dates.
5. Record evidence references.
6. Review the record at each cycle and carry forward the open items.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording topics discussed rather than decisions made.
- Leaving actions without owners or dates.
- Closing a breach without evidence of remedy.
- Restarting the record when personnel change.
- Keeping the evidence separate and unreferenced.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Monitoring activities and results recorded.
- [ ] Decisions with reasoning recorded.
- [ ] Breaches with closure recorded.
- [ ] Open items owned and dated.
- [ ] Evidence referenced.
- [ ] Review cycle recorded.

The open-items register is the part that survives a change of personnel best: a dated list of what was asked for and what remains outstanding tells a successor exactly where the previous committee had got to, which is precisely the knowledge that otherwise walks out of the room with the people who held it.

The record should also carry the questions that were asked and answered, not only the decisions taken: a committee that asked why a holding was added and received a satisfactory answer has performed oversight, and the absence of that exchange in the record is what makes a later reviewer conclude that nobody looked.
