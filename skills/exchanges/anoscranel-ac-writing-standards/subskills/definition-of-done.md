# Definition of Done

## Standard

⚠️ The primary standard is paywalled; these points come from secondary sources and the public summary.

The lifecycle frame first: the editorial sequence, in order, and which passes may change what a document says. Then one line per stage: the conditions under which that stage counts as done. The conditions are generic; each document's craft work decides how to meet them.

The sequence: brief → research and sources → outline → draft → structural revision → line edit → copyedit → proofread → publish.

Two passes change meaning: structural revision (order, cuts, re-argued claims) and line editing (the sentence as written). Two do not: the copyedit (consistency against the style sheet) and the proofread (errors in the final artifact). A pass may never widen beyond its layer: a copyedit that re-argues the argument has broken the frame.

- **Brief**: purpose, reader, venue rules and success test are recorded; claims the brief cannot support are marked as research tasks.
- **Research**: a source log exists; every load-bearing claim maps to a judged source or is marked unsupported.
- **Outline**: the order answers first; headings carry the answer; the reader's first three questions are answered by the first sections.
- **Draft**: every outline section is filled; no gap is left silent.
- **Structural revision**: the heading list alone tells the story; every claim has grounds; every unsupported ground is a cut, recorded.
- **Line edit**: sentences are tightened with meaning and qualifiers held fixed; verdict shifts are flagged, never silent.
- **Copyedit**: the project style sheet is applied; every settled decision is recorded; one live ruling per point.
- **Proofread**: the pass runs on the final artifact, in a changed medium; two independent passes are counted and the residual estimated; the stop rule is the agreed budget, not perfection.
- **Publish gate**: no placeholder left, every copyedit query closed, names-dates-numbers verified against the source log, clearance done, human approval recorded.

## Decision rules

The sequence: brief → research and sources → outline → draft → structural revision → line edit → copyedit → proofread → publish. Two passes change meaning (structural revision, line edit); two do not (copyedit, proofread). A pass may never widen beyond its layer: a copyedit that re-argues the argument has broken the frame. Every clause of a stage's line must be true before that stage counts as done.

The agent prepares the text and the gate list. A human publishes, sends or submits; nothing ships without that human act.

## Procedure

1. Name the stage. 2. Read its line. 3. Check each clause against the document. 4. Report gaps as the remaining work.

## Anti-patterns

- Letting a pass widen beyond its layer (a copyedit that changes meaning).
- Treating a partially-met line as done.

## Done checklist

- [ ] The stage's line was read in full and each clause answered true or false.
- [ ] The publish gate recorded human approval.
