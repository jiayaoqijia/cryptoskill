# Private credit marks

## Standard

A private loan does not trade, so its value is an estimate. The manager produces the mark, using comparable transactions, a model, or a valuation agent, and an auditor tests the process rather than reproducing the price. That structure means the reported net asset value is a considered opinion, and the volatility it displays is understated by construction because the inputs are smoothed.

The practical consequences are three. Correlations between private credit and public markets appear lower than they are, because the private marks lag. Reported drawdowns are shallower, because a mark is not a sale. And the exit assumption — that the loan will repay at par — is doing much of the work, which is why a fund with a high proportion of PIK and a rising proportion of amendments deserves a closer look at its valuation policy.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.federalreserve.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the valuation policy and who produces the mark.
- Record the proportion of the book priced by an independent agent.
- Record the proportion of PIK and of amended loans.
- Record the mark dispersion across managers holding similar credits.
- Record the fund's stated exit assumption and whether it has been revised.
- Record the discount at which any secondary transaction in the fund's interests has cleared.

## Procedure

1. Read the valuation policy in the fund's documents.
2. Record who marks, and with what inputs.
3. Record PIK and amendment proportions.
4. Compare marks across managers for similar exposures.
5. Record the exit assumption and any revision.
6. Write the valuation assessment with its limits.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating net asset value as a traded price.
- Comparing private credit volatility with public credit volatility directly.
- Ignoring a rising proportion of PIK and amendments.
- Assuming an independent agent removes the manager's discretion.
- Treating audited as equivalent to market-verified.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Valuation policy recorded.
- [ ] Mark producer recorded.
- [ ] PIK and amendment proportions recorded.
- [ ] Cross-manager comparison made.
- [ ] Exit assumption recorded.
- [ ] Assessment written with limits.

The most informative comparison is the mark against the price at which any part of the same credit has actually changed hands: a secondary trade in a fund's interest, a co-investor's sale, or a new issue of the same borrower's debt all provide a market observation that the fund's own mark can be tested against, and a persistent gap between the two is the finding.
