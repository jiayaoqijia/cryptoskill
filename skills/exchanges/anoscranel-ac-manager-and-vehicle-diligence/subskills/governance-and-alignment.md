# Governance and alignment

## Standard

Alignment is usually asserted through two facts: the manager has its own money in the fund, and the investors have rights to remove it. Both are worth quantifying. A general partner commitment of one percent of fund size is a different statement from one of five, and a commitment funded by the fees the manager receives is different again, because it is the investors' money returning to the fund.

The investor rights are the other half. The ability to remove the manager for cause, to replace it without cause by a supermajority, and to sit on a limited partner advisory committee with real information rights are the mechanisms through which governance operates between fundraising cycles. A fund with a large advisory committee and no removal right has given its investors a forum rather than a power.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the general partner commitment as a percentage of fund size and its funding source.
- Record the removal provisions: for cause, without cause, and the thresholds.
- Record the advisory committee's composition, rights, and access to information.
- Record the manager's ownership and whether key staff hold equity in the business.
- Record any related-party transactions or affiliated service providers.
- Record the reporting obligations and their frequency.

## Procedure

1. Record the commitment and its source.
2. Record removal provisions and thresholds.
3. Record the advisory committee's powers.
4. Record ownership and staff equity.
5. Record related-party arrangements.
6. Write the alignment assessment.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a stated commitment as a funded one.
- Ignoring that fee-funded commitments recycle investor money.
- Assuming an advisory committee has removal power.
- Overlooking affiliated service providers that earn fees.
- Reading alignment from the marketing rather than the documents.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Commitment and source recorded.
- [ ] Removal provisions recorded.
- [ ] Advisory committee powers recorded.
- [ ] Ownership recorded.
- [ ] Related-party arrangements recorded.
- [ ] Assessment written.

The removal right deserves particular attention because it is the only power that matters in the situation it exists for: a clause that permits removal for cause but defines cause narrowly, or requires a supermajority that no dispersed investor base can assemble, provides a remedy in theory and none in practice. Reading the threshold against the actual investor register is what tests whether the right is exercisable.
