# Equity red flags

## Standard

Accounting problems announce themselves through a small set of patterns that are cheap to check. Revenue that grows faster than the cash collecting it, receivables and inventory drifting upward against sales, related-party transactions that move value between entities, frequent changes of auditor or chief financial officer, and complex structures that exist to keep liabilities off the balance sheet.

The flags are not proofs of fraud. They are conditions under which the reported numbers stop being a reliable basis for valuation, which means the research either resolves them or records that it could not. A flag that is explained is a note; a flag that is ignored becomes a loss.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Run the checklist before valuation, never after; a valuation built on unreliable numbers is wasted work.
- Receivables and inventory growth are compared with revenue growth over at least three years.
- Any related-party transaction is recorded with the parties and the amounts.
- Auditor or finance-leadership changes are recorded with the stated reason and the date.
- Restatements are always material; record what changed and why.
- Unresolved flags cap the confidence of every downstream conclusion.

## Procedure

1. Compare receivables and inventory growth with revenue growth across three years.
2. Read the revenue-recognition policy and any change to it.
3. List related-party transactions and their amounts.
4. Check auditor tenure, changes, and any qualifications.
5. Check for restatements and their cause.
6. Record each flag, its evidence, and whether it was resolved.

## Anti-patterns

- Running the checklist after the valuation is written.
- Accepting management's explanation without a document.
- Treating a small related-party amount as immaterial without context.
- Ignoring an auditor change because the new one is well known.
- Proceeding with a thesis because the flags are "probably nothing".

## Done checklist

- [ ] Receivables and inventory versus revenue compared over three years.
- [ ] Revenue-recognition policy and changes read.
- [ ] Related-party transactions listed with amounts.
- [ ] Auditor tenure, changes, and qualifications recorded.
- [ ] Restatements checked and recorded.
- [ ] Each flag marked resolved or unresolved.

## Sources

- Full-text filing search for disclosures and restatements: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/>
- Investor education on fraud warning signs: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>

The checklist is cheap and the cost of skipping it is asymmetric: a resolved flag costs an hour, an unresolved one can cost the position. Run it early, record the answers, and keep the record even when every answer is clean.
