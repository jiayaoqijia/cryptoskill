# Narrative Spine

## Standard

- **Minto Pyramid Principle**: state the answer first, then group the supporting arguments beneath it. SCQA (Situation, Complication, Question, Answer) builds the opening. <https://www.barbaraminto.com/>
- **Duarte, one big idea**: a deck carries one storyline. Extra plotlines break it. <https://www.duarte.com/slideology/>
- **Alley, assertions as skeleton**: section titles and headlines state claims, so the spine reads as an argument when skimmed. <https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-1-4419-8279-7>
- **Reynolds, story before slides**: arrange the story on paper before any layout exists. <https://www.presentationzen.com/>

## Decision rules

1. If the audience already knows the situation, compress Situation and Complication to one line each. Most exec decks qualify.
2. If two pitches must live in one deck, split into two decks. One story per deck is absolute.
3. If a section heading is a noun label ("Market"), rewrite it as a claim ("Our segment grew while the market shrank").
4. If a section only informs and does not advance the answer, it moves to the appendix.
5. The Complication must be real. A manufactured tension reads as theatre to an executive.
6. Order sections by the decider's questions, not by the chronology of the work.
7. For a skeptical room, lead the support with the strongest claim. For a hostile room, surface the objection early.

## Procedure

1. Write the Answer sentence first: the one recommendation.
2. Draft four SCQA sentences in the outline before any slide exists.
3. Split the Answer into two to five supporting claims. Each becomes a section.
4. Write each section heading as a full-sentence claim.
5. Sequence the sections in the decider's question order from the brief.
6. Skim the headings only. They must read as a complete argument.
7. Tag every planned slide under its section claim. A slide that fits none is cut or appendix.
8. Read the opening SCQA aloud. Fix any sentence that sounds invented.

## Anti-patterns

- An agenda slide standing in for a spine. An agenda is navigation, not argument.
- A mystery-reveal structure for a business reader. Open with the Answer, then support it.
- Parallel plotlines ("both options, you decide") in a deck whose job is one decision.
- Noun-label sections that hide the claim ("Financials", "Roadmap").
- Chronology as structure when the decider needs causality.
- A spine longer than five sections. Group harder.

## Done checklist

- [ ] The Answer sentence exists and appears on slide two or earlier.
- [ ] SCQA drafted in four sentences before slides were outlined.
- [ ] Every section heading is a full-sentence claim.
- [ ] The headings-only skim reads as a complete argument.
- [ ] Exactly one storyline. No orphan sections.
- [ ] Orphan slides cut or moved to the appendix.
