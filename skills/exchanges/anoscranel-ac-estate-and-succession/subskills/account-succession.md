# Account succession

## Standard

Different accounts pass by different mechanisms, and each has its own paperwork. A retirement account passes by beneficiary designation; a taxable brokerage account passes by title, transfer-on-death provision, or probate; a bank account may pass by payable-on-death designation. The successor's first problem is not the money but the access: without the right documents, an account can be visible and untouchable for months.

The record therefore documents the mechanism, the documents required, and the responsible institution for each account. It is the difference between an heir who knows what to do and one who discovers the steps one at a time.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.irs.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Each account is recorded with its type, institution, and passing mechanism.
- The documents required for succession are listed per account.
- Contact details and the correct department are recorded, not just a general number.
- Accounts with special rules — retirement, joint, trust-held — are flagged.
- The expected timeline is recorded where known.
- Where the mechanism is unclear, that is recorded as a question for a professional.

## Procedure

1. List accounts with type, institution, and mechanism.
2. Record the documents each institution requires.
3. Record contacts and departments.
4. Flag accounts with special rules.
5. Note expected timelines.
6. Record the open questions.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming every account follows the will.
- Recording a general call-centre number instead of the estate department.
- Missing a small account that will still require paperwork.
- Ignoring the timeline difference between mechanisms.
- Leaving unclear mechanisms unrecorded.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Accounts listed with mechanisms.
- [ ] Required documents recorded per account.
- [ ] Contacts and departments recorded.
- [ ] Special-rule accounts flagged.
- [ ] Timelines noted.
- [ ] Open questions listed.

The step that catches most successors is proving authority: an institution will not discuss an account with someone whose paperwork is incomplete, and the paperwork differs by mechanism. Recording what each institution requires, in advance, converts a chain of discoveries into a sequence of calls.

A single page listing each account with its mechanism, its documents, and its contact turns a family's worst week into a sequence of ordinary clerical tasks, which is the most any plan can promise.
