# Treasury policy record

## Standard

The treasury policy record states the limits, the authority, the permitted instruments, and the review triggers, and it exists so that a decision taken in a stress can be checked against a rule written in calm. That is its whole function: a treasury that improvises during a funding squeeze has no policy, whatever the document says.

The record's second purpose is to make the policy comparable across companies. Two firms with the same cash balance can have very different liquidity positions depending on what the policy permits and how the limits are set, and a record that names the permitted instruments, the maximum maturity, and the counterparty floors allows that comparison to be made on the terms that matter rather than on the balance sheet total.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the liquidity target and the basis for its size.
- The permitted instruments are listed with maximum maturity and minimum rating.
- Counterparty limits are recorded per institution and in aggregate.
- The hedging programme's scope, proportion, and horizon are recorded.
- Authority levels are recorded: who may approve what, and at what size.
- Review triggers are dated: rating change, covenant approach, market dislocation, annual review.

## Procedure

1. Record the liquidity target and its basis.
2. Record permitted instruments and their constraints.
3. Record counterparty limits.
4. Record the hedging programme's parameters.
5. Record authority levels.
6. Set review triggers and amend at each.

## Anti-patterns

- A policy that states principles without limits.
- Permitting instruments without a maturity or rating constraint.
- Recording counterparty limits without an aggregate sector limit.
- Leaving authority levels vague, so any approval is defensible.
- No trigger for a rating change, which is when the policy matters most.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Liquidity target and basis recorded.
- [ ] Instruments and constraints recorded.
- [ ] Counterparty limits recorded.
- [ ] Hedging parameters recorded.
- [ ] Authority levels recorded.
- [ ] Triggers dated.

The record should state what the policy does not permit, since that is where the discipline lives: a policy that lists permitted instruments without listing prohibited ones leaves the boundary to be argued at the moment of pressure, and the argument will be won by whoever wants the higher yield.
