# Sponsor dynamics

## Standard

The sponsor owns the equity and controls the borrower's decisions, and its interests align with the lender's only while the equity has value. When the enterprise deteriorates and the equity is out of the money, the sponsor's rational move is to take risk with the lender's money: incur permitted debt, sell assets inside the baskets, or restructure the liability through a transaction that disadvantages one part of the lender group.

Reading an agreement with that incentive in mind is what turns a covenant list into a risk assessment. The permitted baskets, the asset sale provisions, the ability to move collateral to an unrestricted subsidiary, and the voting thresholds that let a majority of lenders bind a minority are all mechanisms through which a determined borrower can change the lender's position without defaulting.

Primary sources: <https://www.federalreserve.gov/> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the sponsor's ownership, its fund's life, and its exit horizon.
- Record the mechanisms that move value away from lenders: baskets, unrestricted subsidiaries, and asset sales.
- Record the voting thresholds that bind dissenting lenders.
- Record where the sponsor's equity sits relative to the debt in a downside case.
- Record any history of sponsor behaviour in prior restructurings.
- Record the lender's ability to block the mechanisms, and the cost of exercising it.

## Procedure

1. Record sponsor identity, ownership, and fund horizon.
2. Map the value-transfer mechanisms in the agreement.
3. Record voting and blocking thresholds.
4. Estimate the equity's position in a downside case.
5. Record enforcement options and their cost.
6. Write the incentive analysis.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming the sponsor's interests are aligned while equity has value.
- Ignoring unrestricted subsidiary provisions.
- Overlooking the voting thresholds that let a majority act.
- Treating a cooperative relationship as a contractual protection.
- Ignoring the sponsor's fund life, which creates pressure to act.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Sponsor and horizon recorded.
- [ ] Value-transfer mechanisms mapped.
- [ ] Thresholds recorded.
- [ ] Downside equity position estimated.
- [ ] Enforcement options recorded.
- [ ] Analysis written.

The fund's own life creates a timetable that the lender should record: a sponsor nearing the end of its fund term has pressure to exit or to extend, and that pressure can arrive as a sale, a dividend recapitalisation, or a restructuring proposal. Knowing which window the sponsor is in is part of reading the credit, and it is information the lender usually has.
