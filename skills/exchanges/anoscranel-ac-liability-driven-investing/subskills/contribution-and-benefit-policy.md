# Contribution and benefit policy

## Standard

The liability is not a fixed fact. Contribution policy determines how quickly a deficit is funded, and benefit design determines what the obligation is: an indexation promise, an early-retirement provision, or a longevity guarantee each changes the cash-flow stream and its sensitivity to assumptions. A funding ratio computed on the current benefit schedule is a measurement of today's promise, not of a promise that can be renegotiated.

The interaction matters for the investment side because it changes the horizon. A scheme with a large deficit and a fixed contribution schedule has a short effective horizon, since contributions cannot rise to cover a shortfall. A scheme with a flexible contribution policy can absorb volatility over a longer period, and the same portfolio is therefore appropriate for one and not the other.

Primary sources: <https://www.cfainstitute.org/> and <https://www.federalreserve.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the benefit formula and the indexation provisions.
- Record the contribution schedule and any flexibility in it.
- Record the deficit and the period over which it is intended to close.
- Record the assumptions behind the benefit projection.
- Record any provision that allows benefits to be changed and the conditions.
- Record the effect of a change in longevity assumption on the liability.

## Procedure

1. Record the benefit formula and indexation.
2. Record the contribution schedule and flexibility.
3. Record the deficit and its intended closure period.
4. Record the projection assumptions.
5. Record changeable provisions and their conditions.
6. Write the policy summary with its horizon.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating the benefit promise as immutable when it contains a change provision.
- Ignoring contribution flexibility when setting the investment horizon.
- Assuming the deficit closes on schedule without contributions.
- Overlooking indexation as a source of liability duration.
- Treating a longevity assumption as fixed.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Benefit formula recorded.
- [ ] Contribution schedule recorded.
- [ ] Deficit and closure period recorded.
- [ ] Assumptions recorded.
- [ ] Changeable provisions recorded.
- [ ] Horizon stated.

The provision that changes the arithmetic most is indexation: a promise to increase benefits in line with inflation makes the liability sensitive to real rates rather than nominal ones, which means the correct hedge is inflation-linked rather than nominal, and a scheme that hedges with nominal bonds leaves the real exposure open even when its nominal duration looks matched.
