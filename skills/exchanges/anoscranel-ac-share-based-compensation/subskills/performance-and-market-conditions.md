# Performance and market conditions

## Standard

Vesting conditions split into two kinds, and the split matters because the accounting treats them differently. A performance condition depends on the company's own results — a revenue target, an earnings threshold, a return measure — and the expense is recognised when vesting becomes probable. A market condition depends on the share price or on total shareholder return relative to a peer group, and its cost is recognised from the grant date regardless of whether it ultimately vests.

The distinction has a practical consequence for the reader. A grant with a demanding market condition can be expensed in full while never vesting, which means the reported expense overstates the eventual share issuance; a grant with a performance condition can be expensed only when the target looks achievable, which means the expense can arrive late and in a lump. Recording which kind applies is what makes the forward supply interpretable.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.fasb.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record whether each material grant carries a performance or a market condition.
- Record the specific metric and the threshold for a performance condition.
- Record the peer group and the measurement period for a market condition.
- Record the probability assumed and whether it has changed.
- Record the expense recognised to date and the unrecognised balance.
- Record the maximum shares that could vest, which is the supply at full achievement.

## Procedure

1. Read the compensation note for the condition types.
2. Record the metric, threshold, and measurement period.
3. Record the probability assumption.
4. Record the expense recognised and the remaining balance.
5. Record the maximum vesting quantity.
6. Write the summary with the supply at each outcome stated.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a market condition as certain because it is being expensed.
- Assuming a performance condition will vest because the target was met once.
- Ignoring the measurement period, which can extend for years.
- Overlooking the maximum vesting quantity, which is the dilution ceiling.
- Comparing companies with different condition mixes on expense alone.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Condition types recorded per material grant.
- [ ] Metrics and thresholds recorded.
- [ ] Peer group and period recorded.
- [ ] Probability assumption recorded.
- [ ] Expense and balance recorded.
- [ ] Maximum vesting quantity recorded.
