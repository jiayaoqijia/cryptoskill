# Deal structure and consideration

## Standard

Consideration is what changes hands, and its form decides who bears which risk until closing. Cash fixes the price at signing: the seller is whole and the buyer holds all post-signing value change. Stock makes the sellers co-investors in the combination: price moves between signing and closing for both sides, and the exchange ratio is the number that allocates that movement. A collar can bound the ratio; a fixed ratio transfers all market risk to the seller.

Cash costs financing certainty and is limited by what the buyer can fund. Stock costs dilution and requires the buyer's own shareholders to accept the valuation being paid — paying in stock when you believe your shares are undervalued transfers that undervaluation to the sellers. Mixed consideration lets each side choose, and because elections are typically prorated when oversubscribed, the election outcome itself reveals what informed parties think of the relative paper.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://www.law.cornell.edu/>.

## Decision rules

- Record consideration mix and the exchange ratio mechanics for stock deals, including any collar and its bounds.
- For cash, record the financing sources and their conditions precedent.
- For elections, record the proration mechanics and the deadline behavior.
- State which side bears market risk between signing and closing under the chosen structure.
- Record how the structure interacts with deal protection: break-up fees, no-shop clauses.
- Note that the buyer's stock issuance may require shareholder approval and its own registration process.

## Procedure

1. Establish the buyer's funding capacity and the seller's risk preference.
2. Model the consequences of each structure: fully fixed, fully floating, mixed.
3. For stock, obtain the exchange ratio and any collar terms from the merger agreement.
4. For elections, obtain the election and proration mechanics.
5. Record the break-up fee and any other deal protection devices.
6. Write the structure summary with the risk allocation stated plainly.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating fixed exchange ratio as fixed value.
- Assuming cash capacity without the financing conditions.
- Ignoring the dilution implications of stock consideration.
- Treating elections as standard without analysing proration.
- Overlooking shareholder-approval requirements on large stock issuances.
