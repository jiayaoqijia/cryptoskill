# Thirteen-D and amendments

## Standard

A 13D is filed when a holder acquires more than five percent of a class with a purpose of influencing control, and it must be amended promptly when material facts change. The filing discloses the position, the source of funds, the purpose of the transaction, and any plans or proposals. That last item is the reason the form matters: it is one of the few public statements of intent in the equity market.

The amendments carry the signal. An increase in position, a change in purpose from passive to active, a new agreement with other holders, or a reduction below the threshold each triggers an amendment, and the sequence tells a story that no single filing does. A holder that files a 13D and then amends to describe discussions with the company has moved from accumulation to negotiation, and the market reads that transition.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the filing date, the position size, and the date of the triggering acquisition.
- Record the stated purpose and any plans or proposals item.
- Record the source of funds where disclosed.
- Record each amendment, its date, and what changed.
- Distinguish a 13D from a 13G and record any switch between them.
- Record any group formation or agreement with other holders.

## Procedure

1. Read the original filing and record the position and purpose.
2. Record the plans and proposals item verbatim where it matters.
3. Read each amendment and record the change.
4. Record the sequence as a timeline.
5. Note any switch between 13D and 13G.
6. Write the filing summary with the timeline.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading only the latest amendment and missing the sequence.
- Treating a stated purpose as a binding plan.
- Ignoring a switch to passive filing, which may reflect a change of approach.
- Missing group formation with other holders.
- Assuming the position disclosed is the position held today.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Original filing recorded.
- [ ] Purpose and plans recorded.
- [ ] Amendments recorded with dates.
- [ ] Timeline built.
- [ ] Filing type changes recorded.
- [ ] Summary written.

The timing of a filing relative to the position's accumulation also carries information: a filer that crosses the threshold and discloses within days is behaving conventionally, while one that discloses later relative to the acquisition dates may be signaling a dispute over the requirement. Either way the filing dates and the acquisition dates belong side by side in the record, because the gap between them is a fact rather than an inference.
