# Tender and odd lot

## Standard

A tender offer invites holders to sell at a stated price, usually above the market, and the terms decide how much of each holder's submission is accepted. A fixed-price tender for a limited number of shares prorates: if holders tender more than the company will buy, each submission is accepted in proportion. An odd-lot provision, where present, gives small holders priority so that their entire submission is accepted before proration applies.

The arithmetic of a prorated tender is a weighted outcome. A holder who tenders everything receives the offer price on the accepted portion and keeps the rest, whose price after the offer reflects the reduced float. The odd-lot provision changes the calculation for holders below the threshold, which is why the record should state the holder's size against that threshold rather than treating the offer as uniform.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the offer price, the maximum shares, and the expiration date.
- Record whether proration applies and any odd-lot priority provision.
- Record the odd-lot threshold and the holder's position relative to it.
- Record the withdrawal rights and their deadline.
- Record the expected acceptance ratio and what it implies for the holder.
- Record the post-offer expectations for the unaccepted shares.

## Procedure

1. Record the offer terms.
2. Determine proration and odd-lot provisions.
3. Compare the position with the threshold.
4. Record withdrawal rights and deadlines.
5. Compute the weighted outcome under a range of acceptance ratios.
6. Write the summary with the weighted result.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming a full submission is fully accepted.
- Ignoring an odd-lot provision that applies to the position.
- Forgetting withdrawal rights before the deadline.
- Treating the offer price as the outcome for the whole position.
- Overlooking the price of the retained shares after the offer.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Offer terms recorded.
- [ ] Proration and odd-lot recorded.
- [ ] Threshold compared with position.
- [ ] Withdrawal rights recorded.
- [ ] Weighted outcome computed.
- [ ] Retained-share expectation recorded.

The record should also state whether the offer is for all shares or a fixed maximum, because a fixed maximum with proration turns the decision into a question about the expected acceptance ratio, while an any-and-all offer makes the decision a simple comparison between the offer price and the holder's view of value.
