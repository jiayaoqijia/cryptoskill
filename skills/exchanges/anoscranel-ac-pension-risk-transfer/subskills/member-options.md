# Member options

## Standard

Members of a defined-benefit scheme hold options: to take a tax-free cash lump sum instead of part of the pension, to transfer out, or to retire early on actuarially reduced terms. Each option, when exercised, changes the scheme's liability, and the direction of the change is not always the same: a cash commutation can reduce or increase the liability depending on the terms offered, and a transfer-out removes the member and their longevity exposure entirely.

The assumption that matters is take-up. A scheme assuming ten percent of members commuted cash at the last valuation and experiencing thirty percent has a liability different from the one on its books, and the difference emerges slowly and unevenly across the membership. Reading the disclosure means looking for the take-up assumption and comparing it with the scheme's own experience.

Primary sources: <https://www.fca.org.uk/> and <https://www.bis.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record each option available and its terms.
- Record the assumed take-up rate and the basis for it.
- Record the scheme's actual experience against the assumption.
- Record the effect of each option on the liability, in direction and magnitude.
- Record the transfer-value basis and any restriction on exercising it.
- Record the options that would change the insurer's pricing if exercised before a transfer.

## Procedure

1. Read the actuarial valuation for the option assumptions.
2. Record each option and its terms.
3. Record assumed take-up and actual experience.
4. Record the liability effect of each option.
5. Record the transfer-value basis and any restrictions.
6. Write the analysis with the assumption gaps stated.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming take-up matches the last valuation without checking experience.
- Treating all options as liability-reducing.
- Ignoring transfer-out activity, which removes both members and longevity risk.
- Overlooking the interaction between member options and a pending insurer transaction.
- Assuming a transfer-value basis is fixed when it is reviewed periodically.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Options and terms recorded.
- [ ] Assumed take-up recorded.
- [ ] Actual experience recorded.
- [ ] Liability effect per option recorded.
- [ ] Transfer basis recorded.
- [ ] Interaction with transfer recorded.

The interaction with a transfer is worth stating explicitly: members with valuable options tend to exercise them before an insurer prices the scheme, which is why insurers ask for option experience and why a scheme that has not resolved its option terms may find the quote wider than expected. Getting the options settled first is part of the preparation.
