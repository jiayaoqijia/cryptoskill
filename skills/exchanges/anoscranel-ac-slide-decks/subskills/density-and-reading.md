# Density and Reading

## Standard

- **Duarte, glanceability**: a slide is processed in about three seconds or it is a document. <https://www.duarte.com/slideology/>
- **The 10-second skim rule**: a reader flipping at about ten seconds per slide must grasp each claim. ⚠️ practitioner convention; a heuristic, not a standard.
- **Kawasaki, 30-point floor (pitch decks under 10/20/30)**: dense text forces small type, so a hard type floor works as a density tripwire. <https://guykawasaki.com/the_102030_rule/> ⚠️ practitioner convention.
- **Reynolds, signal-to-noise**: remove anything that carries no message, including redundant labels. <https://www.presentationzen.com/>
- **Slides are not documents**: when the deck must be read without a speaker, produce a separate leave-behind version rather than overstuffing the live deck. <https://www.duarte.com/slideology/>

## Decision rules

1. Live deck: a working ceiling of 30 visible words per slide, counting headline, body, and labels. ⚠️ house heuristic, not a canon number.
2. Overflow gets split, never shrunk. If the fix is a smaller font, the fix is wrong.
3. One visual per slide. Multiple charts become a sequence.
4. If the decider must compare numbers, those numbers live on one slide even at high density. Comparability beats purity.
5. Leave-behind: full sentences are allowed, but the headline-assertion rule still binds. Cite sources on the slide.
6. Never ship one file pretending to serve live and read-alone audiences. Ship two files.

## Procedure

1. Count the visible words per slide: headline plus body plus labels.
2. Flag every slide over the budget for its format.
3. Run the skim test: state the claim for each slide aloud in under ten seconds.
4. Split overloaded slides claim by claim (the grammar rules in `subskills/slide-grammar.md` apply).
5. Move comparative detail to an appendix and name the route to it on the slide.
6. Build the leave-behind by writing paragraphs under each headline. Never by shrinking the live deck.
7. Re-count after the split. Density work is done when every slide passes twice.

## Anti-patterns

- Shrinking fonts to fit content. Fix: split the slide.
- One deck serving both the live talk and the lonely reader. Fix: ship the pair.
- Bullets nested three levels deep. Fix: nested bullets are unwritten slides.
- Speaker notes pasted onto the slide. Fix: notes stay in the notes field.
- A table of twelve columns on a live slide. Fix: chart the claim, table the appendix.
- Cutting the numbers the decider must compare, to hit a word budget. Fix: rule four.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Word counts within budget per format.
- [ ] Every slide passes the 10-second skim test.
- [ ] No font shrank to fit content.
- [ ] A leave-behind variant exists when read-alone is expected.
- [ ] Dense comparisons grouped deliberately, not scattered.
- [ ] Sources cited on leave-behind slides.
