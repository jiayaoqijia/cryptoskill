# Collective action clauses

## Standard

A collective action clause lets a specified majority of holders amend the bond's terms and bind the remainder. Without one, a single holder can refuse a restructuring and hold the entire issue for its full claim; with one, a majority can restructure and the minority is bound, which is what makes a negotiated outcome possible at all.

The drafting decides how much a clause achieves. A single-series clause applies to one bond; an aggregation clause pools votes across series, which prevents a debtor from restructuring the easy bonds and leaving the difficult ones; and the threshold — commonly seventy-five percent of principal — determines how many holdouts can be tolerated. The record should state the clause type, the threshold, and whether aggregation applies.

Primary sources: <https://www.imf.org/> and <https://www.bis.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record whether the instrument has a collective action clause.
- Record the amendment threshold as a percentage of principal.
- Record whether the clause aggregates across series, and if so across which pool.
- Record the powers the clause grants: maturity extension, coupon reduction, principal reduction.
- Record any provision requiring uniform treatment across holders.
- Record the governing law, since the clause's operation depends on it.

## Procedure

1. Read the clause from the bond's terms.
2. Record the threshold and the voting mechanism.
3. Record whether aggregation applies and its scope.
4. Record the amendments the clause permits.
5. Record the governing law.
6. Write the clause summary with the holdout space calculated.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming every bond has a clause.
- Reading the threshold without the aggregation scope.
- Ignoring the powers the clause grants, which may be narrower than assumed.
- Overlooking the governing law, which can limit enforcement.
- Treating a clause as preventing holdouts rather than limiting them.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Clause presence recorded.
- [ ] Threshold recorded.
- [ ] Aggregation scope recorded.
- [ ] Permitted amendments recorded.
- [ ] Uniform treatment provision recorded.
- [ ] Governing law recorded.

The threshold's practical meaning is the holdout space it permits: at seventy-five percent, a quarter of the principal can refuse and still be bound, which is a large enough minority to fund a legal challenge and small enough that the debtor can proceed. Reading the threshold as a number rather than as a negotiation position understates what it decides.
