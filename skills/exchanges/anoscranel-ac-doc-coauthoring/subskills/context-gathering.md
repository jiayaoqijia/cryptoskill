# Context Gathering

## Standard

Stage 1 of the co-authoring workflow closes the gap between what the author knows implicitly and what the co-author needs explicitly to build an accurate document. Context gathering replaces unstructured guessing with a systematic interview covering core dimensions: document purpose, target reader, the exact decision or action the document must drive, publishing venue, operating constraints, available source material, and the success test. The standard dictates that all necessary parameters must be captured before any section planning or drafting begins. According to standard technical communication frameworks and ISO 24495-1 guidelines, defining the reader's information needs upfront prevents wasted drafting effort and ensures structural relevance from the first paragraph onward.

## Decision rules

1. Execute context gathering as the mandatory first step of any accepted co-authoring workflow, never skipping directly to outlining or drafting.
2. Limit the initial inquiry to essential dimensions (purpose, reader, decision, constraints, success test) to avoid overwhelming the author with survey fatigue.
3. Stop gathering context as soon as the core decision mandate and reader profile are clear, avoiding endless clarification loops.
4. Capture all gathered answers in a structured workspace artifact or working memory so subsequent stages can reference them directly.
5. If the author provides incomplete context on constraints or venue, make reasonable explicit assumptions and ask for quick confirmation.

## Procedure

1. Present a structured set of targeted questions covering purpose, reader persona, decision trigger, venue, and success test.
2. Review existing source materials or notes provided by the author to extract implicit constraints and background facts.
3. Synthesize the responses into a concise context summary confirming shared understanding before moving to section planning.
4. Check whether any critical parameters remain ambiguous; if so, ask at most one round of clarifying follow-up questions.
5. Transition formally to Stage 2 (section planning) once the context summary is acknowledged and approved by the author.

## Anti-patterns

- Starting to write prose or headings before verifying who the reader is and what decision the document serves.
- Asking an exhaustive list of dozens of trivial questions that exhaust the author before writing begins.
- Ignoring author-provided constraints and reverting to generic template structures.
- Failing to record gathered facts, forcing the agent to re-ask questions in later stages.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Purpose and target reader explicitly defined and recorded.
- [ ] Decision trigger and success test established.
- [ ] Constraints, venue, and available source materials inventoried.
- [ ] Context summary confirmed by the author before proceeding.
