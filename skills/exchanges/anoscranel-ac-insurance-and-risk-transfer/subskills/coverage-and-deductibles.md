# Coverage and deductibles

## Standard

Coverage is a limit, and the limit is the most important number in the policy. A deductible is the portion of a loss retained; raising it lowers the premium and raises the amount the household must fund. The trade-off is therefore not about preference but about arithmetic: the premium saved should be compared with the additional loss retained and the likelihood of facing it.

Limits have a second dimension that is easy to miss: sublimits. A policy may state a generous total limit while capping a category that matters — water damage, valuables, liability for a rented property — well below the headline. Reading the sublimits is part of choosing the limit, not a detail for the claim.

Primary sources: <https://www.naic.org/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Record the limit, the sublimits, and the deductible for every coverage.
- Compare the premium at two deductible levels and record the difference.
- State the reserve available to fund the deductible.
- Check whether limits are indexed or fixed in nominal terms.
- Record any coverage that is required by contract or law.
- Re-quote when the underlying asset value or liability exposure changes.

## Procedure

1. Record the current limits, sublimits, and deductibles.
2. Request quotes at a higher deductible.
3. Compute the premium saving and the additional retained loss.
4. Check the reserve against the deductible.
5. Verify indexing and sublimits.
6. Record the chosen combination with its rationale.

## Anti-patterns

- Choosing a limit without reading the sublimits.
- Raising a deductible without a reserve to fund it.
- Comparing premiums at different limits.
- Ignoring inflation indexing on a long-held policy.
- Assuming the headline limit applies to every category.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Limits, sublimits, and deductibles recorded.
- [ ] Two deductible quotes compared.
- [ ] Reserve against the deductible stated.
- [ ] Indexing checked.
- [ ] Mandatory coverages recorded.
- [ ] Choice and rationale written.

The deductible decision is reversible at renewal and cheap to revisit, which is why it should be revisited: the right deductible changes as the reserve grows and as premiums move, and a figure chosen years ago against a different reserve may now be either too cautious or too exposed.

A useful discipline is to write the deductible as a monthly figure: divided across the policy year, it shows what the retained risk costs in cash terms and makes the premium comparison concrete rather than abstract.
