# Screening and agreement

## Standard

Screening applies the eligibility criteria to titles and abstracts, and the finding that matters is that a single screener misses studies — not from dishonesty but from reading the same borderline record one way on a Tuesday. Dual independent screening exists to make that error visible: two screeners apply the same criteria separately, disagreements are reconciled by discussion or a third party, and the agreement is measured rather than assumed.

The measurement is the statistic that keeps the process honest. Percent agreement overstates concordance because two raters agree by chance on the easy includes; a chance-corrected statistic — kappa or a prevalence-adjusted variant where the included fraction is small — is reported alongside the raw rate. A very low agreement does not invalidate a review, but it does invalidate a claim that the criteria were clear.

Primary sources: <https://training.cochrane.org/handbook> and <https://prisma-statement.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Two screeners review every record independently at title and abstract stage.
- Records excluded by both are recorded and archived; single-exclusions go to a third or to discussion.
- Full-text screening is also dual for included and for borderline records.
- Report both raw percent agreement and a chance-corrected statistic.
- Record every disagreement and its resolution reason, not only the outcome.
- Pilot the criteria on a sample before full screening begins.

## Procedure

1. Pilot the criteria on fifty records with both screeners; reconcile and refine.
2. Screen all titles and abstracts independently in duplicate.
3. Flag disagreements and resolve by discussion or third-party adjudication.
4. Retrieve full texts for all — and only — screen-inclusive records.
5. Screen full texts in duplicate against the same criteria.
6. Compute and report agreement before moving to extraction.

## Anti-patterns

- Single-reviewer screening justified by workload.
- Reporting only percent agreement.
- Resolving disagreements by deferring to seniority rather than the criteria.
- Modifying a criterion mid-screening without a recorded amendment.
- Not archiving the excluded records for later challenge.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Criteria piloted in duplicate on a sample.
- [ ] Title and abstract screening dual and independent.
- [ ] Disagreements logged with reasons.
- [ ] Full-text screening dual.
- [ ] Agreement statistic reported alongside raw agreement.
- [ ] Exclusions archived.

The statistic is often misread as a grade of the screeners or the review. It is neither: it measures the criteria's clarity under realistic use. A team that reports a modest agreement and then shows that disagreements clustered on one specific rule has done better science than a team reporting near-perfect agreement on a criterion so broad that no two readings were possible.
