# Campaign archetypes

## Standard

Campaigns fall into a small number of types, and the type determines both the ask and the path. A capital-return campaign asks for a buyback or a special dividend and can often be satisfied without a vote. An operational campaign asks for cost cuts or a management change and usually requires board influence. A strategic campaign asks for a sale, a break-up, or a merger and depends on finding a counterparty. A governance campaign asks for board change and runs through the ballot.

The type predicts the resolution. Capital-return demands are the easiest to settle because the board can concede with cash; governance demands are the most likely to go to a vote because the concession is power. A campaign that begins with one demand and shifts to another is signalling that its first ask was refused, and the shift itself is information about the negotiation.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the campaign's stated demand and whether it is capital, operational, strategic, or governance.
- Record whether the demand requires a shareholder vote or only a board decision.
- Record the counterparty required for a strategic demand, and whether one exists.
- Record any shift in demand and the date it occurred.
- Record the company's public response and whether it conceded on any element.
- Record the campaign's stated timeline and any deadline it has set.

## Procedure

1. Read the activist's public materials and identify the demand.
2. Classify the campaign by type.
3. Record whether a vote is required.
4. Record the company's response.
5. Note any change of demand.
6. Write the archetype assessment.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating all campaigns as governance fights.
- Assuming a capital-return demand requires a proxy contest.
- Ignoring a demand shift that reveals the negotiation.
- Treating a strategic demand as achievable without a buyer.
- Reading the company's response as its final position.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Demand recorded and classified.
- [ ] Vote requirement recorded.
- [ ] Counterparty requirement recorded.
- [ ] Demand shifts recorded.
- [ ] Company response recorded.
- [ ] Assessment written.

The type also predicts how the market prices the situation: a capital-return campaign is usually priced as a one-off cash event, while a governance campaign is priced as uncertainty about who controls the balance sheet, and a strategic campaign is priced against the probability of a transaction. Recording which of the three the market appears to be pricing is a useful check on whether the campaign's stated demand is the one that matters.
