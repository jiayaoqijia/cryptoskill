# Backtest Integrity and Validation Desk

## Overview

The Backtest Integrity skill provides a rigorous framework for auditing, validating, and stress-testing quantitative trading strategies. In quantitative finance and digital asset markets, apparent predictive power is frequently an artifact of data snooping, multiple testing, look-ahead bias, and unrealistic cost assumptions. This skill establishes an epistemological defense against false discoveries, ensuring that trading strategies deployed for operational consideration possess genuine statistical edge rather than phantom alpha generated by flawed backtest engineering. 

Rooted in academic consensus—including Bailey and López de Prado on the Deflated Sharpe Ratio, Harvey and Liu on multiple testing haircuts, White's Reality Check, and empirical findings on alpha decay (arXiv 2605.16895)—the skill enforces strict pre-registration protocols, robust null hypothesis testing, realistic transaction cost modeling, and definitive binary kill verdicts.

Primary sources for the claims in this section: <https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2014.40.5.094> and <https://doi.org/10.3905/jpm.2015.42.1.013>.

## When to use

- Pre-registering hypothesis specifications, parameter grids, and decision rules prior to data execution.
- Issuing definitive PROCEED or KILL verdicts and enforcing re-registration barriers for failed strategies.

## When NOT to use

- Collecting raw market data, historical price feeds, or building general data-engineering pipelines (use `ac-market-data-collection`).
- Performing forensic post-mortem analysis on historical liquidation crashes or market cascades (use `ac-cascade-forensics`).
- Recording individual live trade executions, position sizing ledgers, or discretionary trading journals (use `ac-crypto-trading-discipline`).
- Deploying live trading algorithms or managing real-time exchange API connections and order routing infrastructure.

## Status and Honest Limits

This skill operates under strict epistemic boundaries. It evaluates quantitative strategies based on published statistical methods, econometric standards, and empirical findings. It does not predict future market returns, guarantee trading profitability, or endorse specific financial assets. All backtest validations are probabilistic assessments subject to model risk, parameter instability, and structural regime shifts.
