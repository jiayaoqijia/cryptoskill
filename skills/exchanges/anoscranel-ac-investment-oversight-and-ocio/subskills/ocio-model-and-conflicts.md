# OCIO model and conflicts

## Standard

An outsourced chief investment officer takes responsibility for the investment function: policy advice, manager selection, implementation, and reporting. The model consolidates decisions that were previously spread across a committee, a consultant, and several managers, which is its attraction and its risk — the oversight that came from having several parties is reduced to the oversight the delegator can apply to one.

The conflicts are structural and should be named rather than assumed away. A provider that offers its own funds has an incentive to allocate to them; one paid on assets has an incentive to grow assets; one that also earns fees from underlying managers has an incentive to use those managers. The record should state each conflict, the policy that addresses it, and the evidence that the policy was applied.

Primary sources: <https://www.cfainstitute.org/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the scope delegated and the scope retained.
- Record the provider's ownership, affiliates, and product range.
- Record every revenue line the provider earns from the relationship, including underlying fees.
- Record the conflicts policy and the specific controls it imposes.
- Record the evidence that controls were applied in specific decisions.
- Record the termination terms and the transition assistance available.

## Procedure

1. Record delegated and retained scope.
2. Record affiliates and proprietary products.
3. Record all revenue lines.
4. Record the conflicts policy and controls.
5. Collect evidence the controls operated.
6. Write the conflict summary with its evidence.

## Anti-patterns

- Accepting a conflicts policy as proof that conflicts are managed.
- Overlooking fees earned by underlying managers that the provider selects.
- Ignoring the transition cost of leaving as a lock-in.
- Treating the retention of policy authority as oversight without testing it.
- Assuming consolidation removes the need for monitoring.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Scope recorded.
- [ ] Affiliates and products recorded.
- [ ] Revenue lines recorded.
- [ ] Controls recorded.
- [ ] Evidence of operation recorded.
- [ ] Transition terms recorded.

The structural point worth recording is that consolidation moves the oversight burden rather than removing it: a committee overseeing five managers has five sets of reports and five relationships to test, while a committee overseeing one provider has a single relationship that must be tested more deeply, because there is no second opinion inside the arrangement.
