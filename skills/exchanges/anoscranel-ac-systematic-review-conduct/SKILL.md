# Systematic review conduct

## Overview

A systematic review differs from a literature summary in one structural way: the method is written before the evidence is seen, and the review's credibility rests on whether that protocol survived contact with the results. The question is framed in advance, the criteria that decide inclusion are fixed before the search runs, and every departure from the plan is an amendment with a reason — not a quiet adjustment. What gets written up afterwards is the residue of what was conducted.

This skill covers the conduct: framing the question, specifying the search and proving its coverage, screening in duplicate, extracting with a piloted form, assessing bias with a named tool rather than a feeling, and keeping the records that make the whole thing auditable. It is deliberately narrow — the writing of the review is a separate skill, and so is the open-corpus hunt of a question that never claimed to be systematic.

Primary sources: <https://training.cochrane.org/handbook> and <https://equator-network.org/>.

## When to use

- When running a systematic or scoping review under a declared protocol.
- When a review's search, screening, or bias assessment must be reproducible.
- When registering a protocol or amending one mid-stream.
- When the review's inclusion decisions will be contested.

## When NOT to use

- For writing the review as a document (use ac-academic-writing).
- For open-ended research hunting with no protocol (use ac-deep-researcher).
- For adversarially attacking an existing corpus (use ac-adversarial-corpus-challenge).

## Status and honest limits

Conduct is what makes a review systematic rather than authoritative-sounding; if the protocol changed after evidence was seen, the record says so. This skill never writes the conclusions and never judges a study's science — risk of bias is about internal validity of the evidence process, not the truth of the finding. A review conducted perfectly can still be wrong if the underlying literature is thin; the record says what was reviewed, never that enough evidence existed.
