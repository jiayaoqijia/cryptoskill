# Protocol registration

## Standard

Registration time-stamps the protocol before any evidence was seen, and that is the whole point. A registered protocol answers the question every skeptical reader eventually asks: did the review decide its scope when the studies were unknown, or once the shape of the results was already visible? The register entry with the date is what makes the answer verifiable instead of persuasive.

Amendment discipline is the hard part. A protocol that can never change is a protocol that ignores reality — new evidence types, search issues, and tool updates are legitimate reasons to amend. What removes the harm is visibility: each amendment is dated, justified with what prompted it, and recorded. The test is not "never changed" but "never changed silently or in the direction of the desired result."

Primary sources: <https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/prospero/> and <https://training.cochrane.org/handbook>.

## Decision rules

- Register the protocol before any results are known, with eligibility criteria included.
- Record the registration identifier in the review's conduct record.
- Treat every post-registration change as an amendment with a date, reason, and what prompted it.
- Never amend an outcome definition or an analysis direction after seeing results on it.
- Report amendments in the final review, not hidden in a register history page.
- Update the register entry if the review is abandoned or its scope changes materially.

## Procedure

1. Complete the protocol: question, criteria, search plan, tools, outcomes.
2. Register the protocol and record the date and identifier.
3. Begin data collection only after registration.
4. Log any change as an amendment with date, reason, and prompt.
5. Review the amendment list before write-up and report it fully.
6. Update the register entry on material scope change or abandonment.

## Anti-patterns

- Registering after screening has begun.
- Amendments recorded nowhere except the team's memory.
- Post-hoc outcome changes attributed to protocol vagueness.
- Presenting the register entry as proof of no changes rather than the change log.
- Abandoning a review without updating the registration.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Protocol registered before results known.
- [ ] Identifier recorded.
- [ ] Amendments dated, reasoned, and prompted-by-what.
- [ ] No outcome amended post-results.
- [ ] Amendments reported in the review.
- [ ] Register entry updated on abandonment or scope change.

Registration also protects the review team from itself. Once the entry is public, every temptation to widen the population or add a striking outcome acquires a visible cost, and the team's internal debates become questions about the protocol rather than about the results anyone hoped for. The register entry is not bureaucracy; it is the team's decision made once, calmly, before the evidence could argue back.
