# Opting out

## Standard

Opting out removes an investor from the class and preserves the right to bring an individual action. The trade is a certain, proportionate share of a settlement fund against an uncertain, potentially larger recovery from a separate claim, at the cost of litigation expense and time.

The arithmetic that decides it is the size of the individual loss relative to the cost of pursuing it. A holder whose recognised loss is small relative to the legal cost has no rational reason to opt out, however strong the claim appears. A holder with a large loss has a genuine choice, and the choice turns on the defendant's ability to pay, the strength of the evidence specific to that holder's transactions, and whether a funding arrangement is available.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the opt-out deadline and the procedure required.
- Record the investor's recognised loss under the settlement's plan.
- Record the estimated class recovery and the ratio to loss.
- Record the cost of an individual action and any funding available.
- Record the defendant's ability to satisfy a larger judgment.
- Record the effect of opting out on participation in other classes covering the same period.

## Procedure

1. Record the deadline and the required procedure.
2. Compute the loss and the class recovery estimate.
3. Estimate individual action cost and duration.
4. Compare the expected outcomes across a range.
5. Record the defendant's capacity to pay.
6. Write the decision with its reasoning.

## Anti-patterns

- Opting out on principle without the arithmetic.
- Ignoring the cost and duration of an individual action.
- Assuming a larger claim yields a larger recovery.
- Missing the deadline, which forecloses both routes.
- Overlooking overlapping class periods that affect the calculation.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Deadline and procedure recorded.
- [ ] Loss and class recovery computed.
- [ ] Individual action cost estimated.
- [ ] Outcomes compared.
- [ ] Defendant capacity recorded.
- [ ] Decision written with reasoning.

The defendant's capacity to pay is the constraint that most often decides the question: an individual action against a company with ample resources and insurance may recover more than the class settlement, while one against a company whose liabilities exceed its assets recovers a share of the same insufficient pool at a higher cost. Recording capacity alongside the arithmetic is what separates a strategy from an impulse.
