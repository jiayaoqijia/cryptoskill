# Project record

## Standard

The project record states the cash flow, the coverage ratios, the contractual obligations that survive to the end of the term, and the return with its reliance on refinancing. Those four things are what a lender or an equity investor is actually buying, and each of them is a contractual fact rather than a market view.

Its second function is to hold the obligations that appear late. Handback capital, deferred maintenance, and renewal cycles are costs that fall years after the transaction, and recording them at entry means a later review can see whether the project's distributions were funded by its cash flow or by the absence of the work it eventually had to do.

Primary sources: <https://www.worldbank.org/> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the payment regime and the cash flow's main drivers.
- The DSCR is recorded with its definition and its distance to the lock-up.
- Capital expenditure is placed by year, with the renewal trough identified.
- Handback obligations are recorded with their estimated cost and timing.
- The equity return is recorded with its refinancing reliance and gain-share.
- Review triggers are dated: coverage test dates, renewal years, refinancing windows, concession milestones.

## Procedure

1. Record the payment regime and the cash flow drivers.
2. Record the DSCR, its definition, and the headroom.
3. Place the capital expenditure by year.
4. Record the handback obligations.
5. Record the return and its reliance.
6. Set review triggers and amend at each.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording the return without the refinancing assumption.
- Omitting the renewal capital, which is the cost that arrives mid-life.
- Leaving handback out because it is far away.
- Recording coverage without its definition.
- No dated triggers, so the coverage tests are discovered rather than monitored.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Payment regime and drivers recorded.
- [ ] DSCR and headroom recorded.
- [ ] Capital expenditure placed by year.
- [ ] Handback obligations recorded.
- [ ] Return and reliance recorded.
- [ ] Triggers dated.

The record should also state who bears the renewal capital: a project refinanced before its renewal cycle transfers the obligation to whichever lender holds it at the time, and naming that lender at entry turns an unstated assumption into a known allocation.


The record is also the only place where the sponsor's operational capability is written down: an availability project's return depends on maintaining uptime over decades, and naming the operator, its track record, and the replacement mechanism turns a qualitative impression into a stated assumption that can be tested at review.
