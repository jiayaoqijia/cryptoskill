# Dry powder and commitments

## Standard

A commitment is not an investment until it is called. A fund reporting ten percent of a portfolio can be carrying unfunded obligations equal to another twenty percent, and those obligations will be called on the manager's timetable rather than the investor's. The reported allocation is therefore an understatement of the exposure, and the gap is largest exactly when markets are stressed and calls accelerate.

The investor's own dry powder matters for the same reason. A portfolio that is fully invested and also carrying unfunded commitments has committed more capital than it holds, which is a leverage of sorts, and the funding has to come from somewhere: from distributions that may not arrive, from cash that was meant for something else, or from selling assets at the wrong moment.

Primary sources: <https://www.cfainstitute.org/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record committed capital, paid-in capital, and unfunded commitment separately.
- Record the unfunded commitment as a percentage of total portfolio value.
- Record the expected call schedule and the fund's remaining investment period.
- Record the investor's available liquidity against the unfunded amount.
- Record any subscription facility that defers calls and its effect on timing.
- Record the overcommitment ratio where the investor is running a programme.

## Procedure

1. Collect commitments, paid-in, and unfunded per fund.
2. Sum the unfunded across funds.
3. Compute the ratio to portfolio value and to liquid assets.
4. Record the call schedule and investment period.
5. Record facility arrangements that change timing.
6. Write the commitment summary with the coverage ratio.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating committed capital as invested capital.
- Ignoring unfunded commitments in the allocation calculation.
- Assuming calls arrive on the schedule originally projected.
- Overlooking a subscription facility's effect on reported returns.
- Running a programme whose unfunded exceeds available liquidity.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Committed, paid-in, and unfunded recorded.
- [ ] Unfunded share of portfolio computed.
- [ ] Call schedule recorded.
- [ ] Liquidity coverage computed.
- [ ] Facilities recorded.
- [ ] Overcommitment ratio recorded.

The coverage ratio worth writing down is unfunded commitments divided by liquid assets plus expected distributions over the next twelve months: a ratio above one means the programme depends on asset sales or on distributions arriving on schedule, which is a funding assumption rather than a fact.
