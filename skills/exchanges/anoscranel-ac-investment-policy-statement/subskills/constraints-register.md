# Constraints register

## Standard

Constraints are the walls the portfolio may not cross, and most policy failures are a wall nobody wrote down. The register covers four classes: liquidity (funds needed at a date), legal and regulatory (what the owner may hold, where), tax (what is penalised, when), and personal or ethical (what the owner will not hold).

Each constraint is recorded as an operative limit, not as a consideration: a dollar amount, a date, a jurisdiction, or a prohibition. A constraint that cannot be expressed as a limit is recorded as a qualitative preference with its reason, so a later reader can see it was considered rather than forgotten.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Every constraint is expressed as a limit with a threshold, or as an explicit prohibition.
- Liquidity constraints are dated amounts, never percentages of the portfolio.
- Legal constraints name the jurisdiction and the instrument class.
- Tax constraints state the jurisdiction and the event that triggers the liability; the policy does not compute the liability.
- Ethical constraints are written as an exclusion list with the rule that generated it.
- Constraints are reviewed on the same cadence as goals; a constraint that has expired is removed with a date.

## Procedure

1. List liquidity needs with amounts and dates.
2. List legal and regulatory limits by jurisdiction and instrument class.
3. List tax-relevant events by jurisdiction, without computing liability.
4. List exclusions with the principle behind each.
5. Express each as a limit or a prohibition; flag the ones that cannot be.
6. Date the register and set its review.

## Anti-patterns

- Writing "needs some liquidity" without an amount or date.
- Treating tax as a reason to avoid a class without stating the event.
- Leaving an ethical exclusion implicit.
- Copying a template's constraints without checking jurisdiction.
- Keeping an expired constraint on the books.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Liquidity needs dated and quantified.
- [ ] Legal limits by jurisdiction and instrument class.
- [ ] Tax-relevant events listed by jurisdiction.
- [ ] Exclusions listed with their principle.
- [ ] Each constraint expressed as limit or prohibition; exceptions flagged.
- [ ] Register dated and review set.

Constraints change with life more often than goals do: a jurisdiction, an employer, a family obligation. The register is therefore reviewed on the same cadence as the goals, and an expired constraint is removed with a date so the record shows why it no longer applies.
