# Fallen angels and forced selling

## Standard

Many mandates are written to hold only investment-grade debt, and a rating cut below the threshold makes the holding ineligible. The manager must sell, on a timetable set by the mandate rather than by the price, and every such manager sells the same instrument at the same time. That is the mechanism behind the fallen-angel effect: the price move is produced by the eligibility rule, not by a reassessment of the issuer's prospects.

The flow is predictable in direction and not in size. The amount that must be sold depends on how widely the instrument is held by constrained mandates, which is disclosed only in aggregate, and the timing depends on each mandate's cure period. A holder who is not constrained can be paid to provide liquidity; a holder who is constrained has no choice, and the record should state which of the two the position is.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.bis.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the rating and the distance to the investment-grade threshold.
- Record whether the mandate itself is constrained by the threshold.
- Record the estimated size of index-tracked and mandate-constrained holdings.
- Record the cure period or forced-sale deadline the mandate imposes.
- Record the liquidity of the instrument relative to the likely forced volume.
- Record the price behaviour of comparable past downgrades.

## Procedure

1. Record the current rating and the threshold.
2. Record whether the holder is constrained.
3. Estimate constrained holdings from index and fund data.
4. Record the cure period.
5. Compare likely forced volume with typical daily volume.
6. Write the flow assessment with the holder's own constraint named.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming a downgrade is already in the price because it was expected.
- Ignoring the holder's own mandate constraint.
- Comparing forced volume with average daily volume in a calm market.
- Treating the threshold as a single agency's decision when several apply.
- Assuming the cure period is the same for every mandate.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Rating and threshold distance recorded.
- [ ] Own constraint recorded.
- [ ] Constrained holdings estimated.
- [ ] Cure period recorded.
- [ ] Forced volume compared.
- [ ] Past behaviour recorded.

The other side of the flow is worth recording for a holder who is not constrained: the forced seller is price-insensitive by construction, which is the one condition under which liquidity provision is compensated rather than competed away, and the compensation is largest where the constrained holding is widest relative to the instrument's normal volume.
