# Certainty and sensitivity

## Standard

A pooled estimate with a confidence interval is a statement about sampling variation. It says nothing about whether the studies were at risk of bias, whether the results are consistent, whether the evidence is direct, whether it is precise enough for a decision, or whether publication may have selected what got pooled. Certainty rating assembles those judgements into one explicit claim about how much the estimate should be believed, and the rating moves down — or occasionally up — by named reasons, not by a formula that hides them.

Sensitivity analysis is the second half: does the finding survive the assumptions being questioned? Re-running the synthesis restricted to low-risk studies, under the alternate model, without the dominant trial, or with an alternate zero-event handling tells the reader whether the pooled estimate is an artefact of choices or robust across them. A finding that moves when the largest trial is removed is a finding about that trial as much as about the field, and reporting the move is the honesty.

Primary sources: <https://gradeworkinggroup.org> and <https://training.cochrane.org/handbook/current/chapter-12>.

## Decision rules

- Rate certainty with a named framework and per-domain reasons recorded.
- Move ratings only for explicit reasons: risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication concern.
- Pre-specify the sensitivity analyses in the protocol.
- Report every pre-specified sensitivity result, including those that move the estimate.
- Distinguish the point estimate moving from its conclusion crossing a decision threshold.
- Report imprecision as the interval versus the decision, not merely as statistical significance.

## Procedure

1. Extract the per-domain judgements that feed the certainty rating.
2. Record each rating move and its named reason.
3. Run the protocol's pre-specified sensitivity analyses.
4. Tabulate each variant against the primary estimate and note the movers.
5. State whether any interval crosses a decision threshold under a sensitivity.
6. Write the certainty statement with the assumptions that drive it.

## Anti-patterns

- Reporting a certainty rating with no recorded per-domain reasons.
- Running only the sensitivity analyses that keep the result stable.
- Dropping a sensitivity because it makes the finding uncomfortable.
- Confusing a wide interval that still excludes no effect with a strong one.
- Updating a rating upward without a stated, justified reason.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Framework named; per-domain judgements recorded.
- [ ] Every rating move carries its named reason.
- [ ] Pre-specified sensitivities all run.
- [ ] All sensitivity results tabulated.
- [ ] Decision-threshold crossings identified.
- [ ] Certainty statement names its assumptions.
