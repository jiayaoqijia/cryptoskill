# Spin-offs and rights

## Standard

A spin-off distributes shares of a subsidiary to existing holders. The parent's price falls by roughly the value distributed, and the holder ends with two positions whose combined basis must equal the original basis. The allocation between them is the whole problem: it changes the gain reported when either is sold, and it has to be documented.

A rights issue offers existing holders the chance to buy new shares, usually at a discount. Taking up the rights means a new purchase with its own basis; selling the rights is a disposal of a separate asset; letting them lapse is a decision that should be recorded rather than defaulted into.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Record the distribution ratio, the record date, and the allocation method for basis.
- Allocate basis between parent and spun-off entity using the stated method, and record it.
- Rights: record the terms, the subscription price, and the expiry.
- Record the decision on the rights — take up, sell, or lapse — with the reasoning.
- Selling the rights is a disposal with its own proceeds and basis.
- Reconcile both resulting positions with the broker.

## Procedure

1. Record the spin-off ratio, record date, and basis-allocation method.
2. Apply the allocation and record both bases.
3. For rights, record terms, price, and expiry.
4. Record the decision on the rights and why.
5. Treat a sale of rights as a disposal.
6. Reconcile each resulting position.

## Anti-patterns

- Assigning the spun-off shares a zero basis without a method.
- Forgetting that the parent's basis changes.
- Letting rights lapse without recording the decision.
- Treating sold rights as a dividend.
- Reconciling only the parent position.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Ratio, record date, and allocation method recorded.
- [ ] Basis allocated across both positions.
- [ ] Rights terms, price, and expiry recorded.
- [ ] Rights decision recorded with reasoning.
- [ ] Sale of rights treated as disposal.
- [ ] Both positions reconciled.

The allocation method is a choice with consequences, and the record should name it: relative fair value at the distribution date is the common approach, and it requires the prices used. A method stated without the prices is a claim nobody can check.
