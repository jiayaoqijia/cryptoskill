# Out-of-court and exchange

## Standard

Most restructurings happen outside court, through an exchange offer in which holders swap existing debt for a combination of new debt, equity, and cash. The offer is negotiated with a creditor group that has agreed to support it, and its success depends on whether enough of the remaining holders tender — which is why the terms are designed to make holding out unattractive.

The coercion is structural rather than moral. An exit consent lets tendering holders amend the indenture's protective covenants, which strips value from the bonds that non-tendering holders keep; a minimum tender condition means the offer fails entirely if participation is too low; and a subordination feature can push holdouts behind the new money. Reading those three mechanisms tells you what a holder who refuses is actually choosing.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://www.law.cornell.edu/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the offer's consideration by instrument and the exchange ratio.
- Record the minimum tender condition and any exit consent.
- Record what happens to non-tendering holders if the offer succeeds.
- Record the support level already committed by the creditor group.
- Record the deadline and any early-tender premium.
- Record the alternative if the offer fails, which is usually a court process.

## Procedure

1. Read the offer memorandum and the indenture amendments proposed.
2. Record the consideration and the exchange ratio.
3. Identify exit consents and minimum conditions.
4. Record the committed support and the free float.
5. Compute the outcome for a tendering and a non-tendering holder.
6. Write the analysis with the failure case stated.

## Anti-patterns

- Comparing the offer's face value without the exit consent's effect.
- Assuming a high tender rate means a fair offer.
- Ignoring the minimum condition and treating the offer as certain.
- Treating a holdout as a negotiating position when the covenants have already been stripped.
- Forgetting that a failed exchange usually leads to a worse process.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Consideration and ratio recorded.
- [ ] Minimum condition recorded.
- [ ] Exit consents recorded.
- [ ] Committed support recorded.
- [ ] Both holder outcomes computed.
- [ ] Failure case stated.

A further consideration is timing: an exchange offer launched before a maturity wall is reached carries more negotiating leverage than one launched after a payment has already been missed, and the same terms offered earlier can be rejected later because the alternative has changed. The record should note where the offer sits relative to the maturity schedule.
