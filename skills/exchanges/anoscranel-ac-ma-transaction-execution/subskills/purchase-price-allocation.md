# Purchase price allocation

## Standard

In a business combination the purchase price is allocated across the assets acquired and liabilities assumed at their acquisition-date fair values. What the price cannot be allocated to becomes goodwill — the residual that represents future economic benefits that cannot be individually identified. The allocation is not an accounting ceremony: it sets the depreciation and amortisation that will run through the combined income statement for years, and two buyers paying the same price can report different earnings purely through allocation choices.

The items that most often move earnings are intangibles: customer relationships, technology, trade names, and order backlog are valued and amortised, while goodwill is not amortised but tested for impairment. A higher allocation to identifiable intangibles means lower reported earnings than the same deal allocated heavily to goodwill — and the allocation must reflect valuation substance, not the desired earnings profile. Deferred tax and the fair value of assumed debt add further allocation complexity.

Primary sources: <https://www.fasb.org/> and <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the purchase price and the fair values of all identifiable assets acquired and liabilities assumed.
- Recognise identifiable intangibles separately from goodwill where they meet the recognition criteria.
- State the useful lives assigned to amortising intangibles and the amortisation method.
- Measure goodwill as the residual after full allocation, and state the impairment-testing basis.
- Record deferred taxes arising from the fair value adjustments.
- Reconcile the allocation between the preliminary and measurement-period versions.

## Procedure

1. Establish the purchase price including any contingent consideration at fair value.
2. Value and record the tangible assets and assumed liabilities at acquisition-date fair value.
3. Identify and value the separable intangibles.
4. Compute goodwill as the residual.
5. Record the amortisation schedule and deferred tax effects.
6. Track any measurement-period adjustments and their earnings effect.

## Anti-patterns

- Steering the allocation toward goodwill to reduce amortisation.
- Treating preliminary allocation as final and never reconciling the measurement period.
- Omitting contingent consideration and its later remeasurement.
- Overlooking deferred taxes on the fair value step-ups.
- Impairment-testing goodwill without stating the testing basis.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Price and contingent consideration at fair value.
- [ ] Tangible assets and liabilities at fair value.
- [ ] Identifiable intangibles separately valued.
- [ ] Goodwill as residual with testing basis.
- [ ] Deferred taxes recorded.
- [ ] Measurement-period adjustments reconciled.
