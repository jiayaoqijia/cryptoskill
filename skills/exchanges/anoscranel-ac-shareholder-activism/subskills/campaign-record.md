# Campaign record

## Standard

The record states the demand, the type of campaign, the filing timeline, the settlement or contest path, and the vote arithmetic if a contest occurs. It exists so that a later review can distinguish a campaign that failed on its argument from one that failed on its mechanics, which are different failures with different implications for anyone holding the stock through them.

It also carries the counterfactual: what the company did independently over the same period. A share price that rose during a campaign may reflect the campaign, the market, or the company's own improvements, and a record that names those separately is the only one that can attribute the effect. The record ends with what would count as resolution, set before the outcome.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the demand, the campaign type, and whether a vote is required.
- The filing timeline is recorded with dates and amendments.
- The settlement terms or the contest arithmetic are recorded, whichever applies.
- The price change over the campaign is recorded and compared with the market's.
- The sponsor's holding period and economics are recorded where disclosed.
- Resolution criteria are set in advance, with the conditions that would end the thesis.

## Procedure

1. Record the demand and the campaign type.
2. Build the filing timeline.
3. Record the settlement terms or contest arithmetic.
4. Record price performance against a market baseline.
5. Record the sponsor's economics.
6. Set resolution criteria and file the record.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording the campaign's narrative instead of its mechanics.
- Attributing all price movement to the campaign.
- Omitting the filing sequence, which shows the escalation.
- Leaving resolution criteria undefined.
- Reviewing only at the outcome and not at the path.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Demand and type recorded.
- [ ] Filing timeline built.
- [ ] Settlement or contest arithmetic recorded.
- [ ] Price compared with baseline.
- [ ] Sponsor economics recorded.
- [ ] Resolution criteria set.

A record that states what would count as resolution before the campaign finishes also protects the analyst from the most common error in this area: redefining success after the fact so that whatever happened is described as the intended outcome. The criteria were written when the situation was ambiguous, which is exactly when they carry information.
