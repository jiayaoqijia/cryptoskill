# Financing record

## Standard

The record states the facilities the fund uses, the leverage they add, the return adjusted for their effect, and the elections available to the investor with their deadlines. Its purpose is to make the fund comparable with one that does not use these structures, because the headline figures are not.

It also records the investor's own decisions: whether to roll into a continuation, whether to sell an interest, and on what arithmetic. Those decisions are infrequent and consequential, and they arrive with a deadline and a default. Writing the reasoning at the time is the only way a later review can judge whether the choice was sound.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record lists each facility with its size, cost, covenant, and maturity.
- The return is recorded both as reported and as adjusted for financing effects.
- The multiple is recorded beside the IRR, since timing does not affect it.
- Continuation and secondary elections are recorded with their deadlines and defaults.
- The investor's election and its arithmetic are recorded.
- Review triggers are dated: facility renewal, covenant test, election window.

## Procedure

1. List the facilities and their terms.
2. Record reported and adjusted returns.
3. Record the multiple.
4. Record each election with its deadline.
5. Record the decision and its arithmetic.
6. Set triggers and file the record.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording the reported return without the adjustment.
- Omitting the multiple, which is the timing-independent check.
- Leaving an election unrecorded until its deadline passes.
- Recording a decision without the arithmetic.
- No triggers, so facility renewals go unnoticed.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Facilities listed with terms.
- [ ] Adjusted return recorded.
- [ ] Multiple recorded.
- [ ] Elections and deadlines recorded.
- [ ] Decisions recorded with arithmetic.
- [ ] Triggers dated.

The record's comparative value accumulates over a portfolio: once several funds are recorded in the same fields, the total fund-level leverage across the private programme becomes visible, and that aggregate is what determines whether the portfolio's reported risk is the risk it actually carries. Individual records understate the exposure that only appears when they are added together.


A record that names the facility's covenant and its test date also converts a document into a calendar: the investor knows when the fund's leverage is measured, and therefore when a valuation decline becomes a financing event rather than a paper loss.
