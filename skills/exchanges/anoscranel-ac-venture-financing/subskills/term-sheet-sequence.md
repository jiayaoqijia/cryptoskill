# Term sheet sequence

## Standard

A term sheet is the summary of terms the round will trade on: valuation and security type, board composition, liquidation preferences, anti-dilution protections, participation rights, and the control provisions that govern future decisions. It is mostly non-binding in contract terms and entirely binding in practice, because it sets the ground the lawyers will paper and the price the market will remember at the next round.

The sequence matters because each round rests on the one before. The pre-money valuation of a new round is not an isolated judgement; it prices against the company's progress since the last round, and the round's security type — priced preferred versus a SAFE or note — determines whether its terms arrive now or at the conversion event. A company that postpones a priced round by stacking SAFEs is deferring the term negotiation, not avoiding it, and the eventual priced round must clear all the stacked instruments' conversion terms at one setting.

Primary sources: <https://www.law.cornell.edu/> and <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the round's type — priced preferred, SAFE, convertible note — and its economic mechanism.
- Record the pre-money valuation, the new money, and the resulting post-money fully diluted ownership.
- Locate the control terms: board size and composition, protective provisions, and information rights.
- Trace each term back to the prior round's equivalent: what strengthened, what weakened.
- List the conditions precedent and the milestone obligations the round carries.
- Record the option-pool treatment decided alongside the round.

## Procedure

1. Extract the price terms: security type, valuation or cap/discount, amount.
2. Compute post-money ownership including convertible instruments.
3. Extract the control terms and compare them with the prior round's.
4. List the conditions and milestones attached.
5. Model the conversion of stacked instruments if the round is priced.
6. Write the term summary as the negotiation's map, dated.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading a term sheet's valuation without the pool's pre/post-money treatment.
- Treating a SAFE as a priced round: the price arrives at conversion.
- Accepting control terms without comparing them to the prior round's.
- Treating the non-binding nature of the document as meaning the terms are provisional.
- Ignoring stacked converts and their effect on the eventual priced round.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Round type and price mechanism recorded.
- [ ] Post-money fully diluted ownership computed.
- [ ] Control terms located and compared.
- [ ] Conditions and milestones listed.
- [ ] Stacked converts modelled.
- [ ] Pool treatment recorded.
- [ ] Summary dated.
