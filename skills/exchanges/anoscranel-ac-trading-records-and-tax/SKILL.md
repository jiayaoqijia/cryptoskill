# Trading records and tax

## Overview

The tax consequence of trading is computed from records, and the records are almost always worse than the trader believes. Missing fills, unrecorded fees, transfers between venues, and airdropped tokens that arrived with a price nobody wrote down — each becomes a question at filing time, and the answers are only as good as the paper trail.

This skill is the paper trail. It covers what to record, how to keep cost basis defensible, how to reconcile records to venue statements, and how to assemble a pack that a qualified professional can work from quickly. It deliberately does not compute a liability, apply a rate, or interpret a statute: those depend on jurisdiction and on facts a professional must assess.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.irs.gov/>.

## When to use

- When setting up record-keeping before the first trade, which is the only cheap moment.
- When preparing an annual pack for a tax professional.
- When reconciling venue statements against the desk's own records.
- When a jurisdiction, residency, or instrument type changes.

## When NOT to use

- For computing or filing tax: use a qualified professional in the relevant jurisdiction.
- For the trade decision record and journal (use ac-crypto-trading-discipline).
- For portfolio policy and allocation (use ac-investment-policy-statement).

## Status and honest limits

This skill organises evidence; it does not interpret law. Tax treatment varies by jurisdiction, by residency, by instrument, and by facts that no generic guide can capture, and rules change. Every leaf therefore ends at the boundary of preparation, and the record explicitly says which questions were left for the professional. Where a rule is stated, it is a question to verify, not an answer to rely on.
