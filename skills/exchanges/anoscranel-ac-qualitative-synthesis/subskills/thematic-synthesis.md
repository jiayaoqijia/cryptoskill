# Thematic synthesis

## Standard

Thematic synthesis works in two stages with a hard boundary between them. The descriptive stage groups codes into themes that stay at the level of the primary studies' language — what the coded findings say, organised. The analytical stage develops those descriptive themes further, asking what the patterns mean beyond any single study; the analytical theme is the synthesist's contribution, and it is only legitimate because the descriptive stage fixed the evidence first. The two-stage structure is what keeps the interpretive step honest: the analytical themes are accountable to the descriptive ones, not to the synthesist's priors.

The output's value depends on the descent staying traceable. An analytical theme that cannot be walked back down through the descriptive themes and codes to the original passages is an interpretation floating free of the evidence. The traceability that makes the walk possible is the skill's discipline, and the walk should be demonstrated in the record, not merely claimed.

Primary sources: <https://bmcmedresmethodol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2288-8-45> and <https://www.biomedcentral.com/methods>.

## Decision rules

- Complete descriptive themes before any analytical work begins.
- Keep descriptive themes in the primary studies' language.
- Develop analytical themes only from descriptive ones, never from the synthesist's own interest in the question.
- Demonstrate the walk from each analytical theme down to its supporting passages.
- Record codes that were not absorbed by any theme; orphan codes are a finding about the evidence, not waste.
- State where the analytical stage outruns the evidence's thickness.

## Procedure

1. Group the coding phase's codes into descriptive themes, staying at the level of the original language.
2. Review and refine the descriptive themes against the full code set.
3. Develop each descriptive theme analytically: what does this pattern mean across the studies?
4. Trace each analytical theme down through its descriptive themes and codes to source passages.
5. Record orphan codes and thin themes as findings about the evidence base.
6. Publish the trace chain in the record so a reader can walk any theme to its sources.

## Anti-patterns

- Beginning analytical work while descriptive themes are incomplete.
- Analytical themes not derivable from any descriptive grouping.
- Circular development: shaping the descriptive themes to support a favoured analytical idea.
- Discarding orphan codes silently.
- Themes that cannot be walked to their sources when challenged.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Descriptive stage complete before analytical.
- [ ] Descriptive themes in original language.
- [ ] Analytical themes derived only from descriptive ones.
- [ ] Walk demonstrated from theme to passages.
- [ ] Orphan codes recorded.
- [ ] Over-reach flagged where it occurs.
