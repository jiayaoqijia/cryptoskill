# Private credit record

## Standard

The record states the structure the exposure actually carries: the tranche, the waterfall position, the covenants and their definitions, the PIK proportion, the sponsor's incentives, and the valuation policy under which it is marked. Those facts are not in the fund's headline yield, and they are what determine the recovery if the credit deteriorates.

It also states the exit path, because a private credit position has no market to sell into. The routes are repayment at maturity, refinancing by the borrower, a secondary sale at a discount, or a restructuring. Naming which route is assumed, and what the position is worth under each, converts an illiquid holding into a plan rather than an assumption.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the tranche, the waterfall position, and the facility terms.
- Covenants are recorded with their definitions and test frequency.
- The cash yield and the total yield are recorded separately.
- The valuation policy and mark producer are recorded.
- The sponsor's incentives and the value-transfer mechanisms are recorded.
- The exit route is named with its assumed value, and review triggers are dated.

## Procedure

1. Record the structure and the tranche.
2. Record covenants and definitions.
3. Record cash and total yield separately.
4. Record valuation policy and mark producer.
5. Record the sponsor's position and mechanisms.
6. Name the exit route, set review triggers, and file the record.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording the yield without separating cash from PIK.
- Omitting the valuation policy from a record about value.
- Leaving the exit route implicit.
- Recording covenants without their definitions.
- Reviewing only at the fund's reporting dates.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Structure and tranche recorded.
- [ ] Covenants and definitions recorded.
- [ ] Cash and total yields recorded.
- [ ] Valuation policy recorded.
- [ ] Sponsor position recorded.
- [ ] Exit route and triggers recorded.

The record's value is greatest before an allocation rather than after: writing down the structure, the covenants, and the exit route converts a manager's presentation into a set of testable statements, and the questions that arise while writing it are the ones worth asking before the capital is committed rather than at the first amendment.

A record kept in this form also makes the fund's own reporting comparable across periods: the same fields, recorded at each report, show whether the PIK proportion is rising, whether covenants have been amended, and whether the mark has moved without a corresponding change in the borrower's results.
