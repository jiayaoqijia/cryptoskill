# Persuasion

## Standard

- **Persuasion knowledge is the defence.** People mount coping responses when a persuasion episode activates their beliefs about the seller's goals (Friestad & Wright, *JCR* 1994, <https://doi.org/10.1086/209380>). A salient ask that reads as manipulative raises activation.
- **Transportation suppresses counterarguing.** Across 132 effect sizes, transportation moves attitudes at ρ =.44 and cuts critical thoughts at ρ = −.20. Transported readers notice fewer factual errors (van Laer et al. 2014).
- **Absorbed processing decouples persuasion from argument quality, then reverses.** Weak arguments do not lose with a transported reader, but the effect flips for skeptical recipients (Escalas 2007). So it never licenses a weak offer.
- **Controlling language triggers reactance.** A threat to behavioural freedom produces anger and counterargument and often moves the attitude opposite to intent (Rains 2013).
- **Let the reader supply the fact and the number.** Self-generated conclusions hold more certainty than handed-down ones. Every serious discovery methodology converges on the buyer filling in the numbers.
- **Disclosure is a measured trade-off, never a permission.** Disclosure lowers brand attitude and credibility while raising recognition and resistance (Eisend et al. 2020). It lifts no compliance duty.
- **Objection response is regular and mostly mechanical.** The academic standard is point-by-point, each comment quoted verbatim and numbered. Measured, only 6% of letters fully accommodate every suggestion.

## Decision rules

1. Name the reader's strongest objection before drafting; an unaddressed best objection reads as evasion.
2. Place the rebuttal where the doubt fires — price objections at the price, not in a closing FAQ.
3. State the evidence and let the reader draw the conclusion.
4. Strike controlling language: "just buy it", "you must", manufactured urgency.
5. Concede precisely; over-conceding admits more than the comment implied.
6. Disclose where a reasonable reader would ask how the content was created.
7. Route every attestation, integrity call and publish act to a human.

## Procedure

1. List the objections: price, fit, effort, trust, timing, alternatives, risk, need.
2. Mine the reader's own words for each: reviews, calls, tickets, recorded interviews.
3. Write the answering evidence for each objection, with its source.
4. Order the document so each objection is answered where it arises.
5. Draft the objection block: the objection stated fairly, the evidence, the limit of the claim.
6. Run the reactance check: cut every coercive line.
7. Flag each conceded item and each claim that exceeds its grounds.
8. Hand the human the draft and the flag list. **The human sends or publishes.**

## Anti-patterns

- The strongest objection dismissed in one line.
- A conclusion imposed where the reader would have drawn it.
- Disclosure omitted for conversion, then legally required.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Objections listed and mined in the reader's words.
- [ ] Each objection answered where it arises.
- [ ] No controlling language; no manufactured urgency.
- [ ] Concessions precise and flagged.
- [ ] Disclosures placed where a reader would ask.
- [ ] Claim strength never exceeds its grounds.
- [ ] **Human-gated:** sent or published by a named person.
