# 10 — Portfolio Construction, Rebalancing and Behavior

## Purpose

Turn diagnosis into a conditional plan. Construction is not “pick the best coin”; it is choosing exposures, risk budget, liquidity, and implementation rules that fit the mandate.

## Inputs

- IPS-lite objectives, risk budget, constraints.
- Current weights, risk contributions, liquidity buckets.
- Tax situation and implementation costs.
- Candidate alternatives and their evidence.

## Method

1. Define the risk budget and rebalancing bands.
2. Map current weights and risk contributions to limits.
3. Build 2–3 alternatives, not one “optimal” portfolio.
4. Estimate risk, liquidity, cost, tax, and implementation for each.
5. Run a behavioral-bias check.
6. Recommend a conditional action with falsifiers and a review date.

## Strategic vs tactical

- **Strategic asset allocation (SAA):** long-term policy weights; driven by objectives, constraints, and risk budget.
- **Tactical asset allocation (TAA):** temporary deviations; must have a stated signal, size limit, and exit rule.
- Do not let a tactical view quietly become the new strategic allocation.

## Risk budget

1. Set the maximum portfolio loss and drawdown from the IPS-lite.
2. Allocate risk across clusters, not tickers.
3. Set position, cluster, scenario, and liquidity limits.
4. Check each proposed change’s contribution to portfolio risk, not just its weight.
5. If the risk budget is unknown, present alternatives and trade-offs; do not prescribe a size.

## Rebalancing

- Define target weights and bands (e.g., ±5 percentage points; user-specific).
- Rebalance on a schedule or when a band is breached; do not react to every price move.
- Taxable portfolios may use wider bands; include transaction costs and taxes.
- Rebalancing is not a prediction; it is risk control.
- Record the rule before the review, not after.

## Liquidity buckets

| Bucket | Horizon | Examples | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash-like | 0–7 days | Stablecoins, major spot | Check issuer and depeg risk |
| Liquid | 1–4 weeks | Major tokens, deep pools | Exit at 5% participation |
| Semi-liquid | 1–6 months | Staking, vesting, smaller pools | Include queue and haircut |
| Illiquid | >6 months | Locked/vested, NFTs, private positions | Separate risk budget |

## Alternatives

Present 2–3 alternatives, not one “optimal” portfolio:

- **Current:** baseline.
- **Risk-reduced:** lower concentration, more stablecoin/liquidity, lower expected return.
- **Diversified-within-thesis:** add uncorrelated names, cap clusters.
- **Liquidity-first:** raise cash-like bucket; accept lower upside.

For each: expected risk, liquidity, concentration, implementation cost, tax, and what would make it wrong.

## Behavioral biases

| Bias | Portfolio symptom | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Loss aversion | Holding a loser to avoid realizing | Pre-set falsifiers; separate thesis from entry price |
| Recency | Chasing last quarter’s winner | Use long windows and regimes |
| Anchoring | “It will get back to my entry” | Value on forward risk, not cost basis |
| Confirmation | Only reading bullish sources | Required bear case and falsifiers |
| Home/chain bias | Overweighting a familiar ecosystem | Cluster map and explicit cap |
| Overconfidence | Concentrating after a good run | Risk budget and scenario limits |
| Mental accounting | Treating “house money” differently | Consolidate all positions |
| Illusion of control | Trading frequently in a noisy market | Pre-committed rebalancing rules |

## Decision record

- What to do, if anything.
- Why now; what changed.
- Size/implementation range, only with constraints.
- What would falsify the view.
- What would make the position smaller, not just “wrong.”
- Review date and monitoring triggers.

## Failure modes

- Presenting a single “optimal” allocation without constraints.
- Rebalancing on emotion and calling it policy.
- Ignoring tax and transaction costs.
- Treating a high-yield stablecoin pool as cash.
- Adding a new asset without checking cluster overlap.
