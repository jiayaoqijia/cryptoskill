# EM access record

## Standard

The record states the route through which the exposure is held, the registration and certificates obtained, the restrictions that apply to holding and to repatriating, the total cost of access including currency conversion, and the exit path under current rules. Those facts determine whether the position can be turned into cash, and they are not visible in any price.

It also states what would change. Capital controls are policy instruments, and a record that names the conditions under which they could tighten, and the exposure if they did, converts a political risk into a monitored one. That is the difference between an allocation made with an exit in mind and one made on the assumption that the exit will be available when needed.

Primary sources: <https://www.imf.org/> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the route: receipt, local account, fund, or quota-based channel.
- Registration, licences, and certificates are recorded with their dates and holders.
- Restrictions on holding, on income, and on repatriation are recorded separately.
- The total cost of access is recorded, including currency conversion.
- The exit path is recorded with its steps, approvals, and expected duration.
- Review triggers are dated: policy change, quota change, programme termination.

## Procedure

1. Record the route and the instruments held.
2. Record registrations and certificates.
3. Record the restrictions by type.
4. Compute the total access cost.
5. Write the exit path with its timeline.
6. Set review triggers and file the record.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording the exposure without the route.
- Treating registration as permanent.
- Omitting the currency conversion cost from the total.
- Recording the exit as a single step rather than a process.
- No triggers, so a policy change is discovered at the point of exit.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Route and instruments recorded.
- [ ] Registrations recorded.
- [ ] Restrictions recorded by type.
- [ ] Access cost computed.
- [ ] Exit path with timeline recorded.
- [ ] Review triggers dated.

The record also protects against institutional memory loss: the certificates and registrations obtained at entry are precisely the documents an exit requires, and the staff who obtained them may have moved on by the time a sale is attempted. Storing them with the position record rather than with the individual is what makes the exit executable.
