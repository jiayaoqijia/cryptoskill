# Agency methodologies

## Standard

Each agency publishes a methodology per sector, and the methodology states which factors are scored, how they are weighted, and what guidance maps a score to a rating. The documents are long and formulaic, but three things in them matter: the factors that can move a rating on their own, the thresholds where a notch changes, and the adjustments the committee may apply outside the model.

The committee's discretion is the part models cannot capture and the part that produces surprises. A methodology may produce one rating and a committee another, justified by a judgement about parental support, sovereign constraints, or an event. That is why reading a methodology gives you the direction a rating is likely to move, not the timing or the level.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.bis.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the methodology and its version date.
- Record the factors scored and their relative weights.
- Record the guidance thresholds that separate notches.
- Record the adjustments available to the committee.
- Record the factors where the issuer is materially different from the sector median.
- Record the last published methodology change and its effect.

## Procedure

1. Obtain the sector methodology and its version.
2. Record the scored factors and weights.
3. Record the thresholds between notches.
4. Record committee adjustments and their conditions.
5. Compare the issuer's metrics against the thresholds.
6. Write the methodology summary with its sensitivities.

## Anti-patterns

- Applying a generic methodology to a specialised sector.
- Ignoring the version date of the methodology used.
- Assuming the score determines the rating.
- Treating committee discretion as arbitrary rather than as judgement.
- Comparing ratings across agencies without their different methods.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Methodology and version recorded.
- [ ] Factors and weights recorded.
- [ ] Thresholds recorded.
- [ ] Adjustments recorded.
- [ ] Issuer metrics compared.
- [ ] Sensitivities written.

The factor most likely to be decisive and least likely to be modelled is support: a subsidiary's rating can sit above its own metrics because of an assumed parental or sovereign backstop, and a methodology section on support is often the shortest in the document and the most consequential when the parent's own rating moves.

Reading two agencies' methodologies side by side for the same issuer is often the fastest way to see where the disagreement will come from, because the factors they weight differently are the ones that will produce a split rating when the issuer's metrics sit between two thresholds.
