# Monitoring and review

## Standard

Monitoring keeps the policy honest; review keeps it current. They are different activities with different triggers. Monitoring is scheduled and mechanical: are the weights inside their ranges, is the rebalancing rule being followed, have any constraints expired. Review is deliberate and infrequent: have the goals, constraints, or capacity changed enough that the policy itself is wrong.

The failure mode is collapsing the two: reading market news daily and calling it monitoring, then changing the policy in response, which is neither. A written cadence with named triggers prevents that collapse.

Primary sources: <https://www.cfainstitute.org/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Monitoring runs on a fixed cadence and checks only what the policy defined: weights, ranges, rebalancing, constraints.
- Review runs on a slower cadence and asks only whether the policy still fits the life it governs.
- Market moves alone never trigger a policy review; they trigger monitoring.
- Life events (income change, expense change, horizon shift) trigger a review immediately.
- Every review ends with either "policy unchanged, dated" or a versioned amendment.
- The record of both cadences is kept together, so the reason for any change is traceable.

## Procedure

1. Define the monitoring checklist and its cadence.
2. Define the review questions and its cadence.
3. Name the life events that force an early review.
4. Record each monitoring pass with its result.
5. Record each review with its decision and date.
6. Amend the policy only through the versioned process.

## Anti-patterns

- Watching prices and calling it monitoring.
- Changing targets after a bad quarter.
- Reviewing the policy only when something breaks.
- Failing to record a "no change" review.
- Treating a life event as a market event.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Monitoring checklist and cadence written.
- [ ] Review questions and cadence written.
- [ ] Early-review life events named.
- [ ] Monitoring passes recorded.
- [ ] Reviews recorded with decision and date.
- [ ] Amendments versioned.

The two cadences fail in different ways. Monitoring fails by becoming a daily price watch that changes nothing and distracts from the policy; review fails by never happening at all until a crisis forces it. Writing both down, and recording the uneventful passes, is what keeps the distinction alive. A review that concludes nothing has changed is a successful review, and it is worth recording as one.
