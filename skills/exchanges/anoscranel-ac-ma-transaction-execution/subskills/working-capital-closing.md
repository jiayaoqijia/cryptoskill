# Working capital and closing

## Standard

For an operating business bought on a cash-free debt-free basis, the price assumes a normal level of working capital. The peg is that normal level; the closing adjustment trues the price to the working capital actually delivered. Without the mechanism a seller could collect receivables before closing and hand over a starved business at the same headline price; with it, delivering below-peg working capital reduces the price dollar for dollar and delivering above it increases the price.

The adjustment's content is definitional: what counts as working capital — cash-like items, debt-like items, tax, and disputed categories — is negotiated in the agreement, and the definition matters as much as the peg level itself. The process is an estimate at closing, a statement delivered within a deadline, an objection right for the other side, and a final number that survives as part of the price. Escrow of the disputed portion keeps the dispute from delaying the closing itself.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://www.law.cornell.edu/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the peg and the working capital definition, including the treatment of cash, debt, tax, and unusual items.
- Record the estimate-statement-objection timeline with every deadline.
- State the dispute mechanism if the parties fail to agree on the final number.
- Record any escrow for the adjustment and its release conditions.
- Note the seller's covenants on operating the business normally between signing and closing.
- Reconcile the definition against the target's historical working capital to see what the peg represents.

## Procedure

1. Extract the peg and the complete definition from the agreement.
2. Reconcile the definition against historical statements to test peg normality.
3. Record the closing-estimate and statement deadlines.
4. Record the objection and dispute-resolution mechanics.
5. Compute the adjustment mechanics on a worked example, including the dollar-for-dollar price change.
6. Write the closing summary as a dated checklist for execution.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating the peg as the headline number while ignoring the definition's exclusions.
- Assuming the historical working capital basis matches the contractual definition.
- Missing estimate or objection deadlines, which convert the estimate to the final number.
- Starving working capital between signing and closing and relying on the seller covenant not being policed.
- Leaving the dispute mechanism unread until a dispute exists.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Peg and definition recorded.
- [ ] Definition reconciled to historical working capital.
- [ ] Timeline with all deadlines recorded.
- [ ] Objection and dispute mechanics recorded.
- [ ] Escrow and release conditions noted.
- [ ] Adjustment worked through dollar-for-dollar.
