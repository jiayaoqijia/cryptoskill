# Round charter

## Standard

- A charter is a falsification test, not a search brief. Popper's criterion: a claim is testable when it forbids some observable outcome, and a test that cannot fail is not a test (Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, Popper).
- Fix the scoring before the round starts. Pre-registration exists to stop the analyst choosing the test after seeing the data (Nosek et al. 2018; Center for Open Science).
- ⚠️ House convention: one worker carries two mandates in one round. First attack the conclusions, then sweep the source classes for what remains after integration.
- The charter names the corpus version and the claim set, so a later reader can tell exactly what was tested.

## Decision rules

1. Copy each headline conclusion verbatim. A paraphrase becomes a new claim and cannot be scored against the original.
2. Use attack verbs: "attempt to show wrong, unsafe, or overreaching". Never "find gaps" or "review for quality".
3. Every claim ends in exactly one state: UNCHANGED, NARROWED, or OVERTURNED.
4. The round may read anything and add sources. It may not edit the corpus.
5. The saturation sweep reports SATURATED or RESIDUAL for each named source class.
6. If no headline list exists, write one and have the owner confirm it before the round starts.
7. State the time box and the evidence standard in the charter, not in a later message.

## Procedure

1. Freeze a copy of the corpus. Record its version, date, and file count.
2. Extract the headline conclusions from the summary and the conclusions section, verbatim.
3. Number the claims. Each gets an id used by the breaker, the scoring ledger, and every later amendment.
4. Write the attack mandate with the disposition vocabulary and the evidence standard.
5. Write the second mandate: assume all prior material is integrated; sweep each source class; report SATURATED or RESIDUAL.
6. Name the deliverable: one return per claim, with evidence and a corrected statement where needed.
7. State what the round must not touch: no edits, no deletions, no re-scoping.
8. Send the charter unchanged. If it changes mid-round, restart and record why.

Illustrations. A weak charter asked for "anything still missing" and produced new mechanisms. A strong charter listed each conclusion verbatim and asked for one disposition per claim; several broke and the rest came back narrowed or unchanged. In another round, the attack landed on a summary's claim that the work was read-only and its publication permanently gated by checking it against the corpus's own gate table, which gave irreversible gates a promotion rung. The table was the defect.

## Anti-patterns

- **Find mandate.** Produces new material and calls it verification.
- **Paraphrased claims.** The breaker attacks a claim nobody made.
- **Open scope.** "Review the whole corpus" spreads effort and tests nothing.
- **Rubric after returns.** Scoring invented once the results are visible.
- **Breaker with edit rights.** Independence dies at the first silent fix.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Corpus version recorded.
- [ ] Every headline conclusion listed verbatim and numbered.
- [ ] Attack mandate uses the three-state disposition vocabulary.
- [ ] Saturation sweep and its two states sit in the same charter.
- [ ] Evidence standard and time box stated.
- [ ] Prohibited actions listed.
- [ ] Owner confirmed the claim list.
