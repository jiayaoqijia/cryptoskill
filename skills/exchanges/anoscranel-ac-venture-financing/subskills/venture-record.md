# Venture record

## Standard

The venture record assembles the financing history in the form the cap table cannot: each round's terms and its negotiation context, each convertible instrument's conversion outcome, the pool's refresh history, the debt facilities with their maturities and what they presupposed, and the exit or status the sequence reached. Where the cap table is the current state, the record is the layered history that explains why the current state is what it is.

The record's discipline is projection-against-outcome. Each round carries the plan it was raised against — the milestones the money buys, the timeline the plan assumes — and the next entry records what actually happened. The comparison is the diligence artefact for the next round's investors, the archive for the founders, and the honest layer that prevents the retrospective rewriting of what was assumed.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- Record every round: type, price, amount, investors, and dated term sheet, with the pool treatment stated.
- Record every convertible's conversion event: the round it converted into, the conversion terms applied, the shares created.
- Record each facility: amount, maturity, covenants, and what happened at maturity.
- Attach each round's milestone plan and record the outcome against it in the next entry.
- Keep anti-dilution adjustments and their triggering rounds in the record, because they explain later percentages.
- Date every layer; amendments overwrite nothing.

## Procedure

1. List the rounds and instruments in chronological order with their terms.
2. Attach the cap table as of each round, not only the current one.
3. Record conversion outcomes as they happened, not as the instruments were described at issuance.
4. Record each facility's maturity outcome.
5. Attach each round's plan and, at the next layer, the realised outcome.
6. File the record beside the cap table as the ownership narrative.

## Anti-patterns

- Keeping only the current cap table and losing the history that explains it.
- Recording conversions at their issuance-time description rather than their realised outcome.
- Dropping closed facilities from the record, losing the maturity lessons.
- Rewriting a round's stated milestones after the outcome.
- No pool-refresh history, leaving later percentage questions unanswerable.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Rounds and instruments listed chronologically.
- [ ] Cap tables per round attached.
- [ ] Conversion outcomes recorded as realised.
- [ ] Facility outcomes recorded.
- [ ] Plans recorded beside outcomes.
- [ ] Every layer dated.
