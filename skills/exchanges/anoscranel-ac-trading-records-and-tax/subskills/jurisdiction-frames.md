# Jurisdiction frames

## Standard

Tax treatment is a function of jurisdiction, residency, instrument, and facts. No generic skill can resolve it, and a skill that pretends to is dangerous. What this leaf does instead is prepare the questions: the ones a professional will ask first, and the answers the record should already contain so the conversation starts at the analysis rather than at the paperwork.

The frames are structural. Residency determines the taxing jurisdiction and the reporting obligation. Source rules determine where income arises. Thresholds determine whether reporting is required at all. Instrument treatment determines whether the disposal is of an asset, a derivative, or something the statute does not name — and that last case is where professional judgement is indispensable.

Primary sources: <https://www.irs.gov/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Residency for the whole period is recorded, including any change mid-year.
- Each instrument class is listed with the question of how the jurisdiction treats it.
- Thresholds are recorded as questions, never as conclusions.
- Where the statute does not name the instrument, the record says so and flags it for the professional.
- Cross-border facts — accounts, issuers, currencies — are listed explicitly.
- The frame ends where advice begins; the boundary is stated in the record.

## Procedure

1. Record residency and any change during the period.
2. List instrument classes traded.
3. For each, record the jurisdiction's apparent treatment as a question.
4. Record thresholds and reporting obligations as questions.
5. List cross-border facts.
6. Write the handoff note listing the open questions.

## Anti-patterns

- Applying a rate from a blog post.
- Assuming the prior year's treatment without checking a change.
- Omitting a residency change.
- Treating a threshold as an allowance without confirmation.
- Presenting a conclusion where the statute is silent.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Residency recorded with any change.
- [ ] Instrument classes listed.
- [ ] Treatment recorded as questions per class.
- [ ] Thresholds recorded as questions.
- [ ] Cross-border facts listed.
- [ ] Handoff note written with open questions.

## Sources

- Tax administration guidance: <https://www.irs.gov/>
- Investor education on tax and reporting: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>
- Regulatory context for cross-border disclosure: <https://www.sec.gov/investor>

The frame is deliberately written as questions rather than answers because the alternative is worse: a generic answer applied to a specific jurisdiction is the most common source of expensive error in this area. A professional can answer questions quickly; they cannot easily undo a position taken on bad advice from a guide.
