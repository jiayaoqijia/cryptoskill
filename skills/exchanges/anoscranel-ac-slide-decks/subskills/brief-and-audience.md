# Brief and Audience

## Standard

- **Duarte, audience first**: Slide:ology opens on the audience's needs, not the presenter's material. The deck serves the person who must decide. <https://www.duarte.com/slideology/>
- **Kawasaki, deck as decision instrument**: a deck exists to reach agreement, so the brief names that agreement before any slide exists. <https://guykawasaki.com/the_102030_rule/> ⚠️ practitioner convention, not a standard.
- **Minto, the reader's question**: every section answers the question the named reader will actually ask. The decider's questions drive the outline. <https://www.barbaraminto.com/>
- **Title-and-decision trailer**: the title slide states the proposed action, and the closing slide restates it with owner and date. ⚠️ practitioner convention from decision-document desks.

## Decision rules

1. If an executive decides, the title slide carries the ask and the first content slide carries the recommendation. Background moves to the appendix.
2. If the room is a delivery team, lead with status and next actions. Past decisions become footnotes.
3. If the reader is a customer, lead with their problem in their words. Company history never opens the deck.
4. If the decider cannot attend, plan the leave-behind variant. Format budgets live in `subskills/density-and-reading.md`.
5. If the deck must serve more than one decider type, split it. One decider per deck.
6. If no decision or action follows the read, the deliverable is a report, not a deck. Act accordingly.

## Procedure

1. Write the one sentence naming the decision or action the deck must produce after the read.
2. Name the decider by role. "Stakeholders" is not a role.
3. List the three questions the decider will ask first. The outline must answer those three early.
4. Choose the register: exec (conclusions first), team (status first), customer (their problem first).
5. Draft the title slide as a verb sentence: "Approve the October launch", never "Launch plan".
6. Draft the closing slide: restated ask, owner, date.
7. Check every planned slide against the decider's three questions. A slide that answers none moves to the appendix or goes.
8. Pin a two-line brief at the top of the outline: decider, decision, register.

## Anti-patterns

- An agenda slide as first content. Fix: the recommendation is the first content.
- Company history opening for an external decider. Fix: swap slide one for their problem.
- One deck built for both exec and customer audiences. Fix: split the storyline, keep shared evidence slides.
- A title slide that names the topic, not the decision. Fix: rewrite it as a verb sentence.
- "Any questions?" as the last slide. Fix: restate ask, owner, and date.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Decider named by role.
- [ ] Decision sentence written and pinned at the top of the outline.
- [ ] Title slide states the ask as a verb sentence.
- [ ] Register chosen: exec, team, or customer.
- [ ] Closing slide restates ask, owner, and date.
- [ ] The brief fits on two lines.
