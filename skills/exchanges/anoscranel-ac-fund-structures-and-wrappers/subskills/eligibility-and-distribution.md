# Eligibility and distribution

## Standard

A fund's wrapper determines who is permitted to hold it, and that permission is set by the investor's residence and status rather than by the fund's quality. A vehicle registered for retail sale in one jurisdiction may be unavailable to residents of another, and a private vehicle may be restricted to investors meeting a definition of professional or qualified status that the investor either satisfies or does not.

Distribution rules follow the same logic. A fund marketed across borders must be registered or notified in each jurisdiction, and a fund that is not registered cannot be advertised even if a resident could legally buy it on request. That is why two investors in different countries can face different available products from the same manager, and why a fund's marketing material usually carries a jurisdictional legend rather than a single global claim.

Primary sources: <https://www.esma.europa.eu/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the investor's residence and status, since those determine eligibility.
- Record the jurisdictions in which the vehicle is registered for sale.
- Record the eligibility criteria: minimum investment, professional status, or investor category.
- Record the marketing restrictions that apply in each jurisdiction.
- Record the tax reporting the wrapper generates for the investor's jurisdiction.
- Record any restriction on transfers that would breach eligibility.

## Procedure

1. Record the investor's residence and category.
2. Record the vehicle's registrations and their dates.
3. Record the eligibility criteria and whether they are met.
4. Record the marketing and transfer restrictions.
5. Record the tax reporting obligations the wrapper creates.
6. Write the eligibility summary with the open questions named.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming a fund available to one investor is available to another.
- Ignoring the investor category that a private vehicle requires.
- Treating registration in one jurisdiction as global availability.
- Overlooking the tax reporting the wrapper generates.
- Assuming a transfer to another holder is unrestricted.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Investor residence and category recorded.
- [ ] Vehicle registrations recorded.
- [ ] Eligibility criteria recorded and tested.
- [ ] Marketing restrictions recorded.
- [ ] Tax reporting recorded.
- [ ] Transfer restrictions recorded.

The practical consequence is that a wrapper decision is often made by the investor's passport rather than by the portfolio: a strategy that exists only in a vehicle the investor cannot hold is not available, and the same manager's equivalent vehicle in another domicile may carry different protections. The record should state the available wrapper rather than the preferred one.
