# Segment disclosure

## Standard

Segment reporting exists so that a reader can see the parts of a business, and it is only as informative as the segmentation. When segments are amalgamated, a deteriorating unit can disappear inside a growing one; when they are redefined, the comparative figures are restated on the new basis, which removes the ability to compare across the change.

The questions are straightforward and answerable from the filings. Did the number of segments change, and what rationale was given? Were prior periods restated, and does the restatement preserve the old view anywhere? Which segment carries the capital expenditure and which carries the revenue? Is there an unallocated category that absorbs items the company does not wish to assign?

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.nber.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the segment structure and any change across periods.
- Record the stated rationale for a change.
- Record whether comparatives were restated and on what basis.
- Record the largest unallocated or corporate category.
- Record capital expenditure and asset allocation by segment.
- Record segment margins and their trend before and after a change.

## Procedure

1. Record the segment table across periods.
2. Record changes and stated rationales.
3. Record restatement treatment.
4. Record unallocated items and their size.
5. Allocate capital expenditure and assets where disclosed.
6. Write the questions with the periods cited.

## Anti-patterns

- Comparing segment margins across a definition change without restatement.
- Ignoring a growing unallocated category.
- Accepting a rationale without checking what it conceals.
- Reading segment revenue without segment assets.
- Treating one segment as comparable with another company's segment of the same name.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Structure and changes recorded.
- [ ] Rationale recorded.
- [ ] Restatement treatment recorded.
- [ ] Unallocated items recorded.
- [ ] Capital and assets allocated.
- [ ] Questions cited.

The tell that a segmentation change is doing work rather than reflecting a reorganisation is whether the number of segments falls while the business is described as growing more complex: a simpler structure in a more complicated company is a question, and the restated comparatives will usually show the unit that disappeared performing differently from the whole.

The cross-check that makes the analysis concrete is to compare the sum of segment margins with the consolidated margin over the same period: where the segments appear healthy and the total does not, the difference sits in the unallocated category, and that category's growth is the most direct evidence of what the segmentation is not showing.
