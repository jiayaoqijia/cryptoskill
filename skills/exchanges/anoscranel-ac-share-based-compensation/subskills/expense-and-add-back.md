# Expense and add-back

## Standard

Equity compensation is recognised as an expense in the income statement, and it is a non-cash expense, which is why it appears as an add-back in the cash-flow statement and as an exclusion in most adjusted earnings metrics. Both treatments are defensible on their own terms, and together they produce a gap between the reported result and the economic cost.

The arithmetic that closes the gap is straightforward: the expense is real to shareholders because it is paid in ownership, and the cash-flow statement's add-back restores the accounting figure without restoring the dilution. A company that reports an adjusted margin excluding compensation expense, and simultaneously buys back shares to offset the dilution, is spending cash on the offset and excluding the expense from the metric — which makes the offset visible and the cost invisible.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.fasb.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the compensation expense as reported, and its percentage of revenue.
- Record the add-back in the cash-flow statement and its amount.
- Record whether the adjusted metric excludes the expense, and by how much.
- Record the buyback spend in the same period, since it is the economic offset.
- Compare the buyback spend with the expense, which is the honest cost comparison.
- Record any change in the expense's trend across several periods.

## Procedure

1. Read the compensation note and the income statement.
2. Record the expense and its revenue share.
3. Record the cash-flow add-back.
4. Record the adjusted metric's treatment.
5. Record the buyback spend for the same period.
6. Compare the two numbers and write the finding.

## Anti-patterns

- Accepting the add-back as evidence the expense is not real.
- Reading the adjusted metric without the buyback that offsets it.
- Comparing compensation expense across companies with different grant types.
- Ignoring the trend, which shows whether the programme is expanding.
- Treating the cash-flow add-back as free cash flow available to holders.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Expense and revenue share recorded.
- [ ] Cash-flow add-back recorded.
- [ ] Adjusted metric treatment recorded.
- [ ] Buyback spend recorded.
- [ ] Expense compared with buyback spend.
- [ ] Trend recorded.

A further check is whether the expense is stable as a percentage of revenue or rising: a programme that was modest at listing and has grown to a material share of revenue is a compensation model that has changed, and the adjusted metric that excludes it will have flattered the trend throughout.
