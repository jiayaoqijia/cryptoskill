# Deal record

## Standard

The deal record assembles what the transaction actually said and did: the structure and consideration, the model with its synergy schedule and assumptions, the purchase price allocation, the indemnification architecture, the working capital adjustment as executed, and the regulatory timeline as it ran. It exists so that a post-closing review measures the deal against what was decided, not against what is remembered.

Its honesty rests on dating. The synergy schedule was a forecast made at signing; the realised synergy report is a different document and belongs beside it, not over it. The same applies to the closing adjustment: the peg was set before the outcome was known. The record keeps assumptions and outcomes in dated layers, and the comparison between them is the integration review that most organisations say they will do and rarely do.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the structure, consideration, exchange ratio, and financing terms with dates.
- Attach the model's synergy schedule and the accretion/dilution result at signing.
- Attach the purchase price allocation, preliminary and final, with the measurement-period changes.
- Record the indemnification terms as signed, not as later remembered in disputes.
- Record the working capital adjustment executed — estimate, statement, objection, final.
- Date every layer; realised-outcome reports are additions, never overwrites of the forecasts.

## Procedure

1. Record terms and structure with dates.
2. Attach the signing-date model: synergies, accretion/dilution, assumptions.
3. Attach the allocation versions in sequence.
4. Record the reps and indemnity architecture as signed.
5. Record the closing adjustment as executed through its final number.
6. File with dated sections for the post-closing reviews to come.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording the synergy schedule after the outcome and calling it the plan.
- Keeping only the final allocation and discarding the preliminary.
- Editing signed terms during a dispute.
- No integration-review entry twelve months later.
- Confusing the legal file with the execution record.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Terms and structure dated.
- [ ] Signing-date model attached.
- [ ] Allocation versions in sequence.
- [ ] Indemnity architecture recorded as signed.
- [ ] Closing adjustment recorded through the final number.
- [ ] Post-closing review entries scheduled.

The record also earns its keep in the next negotiation: the realised-versus-planned synergy comparison, the disputes that arose from the indemnity language, and the wrinkles in the working capital definition are the evidence that turns the next deal's assumptions from guesses into estimates. A team that closes deals without keeping this layer learns nothing from having done them.
