# Tax-equivalent yield

## Standard

A tax-exempt yield is worth more than it says, and how much more depends on the investor's marginal rate. The conversion is the tax-equivalent yield: the exempt yield divided by one minus the marginal rate. At a high bracket, a three percent exempt yield can be equivalent to a taxable yield well above four percent, which is why the market clears at yields that look low against taxable bonds.

Three complications decide whether the comparison is honest. The investor's own marginal rate is the relevant one, including state and local taxes where the bond is exempt at those levels too. The alternative may itself be tax-advantaged, such as a retirement account where all income compounds untaxed. And the comparison should hold credit and maturity constant, because a lower yield on a safer bond is not a tax effect.

Primary sources: <https://www.irs.gov/> and <https://www.msrb.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the exempt yield and the investor's marginal federal rate.
- Compute the tax-equivalent yield and show the calculation.
- Record state and local tax treatment where the bond is exempt at those levels.
- Record whether the comparison alternative sits in a tax-advantaged account.
- Hold credit quality and maturity constant across the comparison.
- Record the alternative-minimum-tax position where it applies, as a question for a professional.

## Procedure

1. Record the exempt yield and the marginal rate.
2. Compute the tax-equivalent yield.
3. Record state and local treatment.
4. Record the account type of the alternative.
5. Compare like credit and maturity.
6. Write the comparison with the caveats stated.

## Anti-patterns

- Comparing a municipal yield with a corporate yield without the tax conversion.
- Using a headline top rate that does not apply to the investor.
- Ignoring state tax where the bond is exempt in-state only.
- Comparing across different credit qualities.
- Forgetting that a retirement account makes the tax exemption worthless.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Exempt yield and marginal rate recorded.
- [ ] Tax-equivalent yield computed and shown.
- [ ] State and local treatment recorded.
- [ ] Account type recorded.
- [ ] Credit and maturity held constant.
- [ ] Caveats stated.

The comparison also has a break-even interpretation that is often more useful than the conversion itself: at a given pair of yields, there is a marginal tax rate above which the exempt bond wins and below which the taxable one does, and stating that threshold tells an investor directly whether their own bracket puts them on the right side of it.
