# Policy exposure record

## Standard

The policy record states the measure, its date and version, the channel through which it reaches the investment, the quantified exposure, and the trigger that would change the assessment. The channel is the essential field: a tariff reaches a cost line, a control reaches a supplier, a sanction reaches a payment route, and a screening rule reaches the buyer universe, and each is quantified differently.

The record also separates what is in force from what is announced or proposed. Those are different exposures with different probabilities, and a record that merges them cannot be reviewed when a proposal is withdrawn or amended. Stating the status, the effective date, and the version examined is what makes a later review possible rather than a matter of recollection.

Primary sources: <https://www.wto.org/> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the measure, the issuing authority, and the date.
- Record whether it is in force, announced, or proposed.
- Record the channel: cost, revenue, access, ownership, or payment route.
- Record the quantified exposure with its assumptions.
- Record the trigger that would change the assessment.
- Record the version of any list or schedule examined.

## Procedure

1. Record the measure and its status.
2. Record the channel and the affected entity.
3. Quantify the exposure with stated assumptions.
4. Record second-order channels.
5. Set the trigger and the review date.
6. File the record and reference it from the research note.

## Anti-patterns

- Merging proposed and enacted measures.
- Recording a policy without the channel it reaches.
- Quantifying without the assumption stated.
- Omitting the list version for a control or sanction.
- Setting no trigger, so the record is never revisited.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Measure, authority, date recorded.
- [ ] Status recorded.
- [ ] Channel recorded.
- [ ] Exposure quantified with assumptions.
- [ ] Second-order channels recorded.
- [ ] Trigger and review date set.

The status field earns its place when a measure is challenged: an announced tariff that is stayed by a court, or a listing that is delayed, changes the exposure without changing the headline, and a record that captured status and date can be updated accurately while one that captured only the measure has to be rebuilt from news.

Keeping the record attached to the research note rather than in a separate policy file is what makes it used: a reader assessing the investment sees the exposure beside the thesis, and a later review of the thesis automatically revisits the policy assumption rather than leaving it in a folder nobody opens.
