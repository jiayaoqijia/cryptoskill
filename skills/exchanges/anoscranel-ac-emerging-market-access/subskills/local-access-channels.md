# Local access channels

## Standard

Holding local shares requires a channel, and the channels differ. Some markets allow a foreign investor to open a local account through a local broker after registration; some require approval or a licence; some operate a quota system that allocates capacity to institutions; and some restrict certain sectors or issuers to domestic holders regardless of the general rule.

Each channel carries a different operational burden. A registered direct account gives voting and corporate-action rights but requires local tax registration, local settlement arrangements, and compliance with reporting obligations. A quota-based channel gives access under an allocation that can be reduced or withdrawn, and the allocation is often the binding constraint rather than the capital.

Primary sources: <https://www.imf.org/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the channel and the registration or approval it requires.
- Record any quota, its holder, and whether it is transferable.
- Record sector or issuer restrictions that apply to foreign holders.
- Record the reporting obligations attached to the channel.
- Record the rights the channel confers: voting, corporate actions, and information.
- Record the time and cost to establish the channel, and to exit it.

## Procedure

1. Identify the available channels in the market.
2. Record the requirements of each.
3. Record restrictions and quotas.
4. Record reporting and rights.
5. Record establishment time and cost.
6. Write the access summary with the preferred channel.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming the channel available to one investor is available to all.
- Ignoring sector restrictions that override the general rule.
- Treating a quota as permanent capacity.
- Overlooking the reporting burden as a cost.
- Choosing the channel on cost alone when rights differ.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Channels identified.
- [ ] Requirements recorded.
- [ ] Restrictions and quotas recorded.
- [ ] Reporting and rights recorded.
- [ ] Cost and time recorded.
- [ ] Summary written.

The channel decision also determines what happens in a corporate action: a holder through a quota may receive the proceeds of a tender in the local currency with no route to convert, while a receipt holder receives a payment in the foreign market. That difference, rather than the fee, is often what decides the route.


The channel's exit deserves the same attention as its entry: closing a local account, liquidating a quota allocation, and repatriating the proceeds are separate processes, often handled by different departments, and a channel that is quick to open can take months to close. Recording both timings is what makes the comparison honest.
