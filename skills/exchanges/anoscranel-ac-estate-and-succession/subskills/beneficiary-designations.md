# Beneficiary designations

## Standard

A beneficiary designation is a contract instruction: the institution pays the named person, regardless of what the will says. That is the whole of the principle, and it is the source of most succession errors, because designations are set once and rarely reviewed while the will is redrafted and discussed.

The review is mechanical. For each account and policy, record who is primary, who is contingent, the date the form was signed, and whether the institution has the current version. Then compare the set against the intent: the people named should be the people the owner currently wants, at the proportions intended, with a contingent who can receive if the primary cannot.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.irs.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Every account with a designation is listed with primary, contingent, proportions, and the form date.
- A designation older than the last major family change is flagged.
- The set of designations is compared with the will; any conflict is recorded.
- Where the institution cannot confirm the current form, that is recorded as a gap.
- Contingent beneficiaries are required, not optional; their absence is recorded.
- Changes are made by the institution's form, not by letter or note.

## Procedure

1. List every account and policy with a designation.
2. Pull the institution's record of the current form and its date.
3. Record primary, contingent, and proportions.
4. Compare with the will and with current intent.
5. Flag stale forms and missing contingents.
6. Record the actions taken and the dates.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming the will controls an account with a designation.
- Relying on a copy of a form rather than the institution's record.
- Naming one primary with no contingent.
- Leaving a form from before a marriage, divorce, or birth.
- Changing a designation without confirming receipt.

## Done checklist

- [ ] All designations listed with dates.
- [ ] Primary, contingent, and proportions recorded.
- [ ] Conflicts with the will noted.
- [ ] Stale forms and missing contingents flagged.
- [ ] Institution confirmation obtained.
- [ ] Actions and dates recorded.

The most valuable habit is a signed copy of each form kept with the succession record, dated, alongside the institution's confirmation that it is the operative version. When a dispute arises the question is always which form was on file, and the answer is only available if it was captured.
