# Litigation funding

## Standard

Litigation funding is a third party paying the costs of a case in exchange for a share of any recovery. It allows a claimant without the balance sheet to pursue a claim, and it transfers part of the upside to the funder. The terms decide how much: the funder's share, whether it is calculated on gross or net recovery, who bears a costs award if the case fails, and whether the funder can control settlement decisions.

The funding arrangement also shapes the case's trajectory. A funder with a portfolio of claims prefers early settlements with predictable outcomes; one funding a single large claim may prefer to pursue a judgment. Reading who controls the settlement decision, and what the funder's return requirement is, tells you more about the likely path than the merits do.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the funder's share, its basis, and the priority of its return.
- Record who bears adverse costs and any insurance against them.
- Record the settlement control provisions and any consent rights.
- Record the funder's return expectation and any hurdle.
- Record the termination rights and what happens if the funder withdraws.
- Record the disclosure obligations to the court or to the class.

## Procedure

1. Record the funding terms and the funder's share.
2. Record the costs allocation and any insurance.
3. Record settlement control and consent rights.
4. Record the funder's return requirement.
5. Record termination and withdrawal provisions.
6. Write the funding summary.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading the funder's share without its basis.
- Ignoring who bears an adverse costs award.
- Assuming the claimant controls settlement.
- Overlooking a funder's right to withdraw.
- Treating funding as costless to the recovery.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Funder's share and basis recorded.
- [ ] Costs allocation recorded.
- [ ] Settlement control recorded.
- [ ] Return requirement recorded.
- [ ] Termination recorded.
- [ ] Summary written.

The comparison worth making is between the funded recovery and the unfunded one: a funder taking a third of the gross recovery in exchange for bearing the costs leaves the claimant with two thirds of a larger expected value versus all of a smaller one, and the crossover depends on the probability of success and the cost of pursuing it. Writing both outcomes side by side makes the arrangement legible.
