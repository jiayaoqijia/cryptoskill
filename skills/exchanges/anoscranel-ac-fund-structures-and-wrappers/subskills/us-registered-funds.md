# US registered funds

## Standard

A registered fund operates under a statutory framework that constrains what it can hold and how much it can borrow. The diversification test limits the share of assets that can sit in any one issuer; the concentration policy restricts positions in any one industry; and the leverage provisions restrict borrowing and the issuance of senior securities, which is why a registered fund's use of leverage is usually indirect and disclosed.

The practical consequence is that the wrapper, not the manager's preference, sets the boundaries. A manager who wants concentrated positions or meaningful leverage must use a different vehicle, and a fund that appears to hold a concentrated portfolio is either operating within an exception or is not the wrapper it claims. Reading the fundamental policies in the statement of additional information is how the constraints become visible.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Record the fund's diversification status: diversified or non-diversified.
- Record the concentration policy and the industry limits it sets.
- Record the leverage and borrowing provisions, and any senior securities outstanding.
- Record the fundamental policies that can only be changed by shareholder vote.
- Record the fund's use of derivatives and how the framework treats them.
- Record the distribution and fee structure, which the framework also constrains.

## Procedure

1. Read the statement of additional information for the fundamental policies.
2. Record the diversification and concentration limits.
3. Record the leverage and borrowing provisions.
4. Record derivative usage and its treatment.
5. Record the fee and distribution structure.
6. Write the summary with the constraints named.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming a registered fund can hold whatever the manager chooses.
- Ignoring the difference between diversified and non-diversified status.
- Overlooking derivatives, which can create effective leverage within the limits.
- Treating a fundamental policy as changeable without a vote.
- Comparing a registered fund's leverage with a private vehicle's as if the constraints were similar.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Diversification status recorded.
- [ ] Concentration policy recorded.
- [ ] Leverage provisions recorded.
- [ ] Fundamental policies recorded.
- [ ] Derivative use recorded.
- [ ] Fee structure recorded.

A further consequence of the framework is that changes to fundamental policy require a shareholder vote, which makes them slow and visible: a fund that intends to shift strategy materially must either work within its existing policies or seek approval, and the absence of a vote is evidence that the shift is within the boundaries rather than outside them.
