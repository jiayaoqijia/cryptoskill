# Non-GAAP bridges

## Standard

A non-GAAP figure starts from the reported number and subtracts items the company considers unrepresentative. The reconciliation discloses each adjustment, which makes the exercise readable: the question is not whether the adjustments exist but whether they recur. An item excluded in every one of the last eight quarters is, in substance, an operating cost that the company prefers not to count.

The second question is what the adjusted figure is used for. When a company's guidance, covenants, or compensation targets are defined on the adjusted measure, the adjustments are no longer presentational: they determine behaviour and pay. That is why the bridge — the table showing each step from reported to adjusted — is the document to read, rather than the headline figure it produces.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Record the reported figure and the adjusted figure for each period.
- Record each adjustment, its amount, and whether it recurred in prior periods.
- Record how many consecutive periods each adjustment has appeared in.
- Record whether guidance, covenants, or compensation use the adjusted measure.
- Record the adjustments that are cash costs versus non-cash.
- Record the cumulative size of recurring adjustments as a share of reported profit.

## Procedure

1. Pull the reconciliation from the release or the filing.
2. List each adjustment with its amount.
3. Check each against the prior periods.
4. Record which measures govern guidance and compensation.
5. Compute the cumulative share of recurring items.
6. Write the questions the pattern raises.

## Anti-patterns

- Accepting the adjusted figure without the bridge.
- Treating a recurring adjustment as a one-off because it is labelled so.
- Ignoring which measure the company's own targets use.
- Comparing adjusted figures across companies with different definitions.
- Assuming a non-cash adjustment has no economic cost.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Reported and adjusted figures recorded.
- [ ] Adjustments listed with amounts.
- [ ] Recurrence checked per period.
- [ ] Use in guidance and compensation recorded.
- [ ] Cash versus non-cash classified.
- [ ] Cumulative share computed.

The bridge also reveals what a company considers its operating reality: when share-based compensation is excluded, the company is stating that it does not regard the cost as real, and the counter-argument is arithmetic rather than philosophical, since the expense represents claims on future ownership that existing holders bear through dilution.
