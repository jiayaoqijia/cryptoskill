Never screen a narrative token with buyback/burn filters, and never "hold" a family-B winner.

| | **Family A — value accrual** | **Family B — attention/narrative** |
|---|---|---|

| 7 | **Product live & earning BEFORE the token** | ≥6 months of live volume/fees pre-TGE | HYPE perps DEX live 18 months pre-TGE; MORPHO 2.4 years pre-transferability | BLAST "capital before a network" → TVL −95%; STRK token before traction; OM +300x before utility |
| 8 | **Low FDV/mcap at listing** | FDV ≤~3x circulating mcap | HYPE ~2.5–3x; FARTCOIN/SPX ~1x | STRK ~10x; ENA ~10.6x; MORPHO ~8.9x |

**The one-line lesson from the failures:** big revenue **without** a mechanism to the token does
nothing (AETHIR, MORPHO, PUMP), and a big airdrop **with** insider overhang does nothing either
(PENGU, EIGEN). It has to be both.

## Family B — attention/narrative winners

Verified anchor (CoinGecko, 27 Sep 2026): **TRUMP** launched 17–18 Jan 2025, ATH **$73.43 on
19 Jan 2025 — two days after launch**, now **$2.13 (−97% from ATH)**, circulating 282M/1B
(**float 28%**), −71.8% over 12 months. ~400x from launch to ATH, then a permanent bleed.

So the repeatable shape is: **thin float + a violent attention spike = repricing to the upside**,
then insiders (72% of supply) distribute for a year. Winning here is a timing problem, not a
quality problem:

- Early-visible signals: attention arriving from *outside* crypto (celebrity/political/cultural
  event), float still small, social velocity accelerating, exchange listing not yet done.
- Time-to-fade is days-to-weeks, not months. The listing announcement and the first
  "big-name mention" are late, not early.
- Mandatory in the write-up: an explicit **exit rule** (time stop and price stop), because
  hold-and-forget is structurally wrong for this family.
- Skip the family-A score entirely. A 28%-float insider-heavy token can still be a valid *trade*.

Deep research on family B (FARTCOIN / SPX6900 / POPCAT / WIF / BONK + failure cases LIBRA, MELANIA,
HAWK, with thresholds and fade timelines) was dispatched 27 Sep 2026 but **had not returned when

## The screen

`~/.hermes/scripts/winner_scan.py` (built 27 Sep 2026, ~15–90 s, read-only) prints three sections
and dumps `~/.hermes/data/winner_scan_latest.json`:

- **[A]** DeFiLlama screen: `/overview/fees?excludeTotalDataChart=true&excludeTotalDataChartBreakdown=true`
  joined by name with `/protocols` (which carries `mcap`, `gecko_id`, `twitter`, `category`) →
  mcap $8–250M and revenue-30d ≥ $50k → sort by revenue/mcap. `api.llama.fi/protocol/<slug>` gives
  the gecko_id directly, which avoids the CoinGecko search endpoint entirely.
- **[B]** GMGN Solana low caps: `market trending --chain sol --interval 24h --order-by volume
  --min-marketcap 5000000 --max-marketcap 80000000 --min-holder-count 5000 --filter renounced
  --filter frozen`.
- **[C]** Actual concentration for the top 4: `token holders --limit 12 --order-by amount_percentage`,
  summing top-1/5/10 — because section B's `top_10_holder_rate` cannot be trusted (below).
- **[D]** Printed guard rails so the writer cannot skip them: no value-accrual → skip; undisclosed
  vesting → exclude; wallet #1 ≥15% unexplained → exclude; real yield = the share that reaches the
  buyback, not gross fees.

## Traps (all hit on 27 Sep 2026)

1. **`top_10_holder_rate` understates concentration.** GMGN security reported **14.9%** for GP while
   `token holders` showed **wallet #1 alone holding 18.55%** (10,880,467 GP received via **11
   transfer-ins**, not market buys), and a third-party listing page put **top-5 at 42.6%**. The
   aggregate rate evidently excludes certain addresses. Always pull per-address holders and report
   wallet #1 explicitly; note that a wallet funded by transfers is probably treasury/vesting/burn —
   and that unexplained = exclude, never "assume safe".
2. **Gross revenue ≠ yield to holders.** GP's 14.64% revenue/mcap becomes ~**1.1%/month** once only
   the 7.6% buyback share is counted. Always compute the routed share separately. CARDS is the
   cautionary case: $10.77M reported revenue but only ~3.4% of net reached the token, with 72% of
   supply insider-held.
3. **GMGN CLI rate-limit bans escalate.** The CLI returns
   `HTTP 429 … RATE_LIMIT_BANNED … resets at … (~Ns remaining)` and non-JSON stdout; repeated
   requests extend the ban 5 s up to 5 minutes. Parse `~(\d+)s remaining`, sleep, retry ≤2 times, and
   space calls ≥1.2 s. A banned response looks like garbage — check that stdout starts with `{`
   before `json.loads`.
4. **`market trending` requires `--interval`** (1m/5m/1h/6h/24h); without it the CLI errors.
   Useful fields: `creation_timestamp`/`open_timestamp` (not `created_timestamp`), `market_cap`,
   `liquidity`, `holder_count`, `price_change_percent`, `smart_degen_count`, `renowned_count`,
   `rug_ratio`, `bundler_rate`, `entrapment_ratio`, `top_10_holder_rate`, `launchpad_platform`,
   `twitter_username`. `token holders` max `--limit` is 100.
5. **CoinGecko 429s when parallel subagents share the IP.** Prefer DeFiLlama (`gecko_id`) and
   DexScreener for identity/price; keep CoinGecko for the couple of numbers only it has.
6. **Symbol collisions must be resolved before any analysis.** Two Solana tokens are named
   "Graphite/GP": the real one `31k88G5Mq7ptbRDf3AM13HAq6wRQHXHikR8hik7wPygk` (twitter
   @GraphiteProto, live Orca/Meteora pools, official site) and a dead `2NBuL9Lxx6hVPbpP8S4J7hV1LfaHwQ4eoSUXfEYpbonk`
   (zero pools, no socials). Publish the address table in the report itself — this user trades from
   the chain themselves and will copy an address.
7. **Delegated research is a lead, not a fact.** A subagent's LBank-sourced "top-5 = 42.6%" directly
   contradicted GMGN's 14.9%; resolving that conflict produced the decisive finding of the report.
   Spot-check the numbers that change the verdict with your own tool calls before publishing.

## Reporting shape (accepted by the user, 27 Sep 2026)

Full deep dive = Obsidian note (`FULL DEEP DIVE - Pola Winner Low Cap …md`) delivered as a file,
with the chat message carrying: verdict → the pattern table → the scored candidates → the action
table (size, entry trigger, invalidation) → the "JANGAN" list → uncertainties. Every unverifiable
number is marked `[?]`; the rejects are printed **with the number that disqualified them** — that is
what makes the screen read as real rather than as narrative.
