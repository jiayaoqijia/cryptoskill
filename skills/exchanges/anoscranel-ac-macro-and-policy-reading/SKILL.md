# Macro and policy reading

## Overview

Macro data reaches markets as a release, a revision, and a narrative, in that order of reliability. The release is a number with a definition; the revision is the same number corrected later, often by more than the market moved on the first print; and the narrative is what everyone says it means. An investor who reads only the third is trading a story about a number they never saw.

This skill is about reading the first two, and about reading central-bank communication for what it commits to rather than for what it suggests. It does not forecast, and it treats every macro view as a claim with a falsifier.

Primary sources: <https://fred.stlouisfed.org/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## When to use

- Before acting on a macro release or a central-bank decision.
- When a narrative about the economy is driving a portfolio decision.
- When building a monitoring set of indicators.
- When a past macro call is being reviewed.

## When NOT to use

- For instrument mechanics (use ac-fixed-income-and-rates).
- For a company's own fundamentals (use ac-equity-research).
- For asset allocation policy (use ac-investment-policy-statement).

## Status and honest limits

Macro data is revised, definitions change, and the relationship between an indicator and an asset price is unstable across regimes. A macro view is therefore a hypothesis with a short half-life, and the record must say when it expires. Nothing here predicts the economy; the value is in reading the data accurately and knowing what would change your mind.
