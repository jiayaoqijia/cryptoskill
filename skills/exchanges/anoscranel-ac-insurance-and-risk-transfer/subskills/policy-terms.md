# Policy terms

## Standard

The coverage summary describes what the policy is for; the terms describe what it actually does. Three clauses decide most disputes: the definitions, because they fix what the words mean; the exclusions, because they remove perils that were implied; and the conditions, because they set the duties the insured must perform before a claim is paid — notice, mitigation, proof, and cooperation.

Reading them is not legal work, but reading them for a claim is. This leaf prepares the reader: it lists what to look for and records the findings, so that a broker, adviser, or lawyer can be asked a precise question rather than asked to review the whole document.

Primary sources: <https://www.naic.org/> and <https://www.usa.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the definitions of the terms the coverage depends on.
- List the exclusions in the insured's own words, and mark those that could apply.
- Record the conditions: notice deadlines, mitigation duties, documentation requirements.
- Note the clauses that change the policy after a claim, such as repair requirements or replacement valuation.
- Flag any term whose meaning is unclear rather than interpreting it.
- Record the date of the policy version examined.

## Procedure

1. Obtain the full policy document, not the summary.
2. Extract the key definitions.
3. List the exclusions and map them to the risks being transferred.
4. Record the conditions and their deadlines.
5. Note valuation and settlement clauses.
6. Record the findings and the questions for a professional.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading only the summary page.
- Skimming exclusions because they are long.
- Missing notice deadlines in the conditions.
- Interpreting an ambiguous clause without recording the uncertainty.
- Examining an outdated policy version.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Full policy obtained and version dated.
- [ ] Key definitions recorded.
- [ ] Exclusions listed and mapped to risks.
- [ ] Conditions and deadlines recorded.
- [ ] Valuation and settlement clauses noted.
- [ ] Questions for a professional listed.

The exclusions deserve their own list because they are the clauses that convert an apparent coverage into an actual gap. Writing them in plain words, mapped to the risks being transferred, turns a dense document into a set of specific questions that a broker can answer quickly.
