# Cost-basis methods

## Standard

Cost basis is what a disposal is measured against, and when identical assets are bought at different prices the method chosen decides which cost is matched to which sale. FIFO matches the oldest purchase first; LIFO the newest; specific identification lets the seller designate the lot. Each produces a different gain on the same trades, and each jurisdiction permits a different subset.

The discipline is not the choice but its consistency. A method applied inconsistently produces a record that cannot be defended and a pack that a professional must rebuild. The method is chosen in advance, documented with its jurisdiction and its effective date, and applied to every disposal in the period.

Primary sources: <https://www.irs.gov/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- The method is chosen before the first disposal and documented with its jurisdiction and date.
- Specific identification requires a contemporaneous designation; a later reconstruction is not identification.
- Where a jurisdiction requires a method, the record states that it is required, not chosen.
- The method is applied to every disposal in the period; mixing methods within a period is a finding.
- Lot-level records are kept where the method requires them.
- A change of method is recorded as a change, with its effective date and the jurisdiction's permission.

## Procedure

1. Identify the jurisdiction and the methods it permits.
2. Choose the method and record the choice with its date.
3. Verify the record has lot-level detail where the method needs it.
4. Apply the method to every disposal in the period.
5. Test one disposal by hand against the record.
6. Record any change of method separately.

## Anti-patterns

- Choosing the method after seeing which produces the smaller gain.
- Applying specific identification retroactively.
- Mixing methods across lots within a period.
- Failing to record lot-level detail for a method that requires it.
- Changing method without recording the change.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Jurisdiction and permitted methods identified.
- [ ] Method chosen and dated.
- [ ] Lot-level detail present where required.
- [ ] Method applied uniformly to the period.
- [ ] One disposal tested by hand.
- [ ] Any change of method recorded separately.

The practical test of a basis method is whether a reviewer can take any single disposal and trace it to a specific purchase without asking a question. If the trail requires memory, the method is documented but not applied, and the gap will surface exactly when it is most expensive to fix.
