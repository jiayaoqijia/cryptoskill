# Alternatives in portfolio

## Standard

The case for alternatives rests on three claims: diversification from public markets, protection against inflation, and a premium for illiquidity. Each is testable, and the tests are uncomfortable. Diversification measured with marks understates the correlation that appears when the alternatives are pressed to sell. Inflation protection depends on the asset and the regime. The illiquidity premium is real in the long run and fails at the moment an investor needs it.

The honest analysis therefore reports what the alternative contributed historically, using the best available transaction-based evidence rather than mark-based statistics, and states what would have to be true for the contribution to repeat.

Primary sources: <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing> and <https://www.sec.gov/investor>.

## Decision rules

- Each of the three claims is tested separately with its own evidence.
- Diversification is assessed on transaction-based returns where any exist, not on marks alone.
- Inflation behaviour is recorded across at least two distinct inflation regimes.
- The illiquidity premium is recorded with the conditions under which it has not been paid.
- The alternatives' cost — fees, lockups, operational burden — is recorded in the same table as the contribution.
- The role of the allocation is stated in one sentence before any sizing discussion.

## Procedure

1. List the claims being made for the allocation.
2. Test each with the best available evidence.
3. Record the conditions under which each claim failed.
4. Record the costs alongside the contributions.
5. Write the role of the allocation in one sentence.
6. Record what would falsify the role.

## Anti-patterns

- Accepting mark-based volatility as risk.
- Testing inflation protection on one regime.
- Quoting diversification benefits without the stress correlation.
- Comparing a net-of-fee alternative with a gross public-market return.
- Justifying the allocation with a chart of the last decade.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Claims listed.
- [ ] Each tested with evidence.
- [ ] Failure conditions recorded.
- [ ] Costs tabulated with contributions.
- [ ] Role stated in one sentence.
- [ ] Falsifier written.

The contribution test is comparative, not absolute: the alternative is measured against the liquid asset it displaces, net of fees, over the same period. An allocation that diversifies but underperforms by more than the diversification is worth has not earned its place, and the record should show both sides of that arithmetic rather than the favourable one.

Sizing follows from the role, not from the sales material. A holding whose role is 'reduce drawdown in a specific stress' is sized by the drawdown it actually reduced in that stress, not by its share of a model portfolio. Where the evidence for the role is thin, the size is small or the role is empty, and saying so in the record is more valuable than a larger allocation.
