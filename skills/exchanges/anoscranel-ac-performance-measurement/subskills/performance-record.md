# Performance record

## Standard

The performance record states the return, its type, the benchmark, the period, and the attribution, and it is written so that two records from different periods can be compared without re-deriving the method. Those fields are what allow a series of results to become evidence: a return quoted without its type and benchmark cannot be added to anything.

The record also holds the context that a later reader needs to interpret the number: what the market did, what the policy was, and whether the period contained a stress episode. Without that context, a return of five percent in a year when the benchmark fell is indistinguishable from one in a year when it rose fifteen, and a rolling series that mixes the two is not a track record.

Primary sources: <https://www.cfainstitute.org/> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the period, the return, and the return type.
- The benchmark is stated with its construction and any change during the period.
- The attribution is recorded by component, with the segment definition.
- The risk figures are recorded alongside: volatility, drawdown depth and duration.
- The market context is recorded in one or two sentences.
- Records are appended per period and never restated without a dated amendment.

## Procedure

1. State the period and the return type.
2. Record the return and the benchmark return.
3. Record the attribution components.
4. Record the risk figures.
5. Record the market and policy context.
6. File and date the record.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording a return without its type.
- Changing the benchmark mid-series without a note.
- Omitting risk figures so the return stands alone.
- Restating past periods after a method change instead of adding an amendment.
- Recording the result without the context that explains it.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Period and return type recorded.
- [ ] Return and benchmark recorded.
- [ ] Attribution recorded.
- [ ] Risk figures recorded.
- [ ] Context recorded.
- [ ] Entry dated and appended.

A second use of the record is against the future: writing what was expected at the start of the period, and what would have counted as a good outcome, turns the later review into a comparison with a stated expectation rather than a narrative written after the fact to fit the result.
