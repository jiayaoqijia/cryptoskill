# Follow-on and blocks

## Standard

A follow-on offering sells additional shares, either by the company to raise capital or by existing holders to sell down. It is usually marketed overnight and priced at a discount to the last traded price, because the buyer is being asked to absorb a large quantity quickly, and the discount is the compensation for that immediacy rather than a judgment about value.

A block trade is the same economics in a single transaction: a holder sells a large position to a bank, which resells it to institutions at a negotiated discount. The concession is measurable, and it is the right way to read the event: a block priced two percent below the market is a statement about how much supply the market would absorb at that moment, and a block that clears at a narrower discount suggests deeper demand.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.finra.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the offer type: marketed follow-on, overnight, or bought deal.
- Record the size relative to free float and to average daily volume.
- Record the pricing discount to the last trade and to the volume-weighted average.
- Record whether the shares are primary (new) or secondary (existing).
- Record any lock-up the issuer or the seller accepts afterwards.
- Record the use of proceeds for a primary offering.

## Procedure

1. Read the offering announcement or the block print.
2. Record type, size, and discount.
3. Record primary versus secondary.
4. Record any new lock-up.
5. Record proceeds and use where primary.
6. Write the summary with the concession interpreted.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading the discount as a valuation signal rather than a liquidity price.
- Measuring size against market capitalisation instead of free float.
- Confusing a secondary sell-down with new capital raised.
- Ignoring the lock-up the seller may accept after a block.
- Treating an overnight deal as if it were marketed.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Offer type and size recorded.
- [ ] Discount to last trade and VWAP recorded.
- [ ] Primary versus secondary recorded.
- [ ] New lock-up recorded.
- [ ] Proceeds and use recorded.
- [ ] Concession interpreted in writing.

The concession also has a memory: a series of blocks clearing at widening discounts is a market telling you that supply is outrunning demand for that name, and a single block at a narrow discount in an otherwise quiet tape suggests a holder exited into genuine appetite. Reading the sequence rather than the individual print is where the information is.
