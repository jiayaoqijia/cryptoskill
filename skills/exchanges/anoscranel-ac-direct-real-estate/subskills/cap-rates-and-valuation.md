# Cap rates and valuation

## Standard

The capitalisation rate divides net operating income by value, and it is best understood as the return an unlevered buyer accepts at current income, before growth. A low rate implies either low required returns or high expected growth; a high rate implies the reverse or a riskier income stream. The rate is not a market constant, and comparing two properties' rates is only meaningful when the income streams are similarly stable.

The relationship to financing matters. When borrowing costs approach the capitalisation rate, an unlevered return cannot support the debt, and the resulting negative leverage is a signal about the price rather than about the property. The record should state the rate, the financing cost, and whether leverage is positive or negative at the current terms, because a valuation built on an assumption of positive leverage can fail without any change in the property.

Primary sources: <https://www.federalreserve.gov/> and <https://fred.stlouisfed.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the cap rate, the NOI it is applied to, and the date.
- Record the comparable transactions behind the rate and their similarity.
- Record the financing cost and whether leverage is positive or negative.
- Record the growth assumption implied by the rate relative to the risk-free rate.
- Record the expense and capital expenditure assumptions inside the NOI.
- Record how the rate has moved for the property type over a stated period.

## Procedure

1. Record the cap rate, NOI, and date.
2. Record comparables and their similarity to the subject.
3. Record the financing cost and the leverage sign.
4. Derive the implied growth assumption.
5. Record the NOI assumptions inside the rate.
6. Write the valuation summary with its implied growth.

## Anti-patterns

- Comparing cap rates across property types as if they measured the same risk.
- Applying a rate derived from a different quality of income.
- Ignoring negative leverage when the borrowing cost exceeds the rate.
- Treating a cap rate as a market fact rather than a transaction observation.
- Assuming the rate is stable over a holding period.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Cap rate, NOI, date recorded.
- [ ] Comparables recorded.
- [ ] Financing cost and leverage sign recorded.
