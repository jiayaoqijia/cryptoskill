# Availability versus demand

## Standard

An availability-based project is paid for making the asset available, so its revenue depends on uptime rather than on how many people use it. A demand-based project is paid for usage, so its revenue depends on traffic, passengers, or throughput. The distinction decides which risk the equity holds: availability projects carry performance and maintenance risk, while demand projects carry volume risk on top of it.

That difference explains why the two have different debt capacities. An availability payment is contracted and predictable, so lenders will size debt against a high proportion of it; a demand forecast is an estimate, so the debt is sized against a downside case and the equity absorbs the difference. The record should state which regime applies, because the same headline return implies very different risk depending on the answer.

Primary sources: <https://www.worldbank.org/> and <https://www.eib.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the payment regime: availability, demand, or a hybrid with a minimum.
- Record the performance regime and the deductions for unavailability.
- Record the demand forecast's source and its downside case.
- Record the debt sizing basis and the coverage ratio used for each regime.
- Record any traffic or volume guarantee and who provides it.
- Record the sensitivity of revenue to a stated change in volume or uptime.

## Procedure

1. Read the payment mechanism from the concession or contract.
2. Record the regime and the performance conditions.
3. Record the forecast and its downside case for demand risk.
4. Record the debt sizing basis and coverage.
5. Record any guarantee.
6. Write the risk allocation with the sensitivity stated.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a demand forecast as contracted revenue.
- Ignoring the unavailability deductions in an availability regime.
- Comparing returns across regimes without the risk difference.
- Assuming a volume guarantee removes demand risk entirely.
- Using a base-case forecast as the debt sizing basis.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Payment regime recorded.
- [ ] Performance regime recorded.
- [ ] Demand forecast and downside recorded.
- [ ] Debt sizing basis recorded.
- [ ] Guarantees recorded.
- [ ] Sensitivity stated.

The regime also determines what the equity is really being paid for: in an availability project the return comes from operating the asset reliably, so the operational capability of the sponsor is the credit, while in a demand project the return comes from being right about a market the sponsor may not control. That is a difference in the skill required, not only in the risk.
