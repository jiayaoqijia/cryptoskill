# Disclosure regimes

## Standard

Climate disclosure is governed by several overlapping regimes, and they differ in materiality thresholds, in the scope of what must be reported, and in whether the requirement is mandatory or voluntary. A company reporting under one regime may omit what another requires, and a comparison across companies reporting under different regimes is a comparison of different questions.

The gaps matter more than the overlaps. Forward-looking disclosure is often protected by safe harbours, scenario analysis is frequently qualitative, and scope 3 reporting is often permitted to rely on estimates without the methodology disclosed. Reading a disclosure means asking which of those gaps applies, because a complete-looking report can be complete only within a narrow definition.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.bis.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record which regime the disclosure is made under, and whether it is mandatory.
- Record the materiality standard that applies.
- Record which scopes and categories are required versus voluntary.
- Record whether the figures are assured and at what level of assurance.
- Record the safe-harbour protections that apply to forward-looking statements.
- Record the jurisdiction's rules, since they differ and can conflict.

## Procedure

1. Identify the regime and its status.
2. Record the required content.
3. Record what is optional or omitted.
4. Record the assurance level.
5. Record safe harbours and their effect.
6. Write the disclosure assessment with the gaps named.

## Anti-patterns

- Comparing disclosures across regimes as if they were equivalent.
- Treating a voluntary disclosure as complete coverage.
- Assuming an assurance opinion covers the emissions figures.
- Ignoring safe harbours when reading forward-looking claims.
- Missing a jurisdictional conflict that lets a company choose the lighter rule.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Regime and status recorded.
- [ ] Required content recorded.
- [ ] Omissions recorded.
- [ ] Assurance level recorded.
- [ ] Safe harbours recorded.
- [ ] Assessment written with gaps.

The practical check is to read the assurance statement rather than the report: it names what the auditor examined, and the answer is frequently the reported figures rather than the estimates behind them. A company whose emissions are assured but whose scope 3 estimate is not has told you which of the two it stands behind.


A further gap is the treatment of the value chain: some regimes require the largest categories of scope 3 while others leave them voluntary, so two companies in the same industry can report figures that are not comparable in coverage even when both claim to report scope 3. Recording which categories each includes, rather than whether scope 3 is reported at all, is what makes the comparison meaningful.
