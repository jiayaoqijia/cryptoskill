# Greenshoe and stabilisation

## Standard

The greenshoe is an option granted to the syndicate to sell up to fifteen percent more shares than the base deal. The syndicate exercises it either by buying shares in the market — which supports the price — or by taking the additional shares from the issuer, depending on where the price trades relative to the offer. The mechanism is a short position that the syndicate covers, and its cover is what produces the familiar pattern after a large issue.

Reading it correctly means recognising that post-issue price support is a contractual activity with a limit. The stabilising manager can bid at or below the offer price for a defined period, and the capacity to do so is the size of the over-allotment. Once the option is exercised or the period ends, the bid disappears, and a stock that was held above the offer by that bid can move once the support is withdrawn.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.finra.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the greenshoe size as a percentage of the base deal.
- Record the stabilisation period and the price at which support may be provided.
- Record whether the option was exercised and in which form.
- Record the syndicate's resulting position after the option is settled.
- Record the date the stabilisation window closes.
- Record any lock-up or market stand-off that overlaps the window.

## Procedure

1. Read the underwriting agreement for the option terms.
2. Record the size and the stabilisation period.
3. Record the exercise outcome and its form.
4. Record the syndicate position afterwards.
5. Record the window's closing date.
6. Write the summary with the post-window expectation stated.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating post-issue support as a permanent bid.
- Ignoring the option's settlement form, which determines whether new shares are issued.
- Assuming the stabilisation window is disclosed precisely.
- Overlooking the overlap between the window's end and a lock-up expiry.
- Reading price stability during the window as demand.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Greenshoe size recorded.
- [ ] Stabilisation period recorded.
- [ ] Exercise outcome recorded.
- [ ] Syndicate position recorded.
- [ ] Window close recorded.
- [ ] Overlap with lock-ups recorded.

A further detail is that the option's exercise can be partial, and the partial outcome is itself informative: an underwriter that exercises only part of the shoe has found less demand than the full allocation implied, and one that exercises fully and then buys in the market has found more. The disclosed settlement form is a small but real read on how the deal was received.
