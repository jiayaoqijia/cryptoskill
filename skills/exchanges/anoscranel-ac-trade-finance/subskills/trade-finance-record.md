# Trade finance record

## Standard

The record states the instrument, the parties, the documents that trigger payment or transfer title, the security taken, and the verification that the goods exist. Those facts determine whether the exposure is a claim on cargo or a claim on a document, and the difference is the whole risk in this asset class.

It also records the residual exposure after every protection has been counted. A letter of credit confirmed by a strong bank leaves the seller exposed to document discrepancies; a prepayment secured on offtake leaves the financier exposed to production; an inventory facility leaves the lender exposed to eligibility rules that shrink the base when the borrower needs it most. Naming those residuals is what makes the record an assessment rather than a summary.

Primary sources: <https://www.icc-ccs.org/> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record names the instrument, its type, and the parties including the banks.
- The document set is recorded, with the payment or title trigger for each.
- Security is recorded by type: assignment, charge, guarantee, or control account.
- Independent verification of the goods is recorded, with who performed it.
- The residual exposure is named after each protection is counted.
- Review triggers are dated: shipment date, presentation deadline, facility renewal.

## Procedure

1. Record the instrument and the parties.
2. Record the document set and the triggers.
3. Record the security taken.
4. Record the verification performed.
5. Name the residual exposure.
6. Set the review triggers and file the record.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording the instrument without the document set.
- Treating a security interest as protection without verification.
- Leaving the residual unnamed.
- Omitting the bank chain and its confirmations.
- No triggers, so presentation deadlines pass unnoticed.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Instrument and parties recorded.
- [ ] Document set and triggers recorded.
- [ ] Security recorded.
- [ ] Verification recorded.
- [ ] Residual exposure named.
- [ ] Review triggers dated.

The record's discipline is most valuable at the point where a protection looks complete: a confirmed credit, a warehouse receipt, and an insurance policy each cover a defined risk and leave the remainder, and writing the remainder down is the only way to see whether the pieces overlap or leave a gap. Three protections that all cover the same risk leave the same exposure as one.
