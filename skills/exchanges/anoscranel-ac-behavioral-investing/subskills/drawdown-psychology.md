# Drawdown psychology

## Standard

A drawdown is a test administered at the worst possible time. Losses loom larger than equivalent gains, the pain is proportional to how recently and how often the loss is observed, and the natural response — act, change, do something — is usually the one that converts a temporary decline into a permanent loss.

The countermeasure is a script written before the drawdown, when the numbers are hypothetical. It states what will be checked, what will not be changed, who will be spoken to, and what evidence would justify a genuine policy change. In the moment, the script replaces judgement rather than informing it.

Primary sources: <https://www.jstor.org/stable/1914185> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- The script is written when the portfolio is at or near its high, never during the drawdown.
- It names a threshold at which it activates, in percentage terms.
- It lists actions that are permitted (rebalance per policy, review documents) and actions that are forbidden (sell out of policy, change targets).
- It names a person to speak to before any discretionary action.
- It sets a waiting period between the impulse and any trade.
- A genuine policy change requires the review process, not the moment.

## Procedure

1. Write the script at a calm moment and state the activation threshold.
2. List permitted and forbidden actions explicitly.
3. Name the person and the waiting period.
4. Define the evidence that would justify a policy review.
5. Store the script with the policy statement.
6. After the drawdown, review how well the script was followed.

## Anti-patterns

- Writing the script during the first drawdown.
- Leaving the permitted/forbidden lists vague.
- Allowing a discretionary sale because "this time is different".
- Skipping the waiting period under time pressure.
- Reviewing the script only when it failed.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Script written at a calm moment with an activation threshold.
- [ ] Permitted and forbidden actions listed.
- [ ] Person and waiting period named.
- [ ] Evidence for a policy review defined.
- [ ] Script stored with the policy.
- [ ] Post-drawdown adherence reviewed.

The script works because it is written by a calmer person and read by a frightened one. Keep it short enough to read in a minute and specific enough that 'wait three days and call someone' is an instruction rather than a sentiment.
