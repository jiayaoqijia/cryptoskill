# Earnings quality

## Overview

Reported profit is an estimate assembled under rules that permit judgement, and the judgement shows up as a gap between earnings and cash. Reading that gap is the craft: accruals that build faster than revenue, working capital that absorbs cash, adjustments that recur under a different name each year, and revenue recognised before the risks have transferred are all visible in the filings if they are looked for in the right order.

This skill is forensic rather than accusatory. Every technique here identifies a pattern that warrants a question, and none of them establishes intent. The output is a set of specific questions with the filing references that raise them, which is a more useful product than a verdict, and a more honest one.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/>.

## When to use

- When reported earnings diverge from operating cash flow.
- When non-GAAP figures differ materially from the reported ones.
- When revenue growth outpaces receivables or inventory in a way that needs explaining.
- When a restatement, a guidance change, or a recurring "one-off" appears.

## When NOT to use

- For the overall investment case and valuation (use ac-equity-research).
- For ratio analysis as a general framework (use ac-equity-research).
- For drafting a report about the findings (use ac-technical-writing).

## Status and honest limits

Accounting standards permit a range of treatments, and a company operating within them is not concealing anything. The patterns here are signals that a question is warranted, not evidence of misconduct, and several have innocent explanations that the filings supply. Nothing in this skill should be read as an allegation, and a quality concern without the specific filing reference behind it is an impression rather than an analysis.
