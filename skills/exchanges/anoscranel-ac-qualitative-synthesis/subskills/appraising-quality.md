# Appraising quality

## Standard

Qualitative appraisal tools — the CASP qualitative checklist among the most used — ask structured questions about a study's conduct: was there a clear statement of aims, was the methodology appropriate, was the sampling strategy suited to the aims, was data collection and analysis rigorous, were the researcher's role and potential bias considered, were ethical issues addressed, is there a clear statement of findings. The tool produces a profile of the study's transparency and rigor, not a score, and not a verdict on whether the findings are true.

Appraisal's purpose in a synthesis is transparency about the strength of the contributing evidence, and occasionally exclusion of studies whose conduct cannot support any finding at all. It is not a licence to weight studies numerically or to exclude findings that are inconvenient but well conducted. An appraisal that only ever excludes the studies that disagree with the emerging synthesis is a breakdown of the method, and the record of exclusions with their reasons is the check against it.

Primary sources: <https://casp-uk.net/casp-tools-checklists/> and <https://jbi.global/critical-appraisal-tools>.

## Decision rules

- Name the appraisal tool in the protocol; do not choose after seeing the studies.
- Appraise all included studies with the same tool, independently where possible.
- Record responses per item; do not collapse to a score the tool does not define.
- Decide in advance what appraisal result triggers exclusion, and how disagreements are resolved.
- Report each study's appraisal profile alongside its contribution to the synthesis.
- Record all exclusions based on appraisal with the specific failing items named.

## Procedure

1. Select the tool in the protocol.
2. Appraise every included study, recording per-item responses.
3. Apply the pre-set exclusion rule; record any exclusions with reasons.
4. Tabulate appraisal profiles against each study's contribution.
5. Note where a finding's support comes predominantly from weaker-conduct studies.
6. Carry these caveats into the confidence-in-findings judgement.

## Anti-patterns

- Excluding a study for disagreeing, then reading its appraisal to justify the exclusion.
- Collapsing a checklist into a mean score and calling that quality weight.
- Appraising only the studies that matter to the emerging theme.
- Inventing an exclusion rule after seeing which studies fail.
- Hiding appraisal profiles from the reader.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Tool named and pre-specified.
- [ ] All studies appraised with the same tool.
- [ ] Per-item responses recorded.
- [ ] Exclusion rule applied as pre-set.
- [ ] Exclusions named with failing items.
- [ ] Profiles visible against contributions.
