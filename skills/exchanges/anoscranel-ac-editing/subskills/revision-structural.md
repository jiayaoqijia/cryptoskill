# Developmental edit

## Standard

⚠️ The primary standard is paywalled; these points come from secondary sources and the public summary.

- **The pass that changes meaning.** Trade publishing runs four named passes: developmental (the whole argument and arc), line (sentence by sentence), copyedit (mechanical, against a style sheet), proofread (the typeset artifact). The first two change meaning; the last two do not (CMOS Q&A: https://www.chicagomanualofstyle.org/qanda/data/faq/topics/ManuscriptPreparation/faq0218.html; ladder in <https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/19760015018>).
- **Purpose, then structure, then argument, then cuts.** The developmental pass answers in order: does this document do its job, is it ordered for the reader, does each claim carry its evidence, what can go.
- **Revision moves claims, not sentences.** 60.2% of primary claims are touched between preprint and publication (<https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.15067>). The claim type is preserved in 96.6% of pairs. Structural work is where meaning changes.
- **Argument repair must be evidence-bound.** In the AI-paper study, standard revisions left residual slop and telling the model to fix the slop triggered reward hacking (§3, <https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.00531>). The fix that worked revised only where the records supported the change.
- **Fresh eyes beat immediacy.** Delay before re-reading, a change of medium, and reading aloud each change what you can see (<https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ498842>).

## Decision rules

1. One pass, one level. Never mix meaning-level edits with sentence edits in the same read.
2. Cut what no reader question needs. A section that answers no brief requirement is removed, not shortened.
3. If a claim loses its evidence in this pass, cut the claim. Do not soften it and keep it.
4. Structure changes come before wording changes: a wording pass on a moved section is wasted work.
5. Do not compress hedges away. Deleting qualifiers while cutting words is an argument change done silently.
6. Re-check the brief after the cuts. The success test must still be reachable.

## Procedure

1. Rest. Leave the draft for at least a night where the schedule allows.
2. Read once for purpose only. Do not edit. Answer: does this do the brief's job?
3. Read the headings alone (out of scope for this leaf: building an outline from scratch). Reorder or cut by what the heading list shows.
4. Check the claim ledger (out of scope for this leaf: the claim-evidence grammar). Every claim with its grounds; every missing ground is a cut.
5. Cut with a record: what went, why, and what the loss costs.
6. Re-read the result at meaning level only, then release it to the sentence pass.
7. Log the structural change list — the audit trail of what the document now claims.

## Anti-patterns

- Fixing sentences to avoid restructuring.
- Compressing a draft by deleting qualifiers, hedges and limitations.
- Cutting the section that carries the answer because it is hard to defend.
- Meaning edits and sentence edits in one pass, so neither is finishable.
- Re-reading the draft in the same session it was written.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Purpose judged against the brief, recorded.
- [ ] Heading list read alone and reconciled.
- [ ] Claim ledger checked; unsupported claims cut.
- [ ] Cut list recorded with reasons.
- [ ] Hedge and limitation check after compression.
- [ ] Draft rested and read at a change of medium.
- [ ] Structural change list written.
