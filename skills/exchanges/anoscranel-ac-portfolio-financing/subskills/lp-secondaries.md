# LP secondaries

## Standard

An LP secondary is the sale of an existing investor's interest in a fund to another buyer. The price is a negotiated discount to the reported net asset value, and the discount reflects the buyer's view of the marks, the remaining term, the quality of the assets, and the leverage inside the vehicle.

The discount is the market's opinion of the marks, which is why it is informative beyond the transaction. A fund reporting stable values while its interests trade at a substantial discount is telling two different stories, and the gap is a measure of the optimism in the reported figure. Recording the discount and its components is the way to read that gap.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the reported net asset value and the transaction price.
- Record the discount and its stated rationale.
- Record the composition of the portfolio, since asset quality drives the discount.
- Record the remaining term and any unfunded commitment transferred.
- Record the fund-level leverage and whether it is assumed by the buyer.
- Compare the discount with those observed on comparable funds.

## Procedure

1. Record the NAV and the price.
2. Compute the discount.
3. Record the portfolio's composition and quality.
4. Record term and unfunded commitments.
5. Record leverage at the fund.
6. Compare with comparable transactions.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading a discount as a judgement on the manager alone.
- Ignoring unfunded commitments that the buyer must fund.
- Comparing discounts across funds with different asset mixes.
- Assuming the reported NAV is the reference the market uses.
- Treating a single transaction as the market level.

## Done checklist

- [ ] NAV and price recorded.
- [ ] Discount computed.
- [ ] Composition recorded.
- [ ] Term and commitments recorded.
- [ ] Leverage recorded.
- [ ] Comparison made.

The unfunded commitment is the component most often missed by a buyer and most often overstated by a seller: an interest with substantial unfunded capital is a commitment to invest more, not only a claim on existing assets, and the price should reflect the obligation as well as the portfolio. Recording it separately from the NAV is what keeps the discount calculation honest.


The comparison worth making is between the secondary discount and the fund's own reported mark movement: a fund whose marks have fallen less than its interests trade below them is a fund whose figures the market is discounting, and the size of that gap is a more useful reading than the discount alone.
