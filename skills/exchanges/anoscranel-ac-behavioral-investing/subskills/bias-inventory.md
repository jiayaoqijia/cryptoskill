# Bias inventory

## Standard

Most bias lists are long and useless because they describe laboratory effects without saying which decision they ruin. The useful inventory is short and maps each bias to a specific moment: loss aversion to the sale you cannot bring yourself to make, anchoring to the price you keep referring back to, recency to the allocation you changed after a bad quarter, overconfidence to the position that grew without a decision.

The inventory's purpose is recognition under load. A bias you can name at the moment it acts is a bias you can interrupt with a rule; one you can only name afterwards becomes a postmortem entry.

Primary sources: <https://www.jstor.org/stable/1914185> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Keep the list to the biases that have damaged this portfolio or plausibly would; a list of twenty is not used.
- Each entry names the decision it attacks, not just the effect.
- Each entry names the countermeasure already in place, or records that none is.
- New entries require a specific incident or a well-evidenced case, not a reading list.
- The inventory is reviewed at the behavioural review, not in the moment.
- Knowing a bias is not a countermeasure; only a rule is.

## Procedure

1. List the candidate biases.
2. For each, write the decision it attacks in one sentence.
3. Record whether a countermeasure exists and what it is.
4. Cut the entries with no plausible decision attached.
5. Review the list at each behavioural review and date the changes.
6. Keep the list where decisions are made, not in a drawer.

## Anti-patterns

- Copying a textbook list of twenty biases.
- Naming a bias without naming the decision it affects.
- Treating awareness as protection.
- Adding entries after every book read.
- Reviewing the list only after a loss.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Short list, each entry tied to a decision.
- [ ] Countermeasure recorded or absence noted.
- [ ] Entries without a plausible decision removed.
- [ ] List reviewed and dated at the behavioural review.
- [ ] List kept where decisions happen.

Keep the list short enough to recite from memory before a decision. A list you have to look up is a list you will not consult at the moment the bias acts, and the moment is the only place the list earns its keep.
