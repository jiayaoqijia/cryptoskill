# Capital return record

## Standard

The record states the company's policy in its own terms, the execution against that policy, the funding behind it, and the tests applied to judge it. Separating policy from execution is what makes the record useful: a company can have a sensible policy and execute it badly, and the two failures look identical from the outside unless they are recorded separately.

It also carries the counterfactual. Every distribution is a decision not to spend that cash elsewhere, and the record names the alternative — debt reduction, capital expenditure, acquisition, retention — so that a later review can ask whether the choice was right rather than only whether it happened. The shareholder-yield figure belongs here, with its components and their period.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://osf.io/prereg/>.

## Decision rules

- The record states the policy: regular dividend, buyback authorisation, special, variable, or a combination.
- Execution is recorded per period: amount authorised, amount used, average price.
- The funding source is recorded: operating cash flow, asset sale, or borrowing.
- The alternative use of the same cash is named and its rough value recorded.
- Shareholder yield is computed with dividends and net repurchases, and the period stated.
- Review triggers are dated: policy change, coverage deterioration, authorisation expiry.

## Procedure

1. Record the stated policy and its history.
2. Record execution per period with prices and amounts.
3. Record the funding source.
4. Name the alternative use and its value.
5. Compute shareholder yield over a stated period.
6. Set the review triggers and file the record.

## Anti-patterns

- Recording the announcement and not the execution.
- Omitting the funding source of a distribution.
- Computing shareholder yield without netting issuance.
- Leaving the counterfactual unnamed.
- Reviewing only in the periods when distributions happen.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Policy and history recorded.
- [ ] Execution per period recorded.
- [ ] Funding source recorded.
- [ ] Alternative use named.
- [ ] Shareholder yield computed with period.
- [ ] Review triggers dated.

The record's most useful column is the policy as it was stated at the time: companies revise the language of their distribution policy without announcing a change, and comparing the current statement with the one recorded two years earlier is often the earliest visible signal that the commitment has weakened.
