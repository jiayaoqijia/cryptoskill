# Appraisal and market value

## Standard

An appraisal is an opinion of value at a date, produced by one of three approaches: the income approach capitalises expected income, the sales comparison approach adjusts recent transactions, and the cost approach builds up from land plus depreciated construction. Each is only as good as its inputs, and each embeds an assumption about what a buyer would accept.

The gap between an appraisal and a transaction price is information rather than error. A property carried at an appraisal that has not been tested by a sale can be worth materially less in a market where buyers have become scarce, and a valuation produced for a financing is not the same as one produced for a sale, because the assumptions serve different purposes.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.federalreserve.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the approach or approaches used and their weights.
- Record the date of the appraisal and the date of the inputs.
- Record the comparable transactions and the adjustments applied.
- Record the capitalisation rate or discount rate used and its source.
- Record the intended use of the appraisal, which shapes its assumptions.
- Record the gap between appraised value and any transaction evidence.

## Procedure

1. Record the approaches used and their weights.
2. Record the appraisal date against the input dates.
3. Record comparables and adjustments.
4. Record the rate used and its source.
5. Record the intended use.
6. Write the appraisal summary with the gap to transactions.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating an appraisal as a market price.
- Ignoring an appraisal whose inputs predate the valuation date.
- Comparing appraisals produced for different purposes.
- Assuming the income approach dominates when comparables are thin.
- Reading value without the intended use stated.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Approaches and weights recorded.
- [ ] Dates recorded.
- [ ] Comparables and adjustments recorded.
- [ ] Rate and source recorded.
- [ ] Intended use recorded.
- [ ] Gap to transactions recorded.

The most useful comparison to record is between two appraisals of the same asset produced for different purposes: a financing appraisal and a sale appraisal on the same property often differ by more than the market moved, and the difference is a measure of how much of the value is assumption rather than observation.
