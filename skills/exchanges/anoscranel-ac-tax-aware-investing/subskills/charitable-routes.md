# Charitable routes

## Standard

Donating an appreciated asset instead of selling it can avoid the gain that a sale would realise while still producing a deduction for the donated value, which is why the route is used for concentrated positions with large embedded gains. The mechanics differ by vehicle: a donor-advised fund accepts the asset and makes grants over time, a private foundation has its own rules and reporting, and a charitable remainder trust pays an income stream before the remainder passes.

The decision has constraints. The vehicle may not accept every asset type, the deduction depends on the asset and the holding period, and the gift is irrevocable. Those constraints make the route a disposition decision rather than a tax optimisation: the capital leaves the portfolio, and the benefit is the charitable outcome plus the tax treatment, not a way to keep the position.

Primary sources: <https://www.irs.gov/> and <https://www.investor.gov/introduction-investing>.

## Decision rules

- Record the asset, its cost basis, and the unrealised gain.
- Record the vehicle and whether it accepts that asset type.
- Record the deduction treatment, which depends on the asset and holding period.
- Record the irrevocability and the loss of access to the capital.
- Record the grants or income stream the vehicle provides.
- Record the alternative of selling and donating cash, for comparison.

## Procedure

1. Record the asset and its embedded gain.
2. Identify an eligible vehicle.
3. Record the deduction treatment as a question for a professional.
4. Compare with selling and donating the proceeds.
5. Record the irrevocable nature and the capital that leaves.
6. Write the comparison.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating the route as a way to retain control of the position.
- Assuming every vehicle accepts every asset.
- Ignoring the holding-period condition on the deduction.
- Comparing the donation with doing nothing rather than with a sale.
- Overlooking the ongoing administration of a private vehicle.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Asset and gain recorded.
- [ ] Vehicle eligibility recorded.
- [ ] Deduction question recorded.
- [ ] Sale alternative compared.
- [ ] Irrevocability recorded.
- [ ] Administration recorded.

The comparison that keeps the decision honest is against the sale, not against inaction: a donor who would otherwise sell and donate cash forgoes the gain either way, while a donor who would otherwise hold has given up the position entirely, and the two baselines produce different conclusions from the same arithmetic.
