# Approach selection

## Standard

The main approaches differ in what they promise. Meta-aggregation collects the findings of primary studies, groups them into categories, and states one or more syntheses from those categories — it stays close to the original authors' words and minimises the synthesist's interpretation. Thematic synthesis codes findings line by line and builds descriptive themes, then analytical ones that go beyond any single study — it aims at conceptual development. Meta-ethnography translates studies into one another, building third-order constructs that are the synthesist's own account — the highest interpretation, and the highest risk of over-reach.

The choice should follow from the question's intent. An intervention-adjacent question about barriers and facilitators fits aggregation or thematic synthesis; a question about how people experience a phenomenon over time often needs the interpretive depth of meta-ethnography, or a realist question about what works for whom under which conditions needs a realist synthesis. Choosing the approach after seeing the findings biases the output toward whatever method would have produced the more striking result.

Primary sources: <https://bmcmedresmethodol.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/1471-2288-8-45> and <https://www.biomedcentral.com/methods>.

## Decision rules

- State the review question's intent before choosing the approach.
- Choose one primary approach in the protocol; name any fallback explicitly.
- Record what kind of output the approach produces: aggregation, themes, or translation.
- Match approach ambition to the primary studies' depth — thin studies cannot carry meta-ethnography.
- Note the approach's known weaknesses alongside the choice.
- Declare any approach change as a protocol amendment with a reason.

## Procedure

1. Restate the question as an intent: describe, aggregate, develop, or explain.
2. List candidate approaches and their outputs.
3. Choose one primary approach, with a stated reason, in the protocol.
4. Confirm primary studies have the depth the approach requires.
5. Name the approach's known weaknesses in the protocol.
6. Record choice and reason; any change is dated and explained.

## Anti-patterns

- Choosing the method after reading the findings.
- Defaulting to thematic synthesis because it is the most familiar.
- Running meta-ethnography on thin descriptive studies.
- Switching approach because the first produced dull findings.
- Mixing approaches within one synthesis without a stated division of labour.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Question intent stated.
- [ ] Approach chosen in the protocol with reason.
- [ ] Output type declared.
- [ ] Study depth matched to approach.
- [ ] Weaknesses named.
- [ ] Any change dated and reasoned.
