# Equity diligence record

## Standard

The research record is the evidence base that valuation stands on. It is written before any price target exists, so that the target cannot quietly reshape the facts. Its structure is the same as any good evidence document: what was examined, what was found, where each finding came from, how confident the analyst is, and what remains unknown.

The record's second job is to become the review document. When results arrive, the record is what makes the review cheap: the thesis is stated, the falsifiers are named, and a change in the facts can be traced to a change in conclusions rather than to a change in mood.

Primary sources: <https://osf.io/prereg/> and <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/>.

## Decision rules

- Findings are written with source, period, and confidence; no finding stands without a traceable origin.
- Unknowns are a section, not an omission.
- The thesis is stated as falsifiable claims: what must be true, and what would show it false.
- The record is append-only; later corrections are dated amendments.
- Review triggers are named: results dates, guidance changes, auditor changes, covenant events.
- Confidence caps the conclusions that valuation may draw.

## Procedure

1. Open with the company, the date, and the question.
2. Assemble findings from the six leaves into one table with source, period, confidence.
3. Write the falsifiable thesis: three to five claims with a falsifier each.
4. Write the unknowns section.
5. Set review triggers with dates.
6. File the record and reference it from the valuation document and any purchase memo.

## Anti-patterns

- Writing the record after the price target so it supports the target.
- Stating a thesis that cannot be falsified.
- Omitting unknowns to make the record look complete.
- Rewriting history when a thesis fails.
- Setting review triggers with no dates.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Company, date, and question stated.
- [ ] Findings table with source, period, confidence per row.
- [ ] Falsifiable thesis with falsifiers named.
- [ ] Unknowns section written.
- [ ] Review triggers dated.
- [ ] Record filed and referenced downstream.

## Sources

- Full-text filing search for sources: <https://www.sec.gov/edgar/search/>

A research record is written for the analyst who will read it in two years, when the thesis has either worked or failed and the reasons are no longer obvious. Write for that reader, not for the one who already knows what happened.
