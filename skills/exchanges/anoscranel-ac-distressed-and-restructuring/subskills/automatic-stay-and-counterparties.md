# Automatic stay and counterparties

## Standard

The automatic stay halts collection activity against the debtor the moment a petition is filed: lawsuits stop, liens cannot be enforced, and ordinary creditors cannot demand payment. That protection is broad but not total, and the exceptions are where counterparty risk actually sits.

The significant carve-outs are contractual. A qualified financial contract counterparty can terminate and close out under a safe harbour, which is why derivatives and repurchase agreements are treated separately from ordinary trade debt. Setoff rights survive in a narrower form, and a landlord or a critical vendor may negotiate treatment that ordinary unsecured creditors do not receive. For a holder, the question is not whether the stay protects the debtor but whether the specific instrument retains its contractual remedies.

Primary sources: <https://www.uscourts.gov/> and <https://www.law.cornell.edu/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the petition date, which fixes the moment the stay begins.
- Record which of the holder's remedies are stayed and which survive.
- Record whether the instrument qualifies for a safe harbour and what that permits.
- Record any setoff or recoupment right and its limits.
- Record critical-vendor or landlord treatment where disclosed.
- Record relief-from-stay motions, which can lift the protection for specific creditors.

## Procedure

1. Record the petition date and the holder's position as of it.
2. Identify the contractual remedies and whether they are stayed.
3. Check safe-harbour qualification for financial contracts.
4. Record setoff and recoupment positions.
5. Track relief-from-stay motions relevant to the position.
6. Write the analysis with the surviving remedies named.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming every contractual remedy is frozen.
- Treating a derivative counterparty as an ordinary creditor.
- Ignoring setoff rights and their documentary basis.
- Forgetting that relief from stay can be granted selectively.
- Assuming the stay protects guarantors in the same way.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Petition date recorded.
- [ ] Stayed and surviving remedies separated.
- [ ] Safe harbour status recorded.
- [ ] Setoff rights recorded.
- [ ] Relief motions tracked.
- [ ] Analysis written with remedies named.

The stay also creates a timing asymmetry that matters to a holder: the debtor can use the breathing space to renegotiate, while the creditor's remedies are frozen, so the value of a claim during the process depends partly on how long the process runs. That is why milestones in a DIP facility and deadlines in a plan are economically significant, not just procedural.
