# Operational due diligence

## Standard

Operational due diligence asks whether the manager can do what it says: whether the trades it reports are the trades that settled, whether the valuations it publishes are the ones its administrator computes, whether the cash it claims exists at a custodian that can confirm it. Those questions are answered by documents and by third parties, not by the manager's description of its controls.

The evidence is available and mostly unused. An administrator's report, a custodian's confirmation, an audit opinion with its qualifications, a regulatory filing history, and the service providers' own reputations are all external to the manager and all checkable. A diligence process that consists of meetings with the manager has examined the manager's account of itself rather than its operations.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.cfainstitute.org/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the administrator, custodian, auditor, and prime broker, and confirm their roles independently.
- Record the reconciliation between the manager's reported positions and the administrator's.
- Record the audit opinion and any qualification or emphasis of matter.
- Record the manager's regulatory registration and any disciplinary history.
- Record the valuation source and whether it is independent of the investment team.
- Record the disaster recovery and systems arrangements as evidenced, not as described.

## Procedure

1. Record the service provider chain and confirm each role.
2. Obtain the administrator's report and reconcile to the manager's figures.
3. Read the audit opinion and its qualifications.
4. Check regulatory registration and history.
5. Record the valuation source and its independence.
6. Write the operational assessment with the evidence cited.

## Anti-patterns

- Accepting the manager's description of its administrator's role.
- Reading the audit opinion for its existence rather than its content.
- Ignoring a qualification or an emphasis of matter.
- Assuming regulatory registration implies operational quality.
- Leaving the valuation source indistinguishable from the investment team.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Service provider chain recorded and confirmed.
- [ ] Reconciliation obtained.
- [ ] Audit opinion read.
- [ ] Regulatory history checked.
- [ ] Valuation independence recorded.
- [ ] Assessment written with evidence.

The reconciliation is the single most valuable test available: comparing the manager's reported positions with the administrator's independent record detects errors, delays, and in the worst case misappropriation, and it is a test that produces evidence rather than assurances. A manager that resists providing it has answered the question in a different way.
