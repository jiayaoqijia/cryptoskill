# Lease structure and tenant credit

## Standard

The stability of a property's income is set by the leases that produce it. Term length, renewal options, break clauses, and the recovery structure determine who pays for what and for how long. A long lease to a weak tenant and a short lease to a strong one carry different risks, and the difference appears not in the headline rent but in the probability that the income continues.

Concentration is the second factor and the one most easily missed. A building whose income depends on one tenant is a credit exposure dressed as real estate, and the tenant's rating, its business, and its lease term together determine how much of the property's value survives if that tenant leaves. The record should state the largest tenants, their share of income, and the lease expiry profile.

Primary sources: <https://www.sec.gov/investor> and <https://www.federalreserve.gov/>.

## Decision rules

- Record the lease term, expiry, and any renewal or break options.
- Record the recovery structure: gross, net, or triple net, and what each covers.
- Record the largest tenants and their share of income.
- Record the tenant's credit standing where it is rated or can be assessed.
- Record the expiry profile by year and the rollover concentrated in any one year.
- Record the incentives, rent-free periods, and fit-out obligations in place.

## Procedure

1. Record each lease's term and options.
2. Record the recovery structure and the costs it shifts.
3. Compute tenant concentration by income share.
4. Record tenant credit where assessable.
5. Build the expiry profile by year.
6. Write the stability summary with the largest exposure named.

## Anti-patterns

- Reading headline rent without the lease term.
- Treating a triple-net lease as riskless because the tenant pays costs.
- Ignoring rollover concentrated in a single year.
- Assuming a tenant's size implies its credit.
- Overlooking incentives that reduce effective rent.

## Done checklist

- [ ] Lease terms recorded.
- [ ] Recovery structure recorded.
- [ ] Concentration computed.
- [ ] Tenant credit recorded.
- [ ] Expiry profile built.
- [ ] Incentives recorded.

The clause that most affects value and is least often read is the one governing what happens at expiry: a tenant with a renewal option at a fixed rent removes the landlord's ability to reprice, and a tenant with a break clause removes the certainty of the term, so an apparently long lease can be an option held by the tenant rather than a commitment.
